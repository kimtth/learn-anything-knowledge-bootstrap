# Nietzsche

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Friedrich Nietzsche was a German philosopher who developed radical critiques of morality, truth, religion, and culture. His mature ideas of nihilism, perspectivism, eternal recurrence, and the overman, together with later political misuse of his work, make his biography inseparable from his influence.

## Death and Significance

![[attachments/nietzsche_001.webp|420]]

*Portrait photograph of Friedrich Nietzsche*

Friedrich Nietzsche, who died in Weimar on August 25, 1900, was a German classical scholar, philosopher, and cultural critic. He became a major modern thinker by exposing motives beneath Western religion, morality, and philosophy. His phrase God is dead ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_is_dead)) named secularism's consequences. Though he opposed nationalism, anti-Semitism, and power politics, his sister later linked him to ideas he hated.

## Early Years

Nietzsche grew up in a household of Lutheran piety. His father was a pastor, and after his death in 1849 Nietzsche lived with his mother, sister, grandmother, and aunts. He studied at Schulpforta, Bonn, and Leipzig, where Friedrich Wilhelm Ritschl guided his classical philology, Arthur Schopenhauer's philosophy impressed him, and he met Richard Wagner.

## Basel Years

In 1869, Nietzsche became professor of classical philology at Basel before completing the usual academic requirements. Wartime service in 1870 left his health permanently damaged. His friendship with Wagner first deepened and then broke because Wagner's Christian motifs, chauvinism, and anti-Semitism repelled him. Early works such as The Birth of Tragedy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Birth_of_Tragedy)) remained shaped by Schopenhauer and Wagner.

## Isolation and Mature Works

From 1879 to 1889, Nietzsche lived in isolation in Switzerland, Italy, and the French Riviera, suffering illness, pain, and near-blindness. He wrote Thus Spoke Zarathustra ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thus_Spoke_Zarathustra)), Beyond Good and Evil, On the Genealogy of Morals, and in 1888 several final works including Twilight of the Idols, The Antichrist, and Ecce Homo. He collapsed in Turin in January 1889 and spent his final years incapacitated.

## Collapse and Misuse

The later misuse of Nietzsche owed much to Elisabeth Forster-Nietzsche, who had married the chauvinist anti-Semite Bernhard Forster. She controlled Nietzsche's papers, created misleading collections such as The Will to Power, committed forgeries, and embraced Adolf Hitler. This helped produce the false public association between Nietzsche and Fascism.

## Mature Philosophy

Nietzsche's mature philosophy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy)) studies values as symptoms of the people and cultures that create them. He attacked the ascetic ideal, which gives suffering ultimate meaning. Christianity made suffering bearable through divine purpose and immortality, while traditional philosophy privileged soul over body, mind over senses, duty over desire, and timeless reality over appearance. Both assumed existence needed justification.

## Nihilism and Perspectivism

Nietzsche saw Europe entering nihilism after the collapse of old absolutes. He feared substitute gods such as nationalism or political ideologies. Perspectivism holds that knowledge always comes from a point of view, not from nowhere. Will to power ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_to_power)) names the drive to interpret, organize, expand, and overcome, while eternal recurrence tests whether one can affirm life without evasion.

## Eternal Recurrence and the Superman

The thought of eternal recurrence asks whether one could will the exact repetition of every moment of life. The person able to affirm this without self-deception would be an Ubermensch, or superman. Nietzsche used this ideal to imagine a life-affirming type beyond ordinary humanity.

## Nietzsche's Influence

Nietzsche's influence shaped philosophy, theology, psychology, literature, and criticism. Martin Heidegger, Albert Camus, Jacques Derrida, Michel Foucault, existentialism, and deconstruction owed him debts. Sigmund Freud, Carl Jung, Thomas Mann, Hermann Hesse, and Rainer Maria Rilke drew on his insight and prose.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1844: Friedrich Nietzsche was born.
- 1869: Nietzsche became professor at Basel.
- 1883-1885: Thus Spoke Zarathustra ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thus_Spoke_Zarathustra)) appeared.
- 1889: Nietzsche collapsed in Turin.
- 1900: Nietzsche died in Weimar.

## Key People

- Friedrich Nietzsche ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Nietzsche)): Philosopher and critic of Western values.
- Richard Wagner ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner)): Composer whose friendship and break shaped Nietzsche.
- Arthur Schopenhauer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Schopenhauer)): Philosopher influential in Nietzsche's early period.
- Elisabeth Forster-Nietzsche ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_F%C3%B6rster-Nietzsche)): Sister who distorted Nietzsche's public image.

## Interesting Facts

- God is dead ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_is_dead)) names a cultural condition, not a simple atheist slogan.
- The Will to Power was assembled from notes Nietzsche did not authorize as a book.
- Zarathustra was issued to German soldiers in World War I.
