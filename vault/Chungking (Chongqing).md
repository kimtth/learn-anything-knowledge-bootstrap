## Introduction

Chongqing (historically Chungking) is a major river port, transportation hub, and industrial centre in southwest-central China on the upper Yangtze. Its strategic location at the junction of the Yangtze and Jialing rivers and its prominence as the wartime capital of Nationalist China during the Sino-Japanese War make it especially significant.

The city became a provincial-level municipality in 1997, greatly expanding its administrative area and population. It is now one of China’s most important inland metropolitan centres.

## Landscape and climate

Chongqing’s core lies on a hilly promontory between two rivers, and the wider municipality extends across lobes of hills, valleys, plains, and mountain ranges. It has an intensely humid, foggy climate and a reputation as the “fog capital.”

## Housing and people

![[attachments/chungking_chongqing_002.webp|420]]

The city historically had crowded hillside housing and narrow streets, though later modernization transformed much of the urban fabric. Chongqing’s population grew dramatically after 1938 as refugees and officials moved there during wartime, and growth continued after 1949 and especially after the 1997 administrative expansion.

## Manufacturing, trade, and transportation

Chongqing has long been an industrial centre, first for textiles and light industry and later for heavy industry, steel, machinery, aluminum, coal, gas, and other manufacturing. It is the commercial hub for a broad inland hinterland and a key transport node for river, rail, road, and air movement.

The Yangtze, the Three Gorges project, improved navigation, and the city’s role as a gateway for southwestern China all deserve special emphasis.

## Government, health, and education

The municipality is governed through the standard Chinese hierarchical party-state structure. It has extensive hospitals, family-planning services, and a large higher-education sector, including numerous universities and colleges.

## History

Chongqing’s history stretches back to ancient states and later imperial incorporation, but the modern period stands out most. It became open to trade in the late 19th century, served as the Nationalist capital during the Sino-Japanese War, suffered heavy bombing, and later developed under the communist state before becoming a municipality in 1997.

## Conclusion

Chongqing is a city whose identity is defined by rivers, hills, wartime resilience, inland industrialization, and rapid late-20th-century expansion.
