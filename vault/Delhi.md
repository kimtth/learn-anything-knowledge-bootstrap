# Delhi

## Introduction

Delhi is both a city and a national capital territory in north-central India. It is made up of Old Delhi, the older historic core in the north, and New Delhi, the planned capital area in the south. The city lies mainly on the west bank of the Yamuna River and is one of India's largest urban areas. As a political, commercial, and cultural center, Delhi has long had importance far beyond its own borders.

## Landscape

![[attachments/delhi_001.webp|420]]

*Jama Masjid 2011*

Delhi has been the center of many powerful kingdoms and empires, so its land is filled with ruins and reminders of earlier ages. The city is often described as having changed its site many times through history, and the older places are gathered in the area known as the Delhi Triangle. This triangle is shaped by the Aravalli hills on two sides and by the shifting Yamuna on the third. The result is a landscape that joins rocky ridges, river edge land, and open plains in a single urban region.

## Climate

Delhi has a very dry climate and very hot summers. In the warm season, which lasts from mid-March to the end of June, the heat often becomes intense, and thunderstorms and squalls are common in April and May. The monsoon usually begins in July and continues into September, bringing most of the yearly rain. The winter season is dry and mild compared with the summer, but January can still feel cold in the early morning and evening. The climate gives the city a strong contrast between dusty heat and wet monsoon air.

## City layout

![[attachments/delhi_003.webp|420]]

*Muhammad Wali Masjid in Delhi*

Delhi's city plan brings together older narrow streets and newer open roads. In Old Delhi, the street network still reflects a defensive city of gates, walls, and closely packed lanes. Many streets are irregular, winding, and sometimes only wide enough for foot traffic. This older pattern makes the area feel dense and busy, and it shows how the city was shaped for protection rather than for modern traffic flow.

## People

Delhi's population changed greatly after 1947, when many Hindu and Sikh refugees came from Pakistan after the partition of India. Since then, the city has continued to grow because of immigration from many Indian states and from nearby countries. New housing areas often have foreign or mixed communities, while older neighborhoods also keep strong local identities. Areas such as Chittaranjan Park and Karol Bagh show how communities have settled in different parts of the city and built distinct social spaces.

## Economy

![[attachments/delhi_002.webp|420]]

*Tomb of Humayun, Delhi*

The service sector is the most important part of Delhi's economy and the largest employer. Many people work in trade, finance, public administration, professional services, and community or personal services. Agriculture, which once mattered much more, now has little economic weight in the territory. Old Delhi has long been a major commercial center in northern India, while New Delhi has become increasingly important in corporate and financial networks since the 1990s.

## Manufacturing

Manufacturing in Delhi began to grow in the early 20th century with activities such as cotton ginning, spinning, weaving, flour grinding, sugarcane processing, and oil pressing. Over time, the city moved toward more varied industry. Electronics, engineering goods, automobile parts, precision instruments, machinery, and electrical appliances became important, while apparel, sports goods, and leather products also remained part of the industrial base. Delhi's factories and workshops show how the city has changed from older textile work to broader modern production.

## Finance and other services

Delhi's role as the national capital has helped it become a banking, wholesale, and distribution center. The Reserve Bank of India is headquartered there, and major banks, including the State Bank of India and many foreign banks, have offices in the city. Delhi is also a center for insurance and is home to the Delhi Stock Exchange. These institutions make the city a major place for money, credit, and financial administration.

## Transportation

Delhi sits in a key geographic corridor, so land routes from northwestern India to the eastern plain have to pass through or near it. Many national highways and railway lines meet in the city, which makes Delhi one of the main transport hubs in India. Indira Gandhi International Airport serves international travel, while the former Palam terminal helps support domestic air services. This combination of road, rail, and air links gives the city major national reach.

## Administration and society

Delhi's government and social organization have changed many times since independence. The city moved from British provincial rule to a centrally administered state, then to a union territory, and finally to national capital territory status in 1991. It is governed through several layers, including the lieutenant governor, the chief minister, and different municipal bodies. This complex structure reflects the city's special role as both an ordinary urban area and the seat of national power.

## Government

Delhi's political status has been one of the most important parts of its modern history. It was a British province until 1947, then a centrally administered state, and then a union territory under the central government. The 1958 corporation for urban and rural areas and the 1991 designation as the national capital territory helped define the city in its present form. The chief administrator is the lieutenant governor, who is appointed by the president of India, and a chief minister also plays a role.

## Health

Health standards in Delhi are higher than the national average, but access to care is uneven. Many people use allopathic and homeopathic clinics, as well as dispensaries that offer traditional treatments based on herbs and minerals. Hospitals are numerous, and many of the largest ones are run by the national government or by the national capital territory. The city's health system is broad, but it does not serve every neighborhood in the same way.

## Education

Delhi's school system has grown along with the city's population. Primary education is close to universal, and many students also continue to secondary school. The national boards for secondary education are based in Delhi, which adds to the city's educational importance. The city therefore plays a major role in both local schooling and national exam systems.

## Cultural life

Delhi's cultural life combines older traditions with modern city habits. Museums, forts, monuments, libraries, auditoriums, botanical gardens, and places of worship are spread across the city. At the same time, there are art galleries, cinema multiplexes, bowling alleys, sports venues, and restaurants serving Indian and international food. This mix gives the city a strong public culture in which heritage and leisure sit side by side.

## Architecture

Delhi's architecture reflects the long sequence of powers that ruled the city. Early Muslim buildings were influenced by Hindu Rajput craft and by Central Asian Seljuq traditions. Later, the Khalji period developed the Pashtun style, with red sandstone, white marble inlays, pointed arches, screens, and rich ornament. Examples such as the Quwat-ul-Islam mosque, the Qutb Minar, the tomb of Iltutmish, and the Alai Gate show how varied and experimental this early Islamic architecture was.

## History

The earliest story connected with Delhi comes from the Mahabharata, which places the city of Indraprastha in the area. Although no remains survive, the legend gives Delhi a very old place in Indian memory. The first known use of the name Delhi is usually linked to Raja Dhilu in the 1st century BCE. Later, the Tomara and Rajput rulers developed sites such as Anangpur, Lal Kot, and Qila Rai Pithora, which became the base for later settlement and defense.
