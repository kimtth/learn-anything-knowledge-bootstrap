## Introduction

![[attachments/connective_tissue_001.webp|420]]

*Diagram of connective tissue fibres and cells*

Connective tissues ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connective_tissue)) are body tissues that maintain the form of the body and organs, provide cohesion, and give internal support. They include fibrous tissues of different density and cellularity and specialized forms such as bone, ligaments, tendons, cartilage, and adipose tissue.

Some organs are suspended by thin connective-tissue sheets called mesenteries, while others are embedded in adipose tissue, whose cells store energy-rich fat. Blood vessels run through connective tissue, so it is closely tied to the nourishment of organs and the removal of waste products.

Bone ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone)) supports the whole body through a skeleton that resists stress because of its laminated structure and mineralized matrix. Ligaments hold bones together, and tendons attach muscles to bones. Both are dense connective tissues with parallel fibre bundles that give great tensile strength.

At joints, bone surfaces are covered with cartilage, which contains abundant intercellular substance and allows smooth gliding movement. The synovial membrane lines joint margins, lubricates joint surfaces, and also belongs to the connective-tissue family.

## Cartilage

![[attachments/connective_tissue_002.webp|420]]

*Hyaline cartilage histology*

Cartilage ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilage)) is connective tissue with abundant firm gel-like ground substance, giving rigidity and resistance to compression. Its cells, called chondrocytes, lie in small spaces called lacunae within the matrix.

Cartilage is avascular, meaning it has no blood vessels inside it. Nutrients and gases diffuse through the watery phase of the matrix to reach the cells. The tissue is enclosed by the perichondrium, a dense fibrous layer with cells capable of secreting hyaline matrix.

Cartilage grows in two ways. Appositional growth adds new matrix and cells from the inner chondrogenic layer of the perichondrium. Interstitial growth occurs when young chondrocytes divide inside lacunae, secrete matrix between daughter cells, and separate into new spaces.

This dual growth makes cartilage useful in the embryonic skeleton, where rapid growth is needed. Many fetal cartilaginous skeletal elements are later replaced by bone.

Hyaline cartilage is the most widespread type. It has a pearl-gray semitranslucent matrix with randomly oriented collagen fibrils and little elastin. It occurs on joint surfaces and in the fetal skeleton.

Elastic cartilage has a pale yellow matrix rich in elastic fibres, making it more flexible than hyaline cartilage. It occurs mainly in the external ear, larynx, and epiglottis.

Fibrocartilage contains many dense parallel collagen bundles. Its cells lie in lacunae arranged in rows between collagen bundles. It occurs in intervertebral disks and at places where tendons attach to bone.

## Bone

![[attachments/connective_tissue_003.png|420]]

*Compact bone osteon diagram*

Like other connective tissues, bone contains cells, fibres, and ground substance. Its special feature is that the extracellular components are impregnated with calcium phosphate crystals as hydroxyapatite. This mineralization gives bone hardness.

Bone also serves as a major calcium reserve, which the body can draw on when calcium is needed elsewhere. Its organization gives high strength with minimum weight, allowing bones to support heavy bodies or provide lightweight internal support and leverage for bird wings.

## Key Events & Developments

- Embryonic development: Cartilage forms much of the early skeleton and is later replaced by bone in many areas.
- [[Biological Growth and Development (Growth)|Growth]]: Cartilage can grow by appositional and interstitial processes.
- Mineralization: Hydroxyapatite deposition makes bone hard and mechanically strong.

## Key People

- Histologists ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histologists)): Study the cells, fibres, matrix, and organization of connective tissues.
- Pathologists: Study disease processes affecting connective tissue, cartilage, bone, and joints.

## Interesting Facts

- Cartilage ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilage)) has no blood vessels, so diffusion through matrix is essential.
- Ligaments connect bone to bone, while tendons connect muscle to bone.
- Adipose tissue is connective tissue specialized for fat storage.
- Hydroxyapatite crystals make bone hard and useful as a calcium reserve.
