## Introduction

Sigmund Freud was an Austrian neurologist whose work transformed modern ideas about the mind, human behavior, and culture. Psychoanalysis is a theory of the psyche, a method of treatment, and a broader way of interpreting society. Although Freud’s ideas have been criticized and revised, his influence remained enormous in psychology, literature, and the social sciences.

## Development of Psychoanalysis

A decisive turning point came during his work with Jean-Martin Charcot in Paris and later with Josef Breuer in the treatment of patients with hysteria. Freud developed the idea that repressed emotional material could emerge through free association and that symptoms often reflected unconscious conflict. This became the basis of psychoanalytic treatment.
The importance of the unconscious ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconscious_mind)), resistance, and transference ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transference)). Freud argued that patients often hide painful memories and wishes, and that analysis could help bring them into awareness. He also introduced the idea that dreams are distorted expressions of hidden desires, a central part of his theory.

## Theory of Sexuality and Development

Freud’s work on sexuality became one of the most controversial parts of his legacy. He argued that sexual drives begin early in life and shape development, personality, and conflict. His model of psychosexual development included oral, anal, phallic, latency, and genital stages, and he used the Oedipus complex to explain the child’s emotional bonds with parents.

This theory has been widely criticized for its gender assumptions and for its dependence on a highly specific family model. Even so, it had major influence on later psychology and on the study of desire, childhood, and family relationships.

## Metapsychology and Culture

In his later work, Freud developed a broader theory of the mind through concepts such as the id, ego, and superego. He also introduced the idea of Eros and Thanatos, or life and death instincts, as forces shaping human behavior. This is his attempt to connect clinical psychology with a larger philosophy of human nature.

Freud then extended psychoanalysis beyond therapy into the study of art, religion, and society. He interpreted works of art and cultural forms through the idea of sublimation, and he examined religion, group life, and civilization as expressions of conflict between instinct and social order. His social theory was often dark and pessimistic, stressing that civilization depends on the repression of instinct.

## Legacy

Freud’s influence was not limited to psychology. His ideas shaped modern discussions of the self, childhood, desire, repression, and the hidden motives behind human action. Despite many critiques, Freud remains one of the most influential figures of the 20th century because he gave modern culture a new vocabulary for understanding the inner life.

## Key Events & Developments

- Freud trained in neurology in Vienna and worked with Charcot in Paris.
- He developed the method of free association and the theory of the unconscious.
- He published major works on dreams, slips of the tongue, and sexuality.
- He proposed the structural model of id, ego, and superego.
- He extended psychoanalysis to culture, religion, and civilization.
