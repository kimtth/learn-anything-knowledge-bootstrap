# Karachi

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Karachi is Pakistan's largest city, principal seaport, and leading commercial and industrial center on the Arabian Sea. Its expansion from an Indus-valley port reflects its roles in trade, finance, manufacturing, transportation, and the national life of Pakistan and Afghanistan.

## Introduction

![[attachments/karachi_001.webp|420]]

*Karachi skyline*

Karachi is Sindh's capital, Pakistan's largest city, main seaport, and major commercial and industrial centre. It stands on the Arabian Sea northwest of the Indus River Delta and serves Pakistan and Afghanistan. Its older names derive from Kalachi-Jo-goth.

Karachi's growth first came from its role as port for the Indus valley and Punjab. Air travel later added importance. The city covered 228 square miles, Greater Karachi 560, and population rose from 9,269,265 in 1998 to an estimated 12,130,000 in 2007.

## Climate

Karachi Harbour ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Karachi)) is protected by Kiamari Island, Manora Island, and Oyster Rocks. The city rises from a low coast to a broad plain crossed by seasonal streams such as the Malir and Layari rivers. The climate is pleasant most of the year, though May and June are hottest, with mean maximum temperatures near 93 F and occasional heat reaching 105 F.

Rainfall and environment are limited but important. Average rainfall is about 8 inches, mostly in June, July, and August. High humidity, stagnant water, factories, and automobiles create pollution problems. Vegetation is sparse, and wildlife includes gazelles, jackals, water birds, and snakes.

## City Layout

The city layout is marked by four west-east arterial roads beginning near Mereweather Tower and the port. The old town near the harbour is unplanned, while Drigh Cantonment, Civil Lines, and Saddar Bazar show planned European-style patterns. Outer growth follows radial roads and neighbourhood units.

Land use is mixed. Central residential districts contain commercial concentrations near M.A. Jinnah Road, I.I. Chundrigar Road, Saddar, and the old town, while government offices cluster near Shahrah-e-Liaquat. Outer areas include suburbs, cantonments, airports, rail facilities, and worker districts.

## Industry

Karachi's industry is led by textiles and footwear, with metal products, food, printing, furniture, machinery, chemicals, petroleum, leather, rubber, electrical goods, and handicrafts also important. The city handles all seaborne trade of Pakistan and Afghanistan.

## Finance

Karachi's finance includes more than 25 banks, major insurance companies, and a stock exchange. Insurance investment supports power, housing, companies, securities, and savings certificates, while the exchange handles most government-security transactions and shares of major institutions.

## Transportation

![[attachments/karachi_003.webp|420]]

[[Transportation]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)) links Karachi to Pakistan's interior, the coast, Iran, and the Middle East. It is the railway terminus, has passenger and circular rail services, Karachi Airport, and one of the busiest ports east of Suez.

## Government

Administration is divided among appointed bodies, including the Karachi Municipal Corporation, suburban committees, the Karachi Cantonment Board, and the Karachi Port Trust. Services include water from Lake Haleji, Malir River wells, and Lake Kalri; electricity; refuse collection; drainage; hospitals; fire services; and provincial police.

## Education

[[Education]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education)) includes more than 900 schools, more than half privately run, including religious-community schools. The University of Karachi is the main higher institution, with graduate departments, a business school, about 75 affiliated colleges, and specialized professional institutions.

Cultural life ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture)) centres on the Arts Council of Pakistan, dance institutions, amateur theatre, cinemas, museums, libraries, parks, beaches, clubs, and the National Stadium. Collections include Indus Valley relics, Gandharan art, and ethnological materials.

## History

Karachi began as a fishing village that grew after traders moved from Kharak Bandar in the early 18th century. The Talpura amirs fortified Manora Head in 1795. The British captured Karachi in 1839, annexed it with Sindh in 1842, and developed it as an army headquarters and Indus-region port.

British-era development connected Karachi to river, rail, telegraph, and sea routes. River steamers began in 1843, port improvements in 1854, rail to Kotri in 1861, telegraph links in 1864, and faster growth after the Suez Canal opened in 1869. By 1914 Karachi was the British Empire's largest grain-exporting port.

Modern Karachi became Sindh's provincial capital in 1936 and Pakistan's capital, chief port, and business centre in 1947. Although the capital moved to Islamabad in 1969, Karachi stayed the economic hub. Late-20th-century migration overloaded services, expanded katchi abadi shantytowns, and contributed to violence and crime.
