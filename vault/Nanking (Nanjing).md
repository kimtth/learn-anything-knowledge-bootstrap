# Nanking (Nanjing)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Nanjing is a major Chinese city whose river setting, economy, transport, institutions, and historic role have repeatedly made it a political center. Its periods as national capital, association with Chiang Kai-shek, and contrast with Beijing link urban development with modern Chinese state history.

## Introduction

![[attachments/nanjing_001.webp|420]]

*Nanjing skyline*

Nanjing, conventional Nanking, is the capital of Jiangsu province in east-central China. It is a Yangtze River port, industrial and communications centre, and historically important capital city. The name Nanjing, meaning Southern Capital, was introduced in 1403 during the Ming dynasty.

## Landscape

![[attachments/nanjing_002.webp|420]]

*Nanjing city wall*

The central districts of Nanjing stand on the southeastern bank of the Yangtze about 160 miles west of Shanghai. The old city is enclosed by a huge Ming wall, originally 21 miles long; roughly two-thirds still stands. The municipality extends across both sides of the Yangtze.

## Climate

Nanjing has four clear seasons. Summers from July to September are hot, winters last from December to March, and spring and autumn are mild. January averages about 37 F, July about 82 F, and annual rainfall is about 40 inches, mostly between June and August.

The city layout resembles a gourd pointing northwest toward Xiaguan on the Yangtze. The Qinhuai River, Purple-Gold Mountains, Qingliang Hill, and Xuanwu and Mochou lakes shape the centre. Gates, boulevards, lotus lakes, temples, hills, and wall remnants give Nanjing a spacious historic character.

## People

![[attachments/nanjing_003.webp|420]]

*Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum relief*

The people of Nanjing speak Mandarin with a marked local accent. Despite industrial growth, the municipality still includes a substantial rural population. Many residents profess no religion, but Christian, Buddhist, and Muslim communities are present.

## Agriculture

Agriculture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture)) remains important in rural districts and southern counties. Rice, wheat, peanuts, fruits, and vegetables are major crops, while lakes and ponds support aquatic plants and fishing. Salted pressed Nanjing duck is a noted specialty.

## Manufacturing

Before 1949, Nanjing was known for satins, velvets, and brocades. It later became an industrial centre producing steel, machine tools, vehicles, clocks, optical instruments, building materials, textiles, food products, petrochemicals, electronics, and automobiles. Nearby minerals include iron, manganese, copper, limestone, lead, zinc, and major strontium reserves.

## Transportation

The Yangtze River is Nanjing's main commercial route, linking it to the delta and central China. The port has specialized wharves, railways connect the city to Shanghai and other regions, and the first Yangtze bridge opened in 1968 with rail below and highway above. Later bridges, light rail, highways, and Lukou International Airport expanded transport.

## Government

Nanjing's municipal government ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality)) belongs to China's hierarchical state and Communist Party structure. The Municipal People's Congress handles orders, taxes, budgets, and economic plans, while the Nanjing People's Government manages executive administration. After reorganization in 1995, Nanjing had 11 districts and 2 southern counties.

## Health

Public health improved during the Nationalist period and under communist public health education. Hospitals and specialized medicine expanded, some clinics were partly privatized after the 1990s, and Nanjing became a centre for training doctors in traditional Chinese medicine.

## Education

Nanjing inherited a strong school system from the Nationalist era. Since the 1950s, primary, middle, technical, adult, and extension education expanded. Leading institutions include Nanjing University, Southeast University, Nanjing University of Aeronautics and Astronautics, and Nanjing University of Science and Technology.

Nanjing's cultural life ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture)) reflects its long history through museums, traditional-style buildings, scientific societies, painting institutes, opera troupes, sports grounds, Wutaishan Stadium, and the Nanjing Olympic Sports Centre completed in 2005.

## History

Nanjing's recorded history begins when the Yue built a castle near Yuhuatai in 472 BCE. Later Chu rulers built Jinling. As Jianye, it became capital of Wu from 229 to 280, then as Jiankang it became the refuge capital of the Eastern Jin and later southern dynasties.

The Six Dynasties period from 220 to 589 made Nanjing a political, economic, and cultural centre south of the Yangtze. Harvests, tea, silk, papermaking, and pottery supported growth, while scholars, poets, artists, and philosophers shaped calligraphy, painting, anthologies, and criticism.

## Chiang Kai-shek

Under the Nationalists, Chiang Kai-shek made Nanjing his capital. The city fell to Japan in 1937, and the ensuing massacre killed large numbers of civilians, with estimates ranging from 40,000 to 300,000. Puppet governments ruled until 1945; communist forces took the city in 1949.

## Nanjing, Jiangsu

After the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949, Beijing became national capital and Nanjing became Jiangsu's provincial capital in 1952. It industrialized, suffered during the Great Leap Forward and Cultural Revolution, and prospered from the 1980s as a Yangtze delta centre.
