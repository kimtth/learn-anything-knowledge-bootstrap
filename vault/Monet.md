# Monet

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Claude Monet was a French painter whose early training, plein-air work, and collaborations helped form Impressionism. His changing treatment of light, color, atmosphere, landscape, Giverny gardens, and serial subjects makes his career central to the development and later assessment of the movement.

## Introduction

![[attachments/monet_001.webp|420]]

*Portrait of Claude Monet*

Oscar-Claude Monet was a French painter and central advocate of Impressionism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionism)). Born in 1840 and raised near Le Havre, he absorbed the sea and fast-changing Normandy weather that shaped his vision. His mature art used repeated studies of motifs under changing light, including haystacks, Rouen Cathedral, London, Venice, and Giverny water lilies.

## Early Life

Monet first succeeded through caricatures sold when he was about 15 and precise sketches of sailing ships. His aunt Marie-Jeanne Lecadre encouraged drawing, but Eugene Boudin decisively introduced him to outdoor painting, fixing his lifelong concern with natural light and direct perception.

## Early Training and Youthful Work

After service in Algeria from 1861 to 1862, Monet returned to Le Havre, painted with Boudin, and met Johan Barthold Jongkind. In Paris he studied with Charles Gleyre and met Frederic Bazille, Alfred Sisley, and Pierre-Auguste Renoir. Early works such as Dejeuner sur l'herbe ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_D%C3%A9jeuner_sur_l%27herbe)) and Women in the Garden ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_Garden)) joined contemporary subjects with outdoor light.

## Formation of Impressionism

![[attachments/monet_002.webp|420]]

*Impression, Sunrise by Monet*

Monet sold little during the 1860s but gained Salon notice, especially for a portrait of Camille. At La Grenouillere, he and Renoir painted bathers, boats, reflections, and water with quick strokes that anticipated Impressionist style. During and after the Franco-German War he worked in London, the Netherlands, and Argenteuil.

## Later Impressionism

![[attachments/monet_003.webp|420]]

*Water Lilies by Monet*

Monet's series method fully emerged in the 1890s, but the Gare Saint-Lazare paintings of 1876-1877 already studied one modern subject through smoke, steam, and light. At Vetheuil, debts, Camille's illness, and his relationship with Alice Hoschede reshaped his life; Camille died in 1879. Even as Impressionism dispersed, Monet kept studying nature in France, Italy, and the Netherlands.

## Giverny and Final Series

In 1883, Monet, Alice Hoschede, her children, and his sons settled at Giverny. He built the garden and water-lily pond that became his final subject. After 1900, London fog, Thames bridges, Parliament, Venice, and especially water lilies turned architecture, water, atmosphere, reflection, and colour into increasingly large and abstract-looking paintings. Despite cataracts, he painted almost until his death in 1926.

## Assessment

Critical acclaim for Monet came slowly, but American collectors supported him from the 1880s. His influence reached Vincent van Gogh, Pierre Bonnard, Henri Matisse, and others, while an American colony at Giverny carried aspects of his style into American art. Interest revived in the early 1950s, when his scale and formal innovation influenced Abstract Expressionists such as Mark Rothko and [[Jackson Pollock]].

## Key Events & Developments

- 1840: Monet was born in Paris.
- 1874: Impressionism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impressionism)) gained public identity through an independent exhibition.
- 1883: Monet settled at Giverny.
- 1926: Monet died after painting despite cataracts.
