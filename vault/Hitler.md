# Hitler

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Adolf Hitler was the Nazi dictator whose racial ideology, authoritarian rule, aggressive expansionism, and leadership of Germany brought Europe into catastrophic war and enabled mass persecution and genocide. His rise and regime illustrate the destructive political consequences of Nazism, propaganda, and totalitarian power.

## Introduction

Adolf Hitler, known by the title Der Fuhrer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BChrer)), was born on April 20, 1889, in Braunau am Inn, Austria. He became leader of the National Socialist (Nazi) Party in 1920/21, chancellor of Germany on January 30, 1933, and ruler under the joined offices of Fuhrer and chancellor after Paul von Hindenburg died in 1934. His father, Alois Hitler, had once used the surname Schicklgruber ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schicklgruber)), but Adolf never used any other surname.

Hitler died in [[Berlin]] on April 30, 1945, as the Nazi state collapsed. His rule had moved from party agitation to dictatorship, world war, racial persecution, and finally German defeat, leaving Europe devastated and Germany facing what later became known as Year Zero, 1945.

## Rise to Power

![[attachments/hitler_003.webp|420]]

*Signed Photograph of Adolf Hitler and His Best Wishes for Reza Shah Pahlavi - Sahebgharanie Palace - Niavar...*

Hitler spent much of his childhood in Linz, remained attached to the city, and left school without higher education. He feared and disliked his father but was devoted to his mother, who died in 1907. His early hope was to become an artist, but in Vienna he twice failed to enter the Academy of Fine Arts and lived insecurely by painting postcards and advertisements.

Life in [[Vienna]] strengthened traits that later marked Hitler: loneliness, secrecy, a bohemian routine, hostility to cosmopolitan life, and hatred of the city's multinational character. In 1913 he moved to Munich. Though rejected for Austrian military service, he volunteered for the German army when [[World Wars (World War I)|World War I]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I)) began, served as a headquarters runner, was wounded and gassed, and received both classes of the Iron Cross.

War gave Hitler discipline, comradeship, and a sense of purpose. After Germany's defeat, he entered politics in Munich in 1919, joined the German Workers' Party, and became its propaganda chief as it turned into the Nazi Party. Postwar resentment, economic hardship, Bavarian hostility to Berlin, and anger over the peace settlement made conditions favorable for a radical right-wing movement.

Through speeches, meetings, and the Volkischer Beobachter, Hitler turned a small party into a movement loyal to him and attracted figures such as Alfred Rosenberg, Rudolf Hess, Hermann Goring, and Julius Streicher. The Beer Hall Putsch of November 1923, attempted with Erich Ludendorff, failed after police and army fire stopped the marchers, but the treason trial gave Hitler publicity.

Prison at Landsberg gave Hitler time to dictate the first volume of Mein Kampf and taught him that power should be gained through legal forms. His ideas made racial inequality, the supposed superiority of the Aryan race, the German Volk, antisemitism, and rejection of the Weimar Republic central to politics. After slow party growth, the 1929 Depression, alliance with Alfred Hugenberg, industrial support, and mass propaganda lifted the Nazi vote from 2.6 percent in 1928 to more than 18 percent in 1930.

## Dictator, 1933–39

Once chancellor, Hitler built an absolute dictatorship. The Reichstag fire of February 27, 1933, became the excuse to suspend freedoms and intensify violence. The Nazis won 43.9 percent in the March 1933 election, and the Enabling Bill of March 23 gave Hitler full powers. Within months, non-Nazi parties, organizations, and labor unions disappeared.

Hitler needed conservative, industrial, and army support, so he avoided a broad social revolution. The challenge from Ernst Rohm and the SA ended in the Night of the Long Knives in June 1934, when Rohm, Edmund Heines, Gregor Strasser, Kurt von Schleicher, and others were killed without trial. After Hindenburg died, the presidency and chancellorship were merged, and the army swore personal loyalty to Hitler.

Economic recovery, falling unemployment, propaganda, and police terror increased the regime's support. Hitler set broad policy and upheld the terror system while leaving many details to rival subordinates. Foreign policy aimed to overturn the Treaty of Versailles, present Germany as a barrier against Bolshevism, prepare expansion eastward, and use Italy as an ally while testing the caution of Britain and France.

Hitler left the Disarmament Conference and League of Nations in 1933, signed a nonaggression treaty with [[Poland]], regained the Saarland, restored conscription, and remilitarized the Rhineland in 1936. He then achieved Anschluss with Austria and used the Sudeten crisis, Konrad Henlein, Benito Mussolini, and Neville Chamberlain's diplomacy to win the Munich Agreement of September 30, 1938.

The promise that the Sudetenland was his final territorial demand soon proved false. Hitler occupied the rest of Czechia on March 15, 1939, forced Lithuania to cede Memel, confirmed the Pact of Steel with Italy, and signed a nonaggression pact with Joseph Stalin's Soviet Union on August 23, 1939. The invasion of [[Poland]] on September 1 brought war with Britain and France.

## World War II

Hitler directed German strategy from the start of [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II)). After Poland fell without producing peace with Britain, he ordered a western offensive, occupied [[Denmark]] and [[Norway]] in April 1940, and adopted Erich von Manstein's plan for an attack through the Ardennes. The western campaign reached the Channel in 10 days, forced Dutch and Belgian surrender, and ended with a French armistice on June 22, 1940, although the halt near Dunkirk allowed much of the British army to escape.

When Britain refused peace, Hitler planned invasion and air war while preparing to attack the Soviet Union. Italian failures drew German forces into Greece, the Balkans, and North Africa, and a coup in Yugoslavia led to another campaign. These operations remained secondary to Operation Barbarossa, launched on June 22, 1941, which captured millions of Soviet prisoners but failed to destroy Soviet resistance.

The Soviet counteroffensive near [[Moscow]] in December 1941 ended hopes for a single victorious campaign. After [[Japan]] attacked Pearl Harbor, Hitler declared war on the United States, widening the conflict. Defeat at Stalingrad, Allied bombing, U-boat failure, worsening relations with army commanders, and the growing role of the SS made German victory increasingly unlikely.

The [[Holocaust]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust)) was central to Hitler's wartime responsibility. No single written extermination order is cited, but his speeches, writings, discussions, recorded statements, and testimony from perpetrators show his role in moving policy from expulsion to extermination of Jews across Europe and European Russia. His January 30, 1939, Reichstag threat spoke of annihilation if war came, and his final testament still demanded struggle against Jews.

After Mussolini was arrested in July 1943, Hitler ordered German occupation of key Italian positions and Mussolini's rescue. Anti-Nazi officers and civilians planned attempts to remove him, the closest being Claus von Stauffenberg's bomb on July 20, 1944. Hitler survived, most conspirators were executed, and military headquarters received National Socialist political officers.

The Allied invasion of Normandy on June 6, 1944, marked the beginning of the end. Several European capitals were liberated or surrendered, and the Ardennes offensive of December 1944 failed. After January 1945, Hitler stayed in the Berlin Chancellery and bunker as Soviet forces approached, then married Eva Braun, named Karl Donitz and Josef Goebbels in his testament, and killed himself on April 30, 1945.

## Historical responsibility and interpretation

Hitler's rise was not inevitable, but he exploited postwar German bitterness, political crisis, propaganda, and personal authority with unusual skill. His power was unprecedented in scope, and his ideas were accepted wholly or partly by millions. Historical judgment places principal responsibility for starting [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II)) on him and treats his responsibility for the Holocaust as clear.

Claims that Hitler was simply mad do not remove responsibility. Medical evidence points to hypochondria, heavy medication during the war, late symptoms of Parkinson disease, and a belief by 1938 that he might not live long, but not to disabling illness through most of his rule. His memory for details and instinct for opponents' weaknesses made his decisions more deliberate, not less.

Hitler's broad support among Germans and Austrians was deeper than support for the Nazi Party itself. Economic recovery, restored national confidence, foreign policy successes, propaganda, and terror sustained trust in him. Open and hidden admirers continued to exist, but the crimes and brutalities tied to his name make his reputation as an incarnation of evil unlikely to change.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1889: Adolf Hitler was born in Braunau am Inn, Austria.
- 1914-18: Hitler served in the German army during [[World Wars (World War I)|World War I]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I)) and received two Iron Cross decorations.
- 1919-23: Hitler entered politics, built the Nazi Party, and failed in the Beer Hall Putsch.
- 1933-34: Hitler became chancellor, gained full powers, crushed opposition, and merged the presidency with the chancellorship.
- 1936-39: [[Germany]] remilitarized the Rhineland, annexed Austria, dismantled Czechoslovakia, and invaded Poland.
- 1941-45: Operation Barbarossa, war with the United States, Allied offensives, the July 20 plot, and Soviet entry into Berlin led to Nazi collapse.

## Key People

- Adolf Hitler ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler)): Nazi leader, German dictator, war leader, and central figure responsible for aggressive war and racial persecution.
- Alois Hitler ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Hitler)): Hitler's father, whose family name replaced Schicklgruber before Adolf's birth.
- Paul von Hindenburg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_von_Hindenburg)): German president whose death allowed Hitler to merge the presidency and chancellorship.
- Ernst Rohm ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Rohm)): SA leader killed during the Night of the Long Knives after threatening Hitler's alliance with the army.
- Benito Mussolini ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini)): Fascist Italian ally, Munich mediator, and later dependent on German rescue.
- Joseph Stalin ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)): Soviet leader who signed the 1939 nonaggression pact before becoming Hitler's major eastern enemy.
- Claus von Stauffenberg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claus_von_Stauffenberg)): German officer whose July 20, 1944, bomb plot nearly killed Hitler.
- Eva Braun ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Braun)): Hitler's wife during the final bunker hours before their deaths.
