# Arthropods

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Arthropods are the largest and most diverse animal phylum, distinguished by segmented bodies, jointed appendages, and a chitinous exoskeleton that must be molted for growth. Crustaceans, chelicerates, insects, and myriapods occupy nearly every habitat and are crucial to food webs, pollination, decomposition, food supplies, and the transmission of disease.

## Introduction

Arthropods ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod)) are members of the phylum Arthropoda, the largest phylum in the animal kingdom. They include familiar forms such as lobsters, crabs, spiders, mites, [[Insects|insects]], centipedes, and millipedes. About 84 percent of all known animal species belong to this phylum.

They live in nearly every habitat on Earth and show an enormous range of adaptations. Their key distinguishing feature is a jointed external skeleton made of chitin bound to protein. The body is usually segmented, and the segments typically bear paired, jointed appendages.

## Diversity and Major Groups

![[attachments/arthropods_002.webp|420]]

*Trilobite fossil showing an extinct arthropod body form*

Living arthropods divide into four principal subphyla: Chelicerata ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelicerata)), Crustacea ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustacean)), Hexapoda, and Myriapoda ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myriapoda)). It also includes the extinct Trilobitomorpha, whose trilobites were dominant in early Paleozoic seas.

The subphyla differ greatly in habitat and structure. Crustaceans are mostly aquatic, especially marine. Many chelicerates, such as spiders and scorpions, are mainly terrestrial. Myriapods live in soil and leaf litter, and insects occupy almost every kind of terrestrial habitat, with some entering fresh water.

## Importance to Humans

Arthropods are of both direct and indirect importance to humans. Many larger crustaceans, such as shrimps, lobsters, and crabs, are important foods. Small crustaceans like copepods and krill are crucial links in marine food chains, feeding fish, whales, and many other animals. Their agricultural importance is also great. Many flowering plants depend on insect pollination, and arthropods living in soil and leaf mold help break down organic matter and form humus. At the same time, some arthropods damage crops, timber, or stored food, and others transmit important diseases.

## Arthropods as Disease Carriers

Although relatively few arthropods inject dangerous toxins, many are medically important because they carry disease. Examples include malaria, yellow fever, dengue fever, African sleeping sickness, typhus, bubonic plague, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, and Lyme disease.

This role makes arthropods highly significant not only ecologically but also in public health and veterinary medicine. Many diseases of domestic animals are likewise spread by them.

## Exoskeleton and Molting

The success of arthropods depends greatly on the evolution of their exoskeleton. This outer covering supports the body, protects it, and works with muscles to make movement efficient. In many terrestrial arthropods, waxes in the outer layer also help reduce water loss.

Because the exoskeleton is rigid, growth requires molting, or ecdysis. The old skeleton is partly digested, split open, and shed, and a new soft skeleton expands before it hardens. This solves the problem of growing inside a hard outer covering.

## Form and Function

Segmentation and specialized appendages are central to arthropod success. Body parts can be modified for walking, feeding, sensing, grasping, reproducing, swimming, or gas exchange. This flexibility in body design allowed arthropods to adapt to many ecological roles.

Their body plan also includes a reduced coelom, a nervous system with a brain and ventral nerve cord, and rich-yolked eggs. Development is often highly modified, which contributes further to their diversity.

## Classification

![[attachments/arthropods_001.webp|420]]

*Ant worker anatomy diagram showing arthropod body regions*

Arthropod classification relies on features such as segmentation, number and form of appendages, antennae, mouthparts, reproductive openings, and respiratory or excretory structures. An annotated classification begins with the extinct trilobites and moves through the major living groups.

Among Chelicerata ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelicerata)) are the Merostomata, including horseshoe crabs, the Arachnida, including spiders, scorpions, ticks, and mites, and the marine Pycnogonida, or sea spiders. A major defining feature of chelicerates is the absence of antennae and the presence of chelicerae as the first appendages near the mouth.

## Crustaceans

Crustacea ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crustacean)) are chiefly aquatic and usually bear two pairs of antennae, mandibles, maxillae, and often a carapace. Their appendages are often biramous, meaning branched. Crustaceans include many familiar marine animals and also freshwater and a few terrestrial forms.

This subphylum is ecologically broad, ranging from large bottom-dwelling crabs and shrimps to tiny planktonic copepods. In the sea, crustaceans remain especially dominant.

## Myriapods and Insects

Myriapoda ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myriapoda)) are chiefly terrestrial arthropods with unbranched appendages and tracheal respiration. It includes centipedes, millipedes, symphylans, and pauropods. These groups often live in soil, under logs, and in leaf mold.

Insecta have a body divided into head, thorax, and abdomen, three pairs of legs, and usually wings on the thorax. With at least one million described species, insects make up by far the largest share of known arthropod diversity.

## Evolutionary Relationships

Relationships both within Arthropoda and between arthropods and other animal phyla remain uncertain. Older views often linked arthropods closely with [[Annelids|annelids]] because of segmented body plans, but modern evidence has complicated that picture.

Relationships within the phylum are also debated. Some evidence links insects closely to crustaceans, while other connections involve myriapods and chelicerates in unexpected ways. Arthropod evolution is an active field of research rather than a settled story.

## Conclusion

Arthropods ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthropod)) are the most successful and varied major group of animals. Their segmented bodies, jointed appendages, and molting exoskeleton gave them extraordinary adaptive power across land, fresh water, sea, and air.

They matter not only because they are numerous but because they shape ecosystems, agriculture, disease transmission, and food webs at every scale. They are biologically dominant and scientifically central animals.

## Key Events & Developments

- Trilobites dominate early Paleozoic seas before extinction.
- Modern arthropod diversity expands across land, water, and air.
- Insects become the most species-rich arthropod group.
- Arthropods become central to agriculture, ecology, and disease transmission.
