## Introduction

Forestry ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry)) is the management of forest land, waters, and related waste land for the purpose of producing wood and protecting natural resources. Modern forestry has expanded beyond timber harvesting to include soil conservation, water management, wildlife protection, and recreation. The subject reaches from ancient human use of wood to its development as a scientific profession.

Forests provide fuel, construction material, paper, and many other products, while also supporting ecosystems and human well-being. Forestry is therefore both an economic activity and a field of environmental stewardship.

## History

![[attachments/forestry_and_wood_production_forestry_001.webp|420]]

*Wood Pellet Machine*
Early humans used wood for fire and construction long before recorded history. Homo erectus used wood for fire at least 750,000 years ago, while the oldest evidence of wood used for construction, found at the Kalambo Falls site in Tanzania, dates from about 60,000 years ago. Early communities along the rivers of India, Pakistan, Egypt, and Mesopotamia used riverbank trees for fuel, building, and tool handles, and carpenters built wooden boats as early as 2700 BCE.

Classical authors recorded much of this knowledge. Theophrastus, Varro, Pliny, Cato, and [[Virgil]] were among the writers who described trees, their classification, growth, and the conditions that affect them. The Romans in particular imported tree seedlings throughout the Mediterranean region and Germany, creating groves like those of Carthage and Lebanon and showing that planned forest care has very old roots.

It then explains the decline of forest conservation after the fall of [[Rome]], when unregulated cutting, fire, and the grazing of sheep and goats destroyed forests. This caused serious soil loss, silting of streams and harbours, and the spread of a scrubby brush cover known as maquis. Formal education in forestry began about 1825 with the first private forestry schools, marking the shift toward a scientific profession.

## Medieval Europe and Early Modern Management

![[attachments/forestry_and_wood_production_forestry_002.webp|420]]

*American forestry (1910-1923)*

In medieval Europe, forest laws were first aimed mainly at protecting game and defining rights. Hunting rights and the sole right to cut and export timber belonged to the feudal lord, while peasants could gather fuel, timber, and litter and pasture a set number of animals. By 1165 land clearing for farming had gone so far that Germany forbade further forest removal. True systematic management began in the German states during the 16th century, when each forest property was divided into sections for harvesting and regeneration to ensure a sustainable yield, supported by accurate maps and estimates of timber volume and growth.
Planned tree nurseries and plantations also arose. The earliest recorded planned nursery was that of William Blair, cellarer of the Abbey of Coupar Angus in Scotland, who raised trees for the Highland Forest of Ferter as early as 1460. After the dissolution of the monasteries, newly rich landowners planted poor land for long-term profit; John Evelyn urged this in his classic textbook Sylva in 1664. Today nearly all of Britain's 2.1 million hectares of woodland is artificial plantation, and the 20th century brought a huge expansion of planted forests on every continent to meet growing needs for wood and paper.

## Forest Types and Distribution

Forestry depends strongly on the type of forest and its environment. Forests are shaped by climate, rainfall, temperature, and soil, which determine whether conifers, broad-leaved trees, or mixed forests dominate. It distinguishes between open forests, savannas, thorn forests, taiga, temperate deciduous forests, subtropical evergreens, and tropical rainforests.

It also describes major tree groups. [[Gymnosperms]] are of very ancient lineage and bear their seeds in cones; the conifers, or softwoods, include pines, spruces, firs, cedars, junipers, hemlocks, and sequoias, and most are evergreen except the ginkgo, larches, and bald cypress. [[Angiosperms]], the dominant plants of the present era, reproduce through the flower and include broad-leaved trees split into monocots and dicots. The monocots cover palms, which give coconuts, dates, and carnauba wax, and bamboos, the giant woody grasses of Asia used as construction material and food. The dicots, or hardwoods, have complex wood suited to particular uses, with oaks, beeches, ashes, birches, elms, alders, and sweet chestnuts typical of temperate deciduous forests.

Forests cover a vast share of the world's land. About 4 billion hectares, or roughly one-third of the world's land, is covered by closed and open forests. Coniferous forests occupy about 1.1 billion hectares in the cooler Northern Hemisphere, with some 85 percent in [[North America]] and the former Soviet Union. The northern coniferous forest, or taiga, stretches from the Pacific to the Atlantic across North America, northern Europe, Scandinavia, Russia, Siberia, Mongolia, and northern Japan, with outliers along the Rockies, Appalachians, Alps, Urals, and Himalayas. Subtropical evergreen forests grow in Mediterranean climates such as California and Australia, while tropical rain forests follow the Equator through West and Central Africa, South Asia, and Central and South America, holding a great variety of species including teak.

## Silviculture and Forest Management

Silviculture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silviculture)) is the branch of forestry concerned with establishing, managing, and improving forests. It relies on knowledge from ecology, plant physiology, entomology, and soil science. Silviculture includes reproduction, intermediate cuttings, and protection, all aimed at maintaining productive and healthy forests.

Its immediate natural-science foundation is silvics, the study of the laws behind the growth of single trees and of the forest as a biological unit, shaped by soils, climate, competition, and relations with animals and insects. Silviculture is divided into three areas: methods of reproduction, intermediate cuttings, and protection. A central concept is the rotation, the period during which one crop or generation grows before harvest. Intermediate cuttings, or thinnings, are made between regeneration and maturity to improve the stand, regulate growth, and provide early returns. Forestry is not only about cutting trees but also about regeneration and long-term planning.

## Forest Ecosystem Services

Forests produce timber, fuel, fibre, pulp, food, fodder, medicines, resins, and habitat, but their importance is not limited to products. They regulate water flow, store carbon, protect soils, shape local climates, support biodiversity, and provide recreation and cultural value. Forestry therefore has to balance harvest with fire risk, pests, erosion, watershed protection, wildlife, local livelihoods, and long-term regeneration. The hardest management problems arise when short-term wood demand conflicts with slow forest growth and ecological complexity.

## Agroforestry and Urban Forestry
Agroforestry ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agroforestry)) grows trees and crops together on the same land. This approach has long been used in developing regions and is now promoted as a way to provide both food and wood products, along with protective benefits such as windbreaks and fodder.

It additionally mentions urban forestry, the management of trees in and around cities. Urban forests provide environmental and social benefits, but they also require special care because city trees are often more highly valued and more intensively managed than rural trees.

## Key Events & Developments

- Early human use of wood dates back to at least 750,000 years ago.
- Roman tree planting and forest management influenced later European practice.
- Germany introduced systematic forest planning in the 16th century.
- Britain expanded artificial plantations in the 17th century and later.
- Modern forestry expanded in the 20th century to include conservation and multiple use.
