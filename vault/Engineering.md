## Introduction

Engineering ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering)) is defined as the application of science to the optimum conversion of the resources of nature to the uses of humankind. The Engineers Council for Professional Development in the United States provides a formal definition: the creative application of scientific principles to design or develop structures, machines, apparatus, or manufacturing processes, either singly or in combination, including the construction or operation of these systems with full awareness of their design, and the ability to forecast their behaviour under specific operating conditions — all with regard to intended function, economics of operation, and safety to life and property.

Engineering practice is organized into several major functions. Research engineers use mathematics, experiment, and inductive reasoning to seek new principles and processes; development engineers turn research into useful working models; and design engineers select methods, materials, and shapes to meet technical and performance requirements. Construction engineers prepare sites and direct the safe, economical placement of materials and crews, while production engineers plan plant layout, choose tools and processes, and arrange testing and inspection. Operation engineers run the machines, plants, and organizations that provide power, transport, and communication, and engineers in management analyze customers' needs and recommend economical solutions.

## Key Events & Developments

- The distinction between military engineer and civil engineer emerged early in history, as the skills used to build war machines were redirected toward civilian infrastructure such as roads, water supplies, and buildings.
- The establishment of the Engineers Council for Professional Development in the United States formalized a definition of engineering that emphasized creativity, scientific grounding, economic awareness, and public safety.
- The growth of professional societies for engineers created organized standards of practice and established the engineer's responsibility to the public as a guiding principle of the profession.

## Interesting Facts

- The words *engine* and *ingenious* both come from the same Latin root, ingenerare, meaning "to create," connecting the craft of engineering directly to the idea of human creativity.
- In Great Britain, the term *engineering* has historically been used more loosely than in the United States, sometimes referring simply to the manufacture or assembly of engines, machine tools, and machine parts.
- The core philosophical distinction of the field is concise: the scientist's role is to know; the engineer's role is to do.
