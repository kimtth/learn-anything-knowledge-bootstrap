# Jakarta

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Jakarta is Indonesia's capital and largest urban center, located on Java's northwest coast. Its port, trade, government, migration, transport, and layered colonial and national history make it the country's dominant political and economic metropolis.

## Introduction

![[attachments/jakarta_001.webp|420]]

*Panoramic view of Jakarta skyline*

Jakarta, formerly Batavia until 1949 and Djakarta from 1949 to 1972, is Indonesia's capital and largest city. It lies on Java's northwest coast at the mouth of the Ciliwung River.

Greater Jakarta ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_metropolitan_area)), or Jakarta Raya, is nearly coextensive with the special capital district, though the district also includes small offshore islands. In 1966 Jakarta gained province-like status as a special capital district. The city is a national administrative, trade, financial, industrial, port, and education centre.

## Landscape and Climate

![[attachments/jakarta_002.webp|420]]

*Jakarta skyline panorama*

Jakarta's site is a low, flat alluvial plain with old swamp areas. Inland parts are slightly higher, but rainy-season flooding remains persistent. Swamp drainage, building, and upland forest loss increased flood risk, while clean drinking water remains scarce.

The climate ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate)) is tropical and humid. Temperatures range from about 75 to 93 degrees F, or 24 to 34 degrees C, and annual rainfall exceeds 67 inches. Sea winds moderate heat, but air and noise pollution remain serious.

The city layout mixes colonial planning, Indonesian life, and later expansion. Kota, or Old Batavia, is the historical centre and includes an important Chinese population. Central Jakarta's business and financial core lies farther south, while Kemayoran, Senen, and Jatinegara became major commercial and transport areas.

## People and Economy

Jakarta's population has grown dramatically since 1940, largely because immigration draws people seeking work. West Javanese, Central Javanese, East Javanese, and Sumatran migrants are major groups, while Arabs, Indians, Europeans, and Americans form smaller communities. The young population gives the city strong natural growth potential.

The economy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy)) combines capital-city control, administration, industry, trade, banking, and services. Jakarta handles about one-fourth of Indonesia's trade and services and roughly two-thirds of its banking and finance. Commerce stays central, while industry and new housing often move outward.

Manufacturing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing)) includes metalwork, repair shops, soap, printing, machinery, cigarettes, paper, glassware, cable, automotive products, textiles, food processing, brewing, and film. Markets such as Pasar Kota, Pasar Senen, Pasar Glodok, Pasar Jatinegara, Pasar Rumput, and Jalan Surabaya support retail trade and daily life.

## Transportation and Services

[[Transportation]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)) connects Jakarta with Java, Sumatra, and wider Indonesia, but congestion and pollution are chronic problems. The port of Tanjung Priok is Indonesia's largest, handling exports from West Java, imports, and transshipment to other islands. Roads, railways, buses, airports, taxis, and ferries link the city to surrounding regions.

[[The Forms Of Government Their Historical Development (Government)|Government]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government)) reflects Jakarta's special district status. The executive branch includes a governor, vice governors, staff, directorates, bureaus, and agencies. The city is divided into five administrative municipalities, while local and national government offices are closely coordinated.

Public services include state electricity, gas, communications, water supply, sanitation, hospitals, clinics, family-planning facilities, schools, madrasahs, vocational institutions, and more than 100 higher-education institutions. The University of Indonesia was founded in 1950.

## Culture and History

![[attachments/jakarta_003.webp|420]]

*Fatahillah Square in Jakarta Old Town*

Cultural life ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture)) includes Taman Ismail Marzuki, wayang dance and drama, gamelan, wayang kulit shadow plays, Jakarta Fair programs, Ancol recreation, Ragunan Zoological Gardens, restored Dutch-influenced historic areas, and Taman Mini Indonesia Indah.

Jakarta's early settlements may date to the 5th century CE, but its official history begins in 1527, when the sultan of Bantam defeated the Portuguese and named the place Jayakerta, or Glorious Fortress. Jan Pieterszoon Coen and the Dutch captured and razed it in 1619, then founded Batavia as the capital of the Dutch East Indies.

Colonial Batavia passed through company, southern-expansion, and modernization phases. Japanese occupation during World War II ended Dutch control in practice, and Indonesian nationalists declared independence on August 17, 1945. Batavia remained internationally recognized until Jakarta's proclamation as capital on December 27, 1949.

Postindependence Jakarta grew rapidly. Sukarno supported large construction projects in the 1950s and 1960s, including the National Monument area, Hotel Indonesia, and the Senayan Sports Complex.
