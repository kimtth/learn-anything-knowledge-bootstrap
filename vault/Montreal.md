# Montreal

## French Montreal

Montreal is a major city in southeastern Quebec and Canada's second most populous metropolitan area. It occupies much of Montreal Island near the Ottawa and St. Lawrence rivers, rises around Mount Royal, and serves as a major St. Lawrence Seaway ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_Seaway)) port between the Atlantic and the Great Lakes.

The city's character is cosmopolitan and strongly bilingual, though French identity is central. English long dominated commerce, while Quebec language law in 1977 restricted English in schools, government, and business. The rise of the Parti Quebecois in 1976 intensified debates over language, identity, and independence.

Montreal's layout has been shaped by the river, Mount Royal, and rising terrain. Boulevard Saint-Laurent traditionally divides east from west. Since about 1958, prosperity, Expo 67, and planning transformed downtown through Place Ville-Marie, connected skyscrapers, and underground shops, restaurants, and theatres, while Old Montreal preserved the historic centre.

## People

Montreal's population grew from about 133,000 in 1871 to roughly ten times that during the 20th century. French speakers make up about two-thirds of residents, while immigration and political change reduced English-speaking dominance. Social tension has reflected French-English separation and economic inequality.

## Economy

Montreal's economy developed from the fur trade into shipping, manufacturing, finance, railroads, aviation administration, and services. It remains a headquarters city for major institutions, including the International Civil Aviation Organization. Older industries included brewing, distilling, wood, and leather, while newer production includes telecommunications equipment and pharmaceuticals.

## Transportation

Public transportation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport)) includes buses and the Metro subway, whose four lines run under the city and to Longueuil. The system dates to 1847 and was electrified by 1894. Dorval handles commercial domestic and international flights, while Mirabel handles charter flights and air cargo.

## Government

Montreal is governed by a mayor, council, and executive committee. The Montreal Urban Community, created in 1969, handled islandwide services such as taxes, traffic, water, sewage, police, fire protection, and pollution control. Modernization, white-collar growth, and French-English inequality created administrative pressure.

## Education

[[Education]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education)) historically followed Quebec's dual Roman Catholic and Protestant public school system, with language tied to religious division. McGill University and Concordia University mainly serve English-language instruction, while the University of Montreal and University of Quebec at Montreal serve French-speaking students. Place des Arts, museums, libraries, galleries, publishing houses, theatres, and ice hockey support a major cultural life.

## History

The site was called Hochelaga when Jacques Cartier visited in 1535-1536. Samuel de Champlain later found Hochelaga gone and identified Place Royale. In 1642, Paul de Chomedey de Maisonneuve founded Ville-Marie, the origin of Montreal. Jeanne Mance founded the Hotel-Dieu hospital, and Marguerite Bourgeoys founded a girls' school.

Montreal grew through seigniorial settlement, farming, exploration, and trade. By 1672 its population reached about 1,500, but it was incorporated only in 1833. It surrendered to British forces in 1760, and later development blended French roots, British commerce, industry, immigration, and metropolitan planning.
