# New Orleans

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> New Orleans is Louisiana's largest city, a Mississippi Delta port and cultural center shaped by tourism, medicine, industry, Carnival, and Mardi Gras. Its urban character also shows the vulnerability of low-lying coastal cities, most dramatically in the levee failures and displacement caused by Hurricane Katrina.

## Introduction

![[attachments/new_orleans_001.webp|420]]

*New Orleans French Quarter*

New Orleans is a city in southeastern Louisiana and one of the most distinctive cities in the New World. It was founded in an imperial conflict zone because it controlled access to the Mississippi-Missouri river system. Its people blended many heritages, and citizens of African descent made it the birthplace of jazz.

The city is known for paradox and contrast. It shares the urban problems of other U.S. cities but preserves an exuberant spirit seen during Carnival and Mardi Gras. It is Louisiana's largest city, a major port, tourist destination, medical, industrial, and educational centre. Hurricane Katrina devastated New Orleans in August 2005, when failed levees flooded nearly the whole city, killed hundreds, caused massive damage, and forced evacuation.

## Population and Area

New Orleans covered about 199 square miles as a city and about 1,907 square miles as a metropolitan area in the source figures. Population fell from 484,674 in 2000 to 343,829 in 2010, while the New Orleans-Metairie-Kenner metropolitan area also declined after Katrina.

## Landscape

New Orleans and Orleans parish are coextensive and occupy a point near the head of the Mississippi River delta. The Mississippi River, Jefferson parish, Lake Pontchartrain, The Rigolets, Lake Borgne, St. Bernard parish, and the river again define its boundaries. The principal settlement lies on the east bank, while Algiers occupies the west bank, connected by the Crescent City Connection bridges.

The city's Crescent City nickname comes from the sharp bend of the Mississippi where early settlement began. Modern New Orleans spread far beyond that bend. Because its saucer-shaped terrain is partly below sea level and receives heavy rainfall, levees and drainage are essential. Katrina confirmed long-standing fears when storm surge overwhelmed the levee system and flooded about four-fifths of the city.

## Hurricane Katrina

![[attachments/new_orleans_002.webp|420]]

*Hurricane Katrina flooding in New Orleans*

Hurricane Katrina in 2005 became the defining modern disaster in New Orleans. Failed levees and flooding exposed the vulnerability of a low-lying city dependent on engineered protection. Less than a month later, another hurricane west of the city caused additional levee failures and localized flooding.

## Climate

New Orleans has a moderate climate. Average daily temperatures are about 60 F from October through March and 77 F from April through September. Freezing weather is rare, and temperatures above 95 F occur only a few days each year.

## People

The population of New Orleans has declined, while surrounding parishes have grown, reflecting suburban movement. White residents fell from nearly two-thirds of the city in 1960 to less than one-third. Many African American residents remained in declining neighbourhoods, prompting Black and interracial political, social, and religious organizations to work on quality-of-life issues. Upper- and middle-class Black communities also shaped local politics and improvement efforts.

## Economy

The port has always been the foundation of New Orleans's economy. The modern port era began in 1879 with South Pass jetties that helped ships overcome sandbars. The Dock Board, created in 1896, administered wharves, while canals and the Mississippi River-Gulf Outlet linked the river to Lake Pontchartrain, the Intracoastal Waterway, and the gulf.

## Industry

Greater New Orleans is a major industrial area. Petrochemical plants line the Mississippi above the city, and NASA established the Michoud Assembly Facility in 1961 to produce Saturn rocket boosters for Moon flights. Manufacturing includes food, clothing, stone, clay and glass products, metals, fabricated metal goods, and transportation equipment. Tourism is also important. Petrochemical and offshore oil activity have caused serious pollution through fires, slicks, and toxic discharges.

## Transportation

[[Transportation]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)) includes airports, railroads, buses, trucks, barges, steamship lines, and major bridges. New Orleans International Airport serves the west of the city, while other air facilities support private, corporate, and naval aviation. The Huey P. Long Bridge and Lake Pontchartrain Causeway supplement the Crescent City Connection.

## Government

New Orleans politics and municipal government have long been dominated by Democratic Party factions. A strong home-rule charter in 1954 replaced the mayor-commission system with a mayor-council system. The mayor oversees municipal departments and boards, while a chief administrative officer supervises departments, budgets, and relations with state and federal agencies. African American political participation increased as Black residents became a city majority, and Ernest N. Morial became the first African American mayor in 1978.

## Health

New Orleans is a medical centre. The Medical Center of Louisiana at New Orleans serves as teaching hospital for Tulane and Louisiana State University medical schools, with an affiliated veterans hospital nearby. Specialists also treat patients from Latin America.

## Education

The city's [[Education|education]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education)) includes Tulane University, Loyola University New Orleans, the University of New Orleans, Delgado Community College, Dillard University, Xavier University of Louisiana, and Southern University at New Orleans. Public schools began in 1841. In 1960, a desegregation crisis began with token integration of two white schools, and within 20 years the system became overwhelmingly Black.

## Cultural Life

New Orleans culture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture)) blends white American, Cajun, Irish, German, Italian, and African American contributions. Free people of colour before the Civil War were musicians, writers, business owners, and landlords, while Black freemen and enslaved people were known for crafts and music. Jazz grew from Black blues and field hollers combined with white dance tunes and hymns.

The city is famous for recreation and celebration. Its Old World Spanish-French architecture in the Vieux Carre, Mardi Gras, music, dancing, and relaxed public spirit have long drawn visitors. Yet tourism suffered after World War II, the decline of regular sea travel, and concerns about poverty and street crime.

## History

The decision to found New Orleans, or Nouvelle-Orleans, was made in Paris in 1717 by John Law's Company of the West. The settlement was named for Philippe II, duc d'Orleans, the French regent. Jean-Baptiste le Moyne de Bienville chose the site and founded the city in 1718, while Adrien de Pauger drafted the first plan of what became the Vieux Carre.

Early colonial New Orleans grew precariously amid hurricanes, mosquitoes, shortages, convict labour, enslaved labour, and swamp conditions. A 1721 census recorded 470 people. New Orleans became capital of Louisiana in 1722, returned to French crown control in 1731, and was secretly ceded to Spain in 1762 before the Treaty of Paris.

- 1718: Bienville began founding the city.

- 2005: Hurricane Katrina flooded and devastated the city.
