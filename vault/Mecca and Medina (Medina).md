# Mecca and Medina (Medina)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Medina is one of Islam's holiest cities and the place where Muhammad established the first Muslim community after the Hijra in 622 CE. Its history as a religious center and the location of Muhammad's tomb connects pilgrimage, early Islamic state formation, and Arabian history.

## Introduction

![[attachments/medina_001.webp|420]]

*Al-Masjid an-Nabawi in Medina*

Medina, ancient Yathrib, is in the Hejaz of western Saudi Arabia, inland from the Red Sea. With Mecca, it is one of Islam's two holiest cities. [[Muhammad and the Religion of Islam (Muhammad)|Muhammad]] reached Medina after the 622 CE flight from Mecca, later conquered Arabia from there, and is visited at his tomb. Only Muslims may enter.

## Landscape

Medina stands on a fertile oasis 2,050 feet above sea level, bordered by lava fields, Hejaz hills, and Mount Uhud. Sacred sites include Quba, the Mosque of the Two Qiblahs, Uhud memorial places, Al-Baqiyyah, and above all the Prophet's Mosque ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prophet%27s_Mosque)). Caliphs and sultans repaired it after fires in 1256 and 1481, while Saudi rulers expanded it in the 20th century.

## People

The residents of Medina are Arabic-speaking Muslims, mostly Sunni. Many pilgrims settle there, making the city one of Saudi Arabia's most populous, and [[Farming and Agricultural Technology (Agriculture, origins of)|farming]] and pottery remain important occupations.

## Agriculture

Agriculture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture)) supplements pilgrimage income through fruits, vegetables, cereals, and especially date palms. Dates are processed for export at a plant built in 1953. Irrigation uses mechanical pumps, subsoil water, an aqueduct from the oasis spring, and winter torrents from wadis such as Al-Aqiq.

## Industry

Early Medina was known for metalworking, jewelry, and armory, but those activities stayed small. By the mid-20th century, industry centered on automobile repair, brick and tile making, carpentry, metalworking, and agricultural technology.

## Transportation

The Hejaz railway connected Medina with Damascus from 1908 to 1916 before destruction in World War I. Roads now link the city with Jiddah, Mecca, Yanbu, Jordan, and the Hejaz, while Al-Jiladain airport connects it with Saudi centers and nearby countries.

## History

Medina's early history included Jewish settlers, likely strengthened after Hadrian expelled Jews from Palestine about 135 CE, and Arab Aws and Khazraj tribes. [[Muhammad and the Religion of Islam (Muhammad)|Muhammad]]'s arrival on September 20, 622 began the Muslim calendar and made Medina the capital of the expanding Islamic state until 661, when Damascus replaced it. Later control passed among native emirs, Meccan sharifs, Egyptian protectors, Ottomans, Wahhabis, and Saudis.

## Sir John Bagot Glubb

Husayn ibn Ali revolted during World War I and disabled the Hejaz railway with help from T.E. Lawrence. After conflict with Ibn Saud, Medina fell to the Saud dynasty in 1925.

- 661: Islamic administration shifted from Medina to Damascus.

- Medina's airport lies outside the sacred limits, allowing non-Muslims to view the city from aircraft.
