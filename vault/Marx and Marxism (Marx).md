# Marx and Marxism (Marx)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Karl Marx was a revolutionary theorist, sociologist, historian, and economist whose work with Friedrich Engels founded Marxism. His writings connect historical analysis, critique of political economy, class struggle, and political action in a sustained attempt to explain and transform capitalist society.

## Introduction

![[attachments/marx_001_mw.webp|420]]

*Portrait photograph of Karl Marx*

Karl Heinrich Marx was a revolutionary, sociologist, historian, and economist whose writings with Friedrich Engels became the basis of [[Marx and Marxism (Marxism)|Marxism]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism)). His work joined political action, historical theory, economic analysis, and a lifelong belief that social struggle could produce a different society.

## Early Life and Education

Marx was born into a Jewish family in Trier; his father Heinrich Marx, a lawyer shaped by the Enlightenment and by Kant and [[Voltaire]], converted to the Evangelical Established Church before Karl's birth. Marx studied at Trier, Bonn, Berlin, and Jena, moving from student disorder and poetry circles toward law, philosophy, and the radical culture of the Young Hegelians ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Hegelians)).

At Berlin, Marx first resisted and then absorbed [[Hegel and Hegelianism (Hegel)|Hegel]]'s philosophy through the Doctor Club and figures such as Bruno Bauer. Bauer's criticism of Christianity, the dismissal of Young Hegelians by the Prussian state, and Adolph Rutenberg's pressure toward political engagement pushed Marx toward atheism, republicanism, and social criticism.

Marx received his doctorate from Jena in 1841 with a dissertation on Democritus and Epicurus. Ludwig Feuerbach's The Essence of Christianity then helped him combine Hegelian dialectic with materialism: history and ideas had to be understood through changing material conditions, not through spirit alone.

As editor of the Rheinische Zeitung in 1842, Marx wrote on censorship, poverty, wood theft, housing, and communism, and the Prussian authorities soon closed the paper. He married Jenny von Westphalen in 1843, moved to Paris, encountered French and German communist workers, and became a revolutionary communist.

In Paris, Marx wrote the Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844, befriended Friedrich Engels, and published criticism of Hegelian political philosophy, including the claim that religion is the opium of the people. Expelled from France, he moved to Brussels in 1845 and renounced Prussian nationality.

In Brussels, Marx and Engels deepened their partnership. The Holy Family attacked Bruno Bauer's idealism, and The German Ideology set out their materialist conception of history, arguing that societies are structured to serve the economically dominant class. Marx also rejected Wilhelm Weitling's moral appeals and attacked Pierre-Joseph Proudhon's search for balance between opposed economic forces.

## The Communist Manifesto

Marx and Engels joined the Communist League in 1847 after the League of the Just sought a political program. From December 1847 to January 1848 they drafted The Communist Manifesto, which argued that all history had been a history of class struggles and that proletarian victory would end class society.

The Communist Manifesto ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Communist_Manifesto)) rejected utopian socialism, small communal experiments, and vague philosophy as substitutes for class struggle. It proposed immediate measures such as progressive income tax, abolition of inheritances, and free education, and closed with the call that workers had nothing to lose but their chains and a world to win.

During the revolutions of 1848, Marx returned to the Rhineland and used the Neue Rheinische Zeitung to support constitutional democracy, coalition with the democratic bourgeoisie, resistance to Prussian counterrevolution, and war with Russia. After trials and acquittal, he was banished in May 1849, and the final issue appeared in red.

In London from 1849, Marx first urged permanent revolution through the Communist League, then broke with militants such as August von Willich who wanted immediate action without material conditions. Years of poverty followed: eviction, child deaths, debt, illness, and dependence on Engels, family bequests, and articles for the New York Tribune.

The London years also produced major intellectual work. Marx wrote The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte in 1852, published A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy in 1859, and worked at the British Museum on drafts later connected with Das Kapital ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kapital)), including writings on surplus value.

Marx's early Promethean tone treated philosophy as a challenge to all gods and authority. This defiance later became political and historical: he saw society as driven by conflict, contradiction, and struggle, not by harmony, moral preaching, or abstract ideals.

## The First International

Marx's political isolation ended with the International Working Men's Association, founded in London in 1864. He wrote its Address and Provisional Rules, served on the General Council, managed factional differences with unusual tact, and helped the organization gain prestige among European workers and unions.

The Paris Commune of 1871 made Marx an international revolutionary figure. After the Commune was crushed, he praised its martyrs in Civil War in France, while Engels later saw it as the first example of the dictatorship of the proletariat.

The Commune also deepened conflicts inside the First International ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_International)). English trade unionists disliked Marx's support for the Commune, while Mikhail Bakunin opposed centralized organization, parliamentary politics, and the idea of a proletarian state. At The Hague in 1872, Marx defeated the Bakuninists and moved the General Council to New York; the International faded and was dissolved in 1876.

In his last decade, Marx suffered illness, depression, and reduced creative energy. He criticized the Gotha Program of the German Social Democratic Party in 1875, hoped a European war would weaken Russian tsarism, approved the courage of Russian terrorists who killed Alexander II in 1881, and still influenced socialist leaders such as Jules Guesde and Henry Mayers Hyndman.

Marx died in London after the deaths of Jenny von Westphalen in 1881 and his daughter Jenny Longuet in 1883. At Highgate Cemetery, Engels said Marx had discovered the law of development of human history and the law of motion of bourgeois society, but above all had been a revolutionist.

Marx's character mixed intellectual force, arrogance, family affection, obsessive ideas, dependence on Engels, and absolute commitment to struggle. His influence came through the dialectical model of society, the claim that economic structure shapes history, and the view that contradictions inside capitalism would produce a new social system.

## Major Works

Marx's major works include The Poverty of Philosophy, The Communist Manifesto, The Class Struggles in France, The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte, A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy, and Das Kapital ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kapital)). Das Kapital volume 1 appeared in 1867; volumes 2 and 3 were edited by Engels and published in 1885 and 1894.

## The Eighteenth Brumaire and Political Economy

The Eighteenth Brumaire analyzed how a bureaucratic absolutist state could form with support from the peasant class. A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy restated the materialist view that historical development depends on economic development.

## The Collected Works

The Collected Works of Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels was planned as a 50-volume English-language edition including correspondence. By 1992, 41 volumes had been published.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1841: Marx received his doctorate and moved toward materialist criticism after reading Feuerbach.
- 1848: Marx and Engels issued the Communist Manifesto ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Communist_Manifesto)) as revolutions spread through Europe.
- 1864: Marx became the leading spirit of the International Working Men's Association.
- 1871: Marx supported the Paris Commune and defended it in Civil War in France.
- 1883: Marx died in London and was buried at Highgate Cemetery.

## Key People

- Friedrich Engels ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Engels)): Marx's lifelong collaborator, financial supporter, and editor of later volumes of Das Kapital ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kapital)).
- Jenny von Westphalen ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenny_von_Westphalen)): Marx's wife, whose family background, hardship, and death shaped his private life.
- Bruno Bauer ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Bauer)): Young Hegelian theologian whose criticism helped shape Marx's early radicalism.
- Ludwig Feuerbach ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Feuerbach)): Materialist critic of Hegel who influenced Marx's turn toward material conditions.
- Mikhail Bakunin ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bakunin)): Anarchist rival whose conflict with Marx helped break the First International ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_International)).

## Interesting Facts

- Marx wrote nearly 500 articles and editorials for the New York Tribune between 1851 and 1862, with Engels supplying about a fourth.
- The Communist Manifesto ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Communist_Manifesto)) was almost delayed by disciplinary threats from impatient London communists.
- Engels disclosed near death that Marx had fathered Frederick Demuth, the son of the family servant Helene Demuth.
- Das Kapital ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Kapital)) was officially described by the International as the Bible of the working class.
