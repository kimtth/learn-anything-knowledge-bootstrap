# Intelligence and Counterintelligence (Intelligence)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Military intelligence is information about enemies and operating areas and the institutions that collect and analyze it for strategic policy and tactical command. Its sources range from human agents and cameras to aircraft, satellites, sonar, and electronic systems, while failures reveal its importance in war and diplomacy.

## Introduction

![[attachments/intelligence_counterintelligence_001.webp|420]]

*Aerial view of CIA headquarters*

Intelligence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence)), in military science, is information about an enemy or an area, and the term also denotes an agency that gathers such information. It is as old as warfare: [[Moses]] sent spies among the Canaanites, [[George Washington]] used a New York intelligence net, and poor intelligence handling helped make Pearl Harbor a disaster in 1941.

Modern intelligence uses satellites, aircraft, electronics, human sources, cameras, and processing systems. It operates at two levels: strategic intelligence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_intelligence)) for policy and high-level planning, and tactical intelligence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_intelligence)) for field commanders and immediate operations.

## Collection Sources

Acoustic intelligence ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acoustical_intelligence)) analyzes intentional or unintentional sound waves. Naval sonar arrays can detect surface ships and submarines and provide highly accurate information about underwater activity.

Imagery intelligence comes from photography, infrared, ultraviolet, satellite images, and aircraft images. Interpreters identify objects, terrain, and activities. Imagery supports order of battle, operations, technical analysis, economics, and arms-control monitoring, including verification under the 1987 Intermediate Nuclear Forces Treaty.

Signals intelligence, or SIGINT, intercepts and analyzes foreign communications and electronic signals. U.S. breaking of Japan's PURPLE code before and during [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]] revealed Japanese moves. ELINT studies noncommunication electromagnetic emissions, and telemetry intelligence analyzes foreign missile-test data.

Radiation intelligence concerns unintentional energy emissions from electronic systems rather than nuclear detonations or radioactive sources. Poor shielding or faulty procedures can leak signals that reveal a system's purpose or capability.

Foreign materiel intelligence studies captured or acquired weapons and equipment. In 1976, a Soviet lieutenant defected to Japan in a MiG-25 Foxbat, allowing Japanese and U.S. technicians to examine the fighter before returning it.

[[Human Intelligence (Intelligence, Human)|Human intelligence]], or HUMINT, comes from people, spies, and covert agents. Oleg Penkovsky supplied British and U.S. intelligence on Soviet missiles before his execution in 1963. The Philby-Burgess-Maclean ring and Walker family cases show how spies can expose allied or naval secrets.

## Analytical Categories

Biographical intelligence studies leaders' views, habits, skills, health, relationships, and careers. It helps judge foreign leaders, such as Fidel Castro after 1959, and evaluate volunteers for clandestine service.

Cartographic and geographic intelligence use maps, charts, terrain, climate, resources, transportation, boundaries, and population distribution. They aided British operations in the Falklands and Israel's 1976 Entebbe rescue, while poor preparation for sandstorms hurt the U.S. Iran hostage rescue attempt in 1980.

Economic and energy intelligence assess production, finance, resources, fuel, technology, supply, demand, and military sustainability. Fuel needs affected Adolf Hitler's drive toward Baku and later halted George Patton's 3rd Army.

Medical, sociological, transportation, and telecommunications intelligence assess disease environments, social divisions, values, roads, railways, waterways, airways, telephones, and broadcasts. They help explain U.S. disease losses in the Spanish-American War, confusion over revolutionary Iran in 1979, and China's limited performance in Vietnam in 1979.

## Counterintelligence

Counterintelligence detects, counteracts, and prevents espionage, clandestine operations, sabotage, terrorism, and assassination for foreign powers or organizations. Against terrorism, attribution is crucial because prevention, disruption, and retaliation depend on identifying the responsible group.

## Key Events & Developments

- Pearl Harbor: Poorly communicated intelligence contributed to the 1941 disaster.
- PURPLE code: U.S. codebreaking exposed Japanese moves before and during [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]].
- MiG-25 defection: A Soviet pilot brought a Foxbat to Japan in 1976.
- INF verification: The 1987 treaty used satellite monitoring of missile sites.

## Key People
