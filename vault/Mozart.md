# Mozart

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was a prolific Austrian composer whose childhood touring, Salzburg employment, Vienna career, operas, concertos, chamber works, and late compositions made him a central figure of the Classical style. The account follows both his changing patronage and his enduring musical achievement.

## Introduction

![[attachments/mozart_001.webp|420]]

*Portrait of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart*

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, born in 1756 in Salzburg and dead in 1791 in Vienna, was an Austrian composer regarded as one of the greatest figures in Western music. With Haydn and [[Beethoven]], he brought the Viennese Classical style to its height. He wrote successfully in every major genre of his time, including opera, symphony, concerto, chamber music, sacred music, keyboard music, serenade, and dance music.

## Chrysostomus Wolfgangus Theophilus Mozart

Mozart was baptized Johannes Chrysostomus Wolfgangus Theophilus Mozart and often called himself Wolfgang Amade or Wolfgang Gottlieb. His father Leopold Mozart, author of a famous violin manual published in 1756, recognized the child's extraordinary talent and treated it as a divine miracle to be shown to Europe. Mozart played chords at three, short pieces at four, and composed by five. His sister Maria Anna, known as Nannerl, was also gifted and joined his early performances.

## Childhood Tours and Early Works

From 1763 to 1766, the Mozart family toured Augsburg, Stuttgart, Mannheim, Mainz, Frankfurt, Brussels, Paris, London, The Hague, Amsterdam, Lyon, and Switzerland. Mozart and Nannerl performed at courts, churches, and public venues. In Paris, Mozart's first music was published; in London, contact with Johann Christian Bach helped him compose his first symphonies. These journeys exposed him to many musical dialects and made him fluent in the styles of Europe.

In Vienna from 1767 to 1768, Mozart wrote the singspiel Bastien und Bastienne and the opera buffa La finta semplice, though court performance hopes were frustrated. In 1769, he became honorary Konzertmeister at the Salzburg court. Still only 13, he had learned to imitate local styles while developing stronger textures, orchestral invention, and an early dramatic sense.

## Italian Journeys and Salzburg Service

Mozart's Italian journeys from 1769 to 1773 brought important operatic commissions. Mitridate succeeded in Milan, Ascanio in Alba outshone a work by Johann Adolph Hasse, and Lucio Silla showed the first clear signs of the mature dramatic composer. He also wrote the motet Exsultate, jubilate for Venanzio Rauzzini.

Back in Salzburg, Mozart worked as court Konzertmeister under Archbishop Hieronymus Colloredo. The post supplied local sacred and instrumental music but felt too small for his gifts. He wrote masses, litanies, divertimentos, serenades, and five violin concertos. La finta giardiniera for Munich in 1775 showed his growing ability to give comic-opera figures personality and expressive orchestral support.

## Mannheim and Paris

In 1777, Mozart left Salzburg with his mother to seek better opportunities. In Mannheim he admired the orchestra, wrote flute music, composed sonatas, and fell in love with soprano Aloysia Weber. His father rejected Mozart's plan to travel with the Webers and ordered him to Paris. There Mozart wrote the brilliant Paris Symphony, K 297, for the Concert Spirituel, but his mother died on July 3, 1778.

Mozart returned reluctantly to Salzburg after Leopold Mozart secured him a better post as court organist. He displayed command of international styles in symphonies, sacred works, the Coronation Mass, the Sinfonia Concertante for violin and viola, and other pieces. Dramatic music attracted him most, and the Munich commission for Idomeneo gave him a major opportunity.

## Idomeneo and Break with Salzburg

Idomeneo, premiered in Munich on January 29, 1781, portrayed serious heroic emotion with unusual richness. Mozart revised the libretto for dramatic logic, adjusted music to singers, and combined Italian opera seria with French-influenced continuity, chorus, and orchestral colour. Soon after, Archbishop Colloredo summoned him to Vienna, where Mozart resented being treated as a servant. The conflict led to his break with Salzburg service and his decision to live as an independent musician in Vienna.

## Vienna: The Early Years

In [[Vienna]], Mozart pursued freelance work, pupils, performance, publishing, and opera. He lodged with the Weber family and eventually fell in love with Constanze Weber, marrying her in 1782 despite Leopold's disapproval. His singspiel Die Entfuhrung aus dem Serail premiered on July 16, 1782, and became an immediate success, using Turkish colour, elaborate arias, strong dramatic modification, and comic music suited to Viennese taste.

## The Central Viennese Period

The mid-1780s were Mozart's most fruitful and successful years. Vienna was the land of the piano, and he triumphed as pianist-composer in concerts, improvisations, fantasies, variations, and especially piano concertos. From 1784 to 1786, he transformed the concerto into a symphonic, melodically rich, dramatically dialogic form, giving wind instruments new importance and blending virtuosity into musical structure.

Mozart also wrote major chamber and keyboard works, including the piano and wind quintet K 452, the C Minor fantasy K 475, and the six quartets dedicated to Haydn. Haydn told Leopold in 1785 that Mozart was the greatest composer known to him, with taste and supreme knowledge of composition. The D Minor piano concerto K 466, admired by Beethoven, showed a darker, more resistant expressive world.

## From Figaro to Don Giovanni

Mozart's financial worries grew after 1786, even though his income was higher than that of many musicians. Court opera success became crucial. With librettist Lorenzo Da Ponte, he created Le nozze di Figaro, based on Beaumarchais's politically charged comedy with much of its explicit sting removed. Figaro's ensembles closely unite music and dramatic meaning, and the opera makes social hierarchy audible through character and musical interaction.

Figaro premiered on May 1, 1786, and achieved particular success in Prague. Mozart visited Prague in 1787, presented the Prague Symphony, and accepted another opera commission. After Leopold's death in May 1787, Mozart wrote the string quintets K 515 and K 516 and then Don Giovanni ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni)), another Da Ponte opera. Its mixture of comedy, social observation, seduction, punishment, and demonic seriousness gave it lasting fascination.

## The Last Travels

After Prague, Mozart finally received a court post as Kammermusicus, replacing Gluck, but the salary did not remove his financial strain. In 1788, he wrote the last three symphonies, K 543, K 550, and K 551, known as the Jupiter, during a period of depression and loan requests to fellow Freemason Michael Puchberg. Travels to Berlin and elsewhere brought hopes for Prussian commissions but little secure income.

The late style appears in Cosi fan tutte, premiered in 1790. Long criticized for its plot about female fickleness, it is better understood as a restrained and penetrating study of human feelings, irony, and emotional maturity. Mozart also arranged Handel, wrote Prussian quartets, composed for mechanical organ, and tried to attract court notice during the coronation of Leopold II.

## Last Works and Death

In 1791, Mozart worked on Die Zauberflote ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_Flute)), the anonymous Requiem commission, and La clemenza di Tito for Prague coronation festivities. La clemenza di Tito was initially dismissed as hasty but later admired for restrained neoclassical style. Die Zauberflote ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_Flute)), premiered on September 30, soon became his most beloved stage work, blending fairy tale, Masonic symbolism, popular theatre, and profound musical characterization.

Mozart became ill in late 1791 and died on December 5. The certified cause was severe miliary fever, with later diagnoses proposed; the poisoning story involving Antonio Salieri has no evidence. Constanze Mozart arranged for the Requiem to be completed so the fee could be collected. Franz Xaver Sussmayr produced the standard completion, using Mozart's materials but adding movements of his own.

## Mozart's Place

At his death, Mozart was widely regarded as the greatest composer of his time and a bold, difficult one. His manuscripts were sold by Constanze, and editions, biographies, the Kochel catalogue, and complete editions gradually secured his reputation. In the 19th century, the repertory favoured Figaro, Don Giovanni ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni)), Die Zauberflote ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_Flute)), minor-key piano concertos, and the last symphonies. In the 20th century, broader reexamination restored the full range of his achievement.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1756: Mozart was born in Salzburg.
- 1763-1766: The Mozart family made a major European tour.
- 1781: Idomeneo premiered and Mozart broke with Salzburg service.
- 1782: Mozart married Constanze Weber and Die Entfuhrung succeeded.
- 1786-1787: Figaro and Don Giovanni ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni)) established his great Da Ponte operas.
- 1791: Die Zauberflote ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Magic_Flute)) premiered, and Mozart died while working on the Requiem.

## Key People

- Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_Amadeus_Mozart)): Composer central to the Viennese Classical school.
- Leopold Mozart ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Mozart)): Father, teacher, manager, and author of a violin manual.
- Maria Anna Mozart ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Anna_Mozart)): Mozart's talented sister and childhood touring partner.
- Constanze Weber Mozart: Mozart's wife and later manager of his legacy.
- Lorenzo Da Ponte ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Da_Ponte)): Librettist of Figaro, Don Giovanni, and Cosi fan tutte.
- Joseph Haydn ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Haydn)): Friend and admirer who praised Mozart's taste and compositional knowledge.

## Interesting Facts

- K numbers refer to the catalogue of Mozart's works by Ludwig von Kochel.
- Die Entfuhrung was said to contain very many notes, a sign of its unusual richness.
- Don Giovanni ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Giovanni)) fascinated later audiences because comic opera collides with demonic seriousness.
- The Requiem became famous partly because it was left unfinished at Mozart's death.
