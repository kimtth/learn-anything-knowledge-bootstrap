# Marseille

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Marseille is France's principal Mediterranean port and the commercial center of Provence-Alpes-Cote d'Azur. Founded as ancient Massalia, its old port, trade, industry, transport links, and multicultural population connect a long maritime history with modern urban life.

## Introduction

![[attachments/marseille_001.webp|420]]

*Marseille Old Port at night*

Marseille, also called Marseilles and ancient Massilia or Massalia, is the capital of the Bouches-du-Rhone departement and the commercial capital of Provence-Alpes-Cote d'Azur. It stands on the Gulf of Lion west of the French Riviera, 536 miles from [[Paris]] by rail, and had 807,071 city residents in 1999 and an estimated 826,700 in 2005.

## The Old Port of

The port city of Marseille was founded more than 2,500 years ago and long defended independence. It stayed free after Julius Caesar's troops took it in the 1st century BC, revived under the viscounts of Provence, and kept a separate administration after Provence joined France.

French opinion often treated Marseille as colourful but marginal, linked with petanque, bouillabaisse, pastis, dialect, and criminal legend. Yet with Aix-en-Provence it forms France's second largest urban agglomeration, and with Fos-sur-Mer it forms the country's largest seaport.

The natural harbour, sheltered by limestone hills near the Rhone estuary, linked Mediterranean sea routes with northern Europe. Greeks from Phocaea founded the port about 600 BC, and the city became known for mixed population, religious diversity, commerce, seafaring services, and openness to change.

After [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]], Marseille became a major European port and industrial centre. It absorbed former European colonists from [[North Africa]] after Algerian independence in 1962, while Fos-sur-Mer drew migrant workers. Later crises brought unemployment, racial tension, and rehabilitation programs in northern districts.

The Old Port ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Port_of_Marseille)) was enlarged in Roman times and from the 16th century onward, but it was too small by the 19th century. La Joliette opened in the mid-1840s, Port-Saint-Louis-du-Rhone followed in 1863, and Fos-sur-Mer opened in 1968.

The city site is constrained by the Etoile Chain, pushing growth toward the Berre Lagoon, Marignane, Vitrolles, and the Huveaune valley. The Panier district was destroyed in 1943, while middle-class districts grew southward.

Postwar planning included Le Corbusier's Unite d'Habitation, completed in 1952, and later high-rise immigrant housing. Since the 1970s, authorities have restored parts of the Panier, pedestrianized the Old Port area, and launched Euromediterranee renewal.

La Canebiere climbs east from the Old Port and remains the best-known commercial street. Historic sites include the Bourse, Hellenistic ramparts, Roman roads, forts, Chateau d'If, la Major, Vieille Charite, Hotel Dieu, Saint-Victor, and Notre-Dame-de-la-Garde.

The people of Marseille have always been diverse. In 1880 more than one in six inhabitants was foreign, and Jews, Greeks, Armenians, Corsicans, former colonials, North Africans, and Africans shaped social life and sharp north-south social contrasts.

## Climate

Marseille has a distinct [[Climate and Weather (Climate)|climate]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate)): hot, dry summers eased by sea breezes, rain peaks in spring and autumn, and mild winters with some frost in December and January.

The city is especially affected in winter by the Mistral, a dry, cold northwest wind that blows forcefully down the Rhone Valley.

## Industry

Marseille was historically more important for trade and commerce than for industry. Soap making began in the 15th century, and food processing, ship work, metallurgy, clothing, chemicals, and precision engineering later developed, though many declined or moved outward.

Heavy industry expanded around the Berre Lagoon in the 1950s after Lavera received large oil tankers. From the late 1960s, Fos added petrochemicals and steelworks tied to sea, rail, highway, pipeline, and Rhone routes.

Newer industrial services grew in electronics, data processing, telecommunications, biomedicine, offshore exploration, and engineering, while outer districts attracted warehousing, transport, lighter industry, and airport-linked activity.

The Marseille-Fos port complex is France's largest and one of Europe's largest. Annual traffic exceeded 90 million tons, led by crude oil, refined products, gas, chemicals, metals materials, exports, and rising containers.

Marseille also had commercial finance importance. Banks founded in 1852 and 1865 backed regional industry and foreign projects such as the Suez Canal Company, while a new international trade centre opened in 1983.

## Transportation

Marseille has strong external [[Transportation|transportation]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)) links, including highways, TGV service to Lyon in one hour and Paris in three, and Marseille-Provence Airport, France's third-ranking passenger airport after Paris and Nice.

Local movement is harder because road congestion remains severe, though a central tunnel, two metro lines, and an eastern tramway improved circulation.

## Government

The government of Marseille is led by an elected municipal council that preserves the city's independence tradition, even as national ministries help finance major projects.

The city has 16 arrondissements grouped into eight secteurs. The mayor has 27 adjoints responsible for planning, culture, finance, employment, and transport.

## Services

Municipal services include lighting, refuse disposal, police and fire relations, and regional firefighting coordination. High unemployment and a large immigrant-worker population make service provision harder.

## Health

Marseille has teaching hospitals at North Hospital and Timone Hospital, a tropical-medicine centre at Michel Levy Hospital, and a Luminy computer centre linking regional hospitals.

## Education

Three universities have city sites: Aix-Marseille I in sciences, Aix-Marseille II in medicine, and Aix-Marseille III in science and engineering, alongside technical universities and graduate schools.

Marseille's cultural life ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture)) includes museums, monuments, opera, theatre, music, dance, sports centres, beaches, and parks. Exchanges with artists from Algeria and North Africa reflect diversity, and Marcel Pagnol made the city a film centre in the 1930s.

## History

Massalia was founded by Greek mariners from Phocaea about 600 BC, though Phoenicians may have arrived earlier. Massalians founded trading posts toward Spain, Monaco, Arles, Nice, Antibes, Agde, and La Ciotat, while Pytheas explored northern coasts in the 4th century BC.

Marseille supported [[Rome]] against Carthage, then chose Pompey against Julius Caesar and fell in 49 BC. It kept free-city status but declined after invasion and epidemic until the 10th century, when viscounts of Provence restored shipping prosperity.

The city bought rights from the viscounts of Provence and formed a republic around the Old Port in the early 13th century. It later acknowledged Charles of Anjou, was sacked by Alfonso V of Aragon, and recovered under Rene I of Provence.

When Provence joined France in 1481, Marseille kept separate administration and resisted central authority. It refused Henry IV until his Catholic conversion and was subdued by Louis XIV in 1660.

Marseille joined the French Revolution enthusiastically, and volunteers marching to Paris in 1792 made La Marseillaise famous. The city later revolted against the National Convention, opposed Napoleon's blockade, welcomed the Bourbon restoration, and remained republican under Napoleon III.

The 19th century brought imperial expansion through the end of Barbary piracy, the conquest of Algeria in 1830, and the Suez Canal in 1869. Major avenues and monuments appeared, and the Durance water project solved supply problems.

During [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]], German occupation from November 1942 to August 1944 damaged the city, while Marseille remained active in the French Resistance. The Panier and Old Port were dynamited in 1943. Postwar Marseille rebuilt, expanded Fos, shifted outward, and remained important despite rivalry from Lyon.

## Interesting Facts

- Marseille has France's oldest chamber of commerce, established in 1599.
- Bouillabaisse, petanque, and Provencal pastis helped shape the city's colourful national image.
- Chateau d'If held real political prisoners and became linked with the fictional count of Monte Cristo.
- Notre-Dame-de-la-Garde rises over the Old Port with a gilded Virgin statue.
