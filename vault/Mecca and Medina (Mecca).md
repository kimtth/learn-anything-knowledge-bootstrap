# Mecca and Medina (Mecca)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Mecca is Islam's holiest city, the birthplace of Muhammad, the direction of Muslim prayer, and the destination of the hajj pilgrimage. Its sacred sanctuary and modern expansion make it a central religious, historical, and urban site in western Saudi Arabia.

## Introduction

![[attachments/mecca_001.webp|420]]

*The Kaaba in Mecca*

Mecca, Arabic Makkah and ancient Bakkah or Macoraba, is a city in western Saudi Arabia. It is the holiest Muslim city because [[Muhammad and the Religion of Islam (Muhammad)|Muhammad]] was born there, Muslims pray toward it, and devout Muslims seek the hajj ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajj)) at least once. Only Muslims may enter, and 20th-century works enlarged the shrine area, housing, sanitation, and transport.

## Climate

Mecca lies about 909 feet above sea level in dry Wadi Ibrahim, near mountains such as Hira and Thawr. The [[Climate and Weather (Climate)|climate]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate)) is hot and dry, with less than 5 inches of yearly rain, yet flash floods remain a danger. The old city centers on the Haram Mosque, the Kabah, and Zamzam.

## Housing

Housing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing)) is dense in the old city, where traditional two- or three-story buildings used local rock, while newer districts use concrete villas. Poor pilgrims sometimes remained after the hajj or umrah, creating slum conditions. During pilgrimage, one to two million worshippers arrive from Saudi Arabia and other Muslim countries.

## Industry

Mecca has little arable land or water, so food comes from nearby wadis, Al-Taif, southern agricultural districts, and imports through Jiddah. Industry is limited to goods such as textiles, furniture, and utensils, while the urban economy depends mainly on commerce, services, and pilgrimage needs.

## Transportation

[[Transportation]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)) is central to the hajj. Mecca has no airport, rail, or water transport, but it uses Jiddah, roads, buses, trucks, taxis, and a local bus system begun in 1979. Pilgrims move from Mecca to Arafat, then Mina and back, so traffic control is essential.

## Government

Mecca is governed by the emir of Makkah administrative district, appointed by the king. A 14-member municipal council, formed after World War II, handles local functions. Water comes from wadis, including Ayn Zubaydah from the 8th century and Al-Aziziyah Spring, while electricity comes from an oil-fueled station.

## Education

[[Education]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education)) is free for boys and girls from primary school through university. Umm al-Qura University, founded in 1979, is in Mecca, along with Madrasat Ahl al-Hadith and the Saudi Arabian Institute for Higher Education.

## Health

Health services are free and generally adequate. Pilgrims can bring illnesses such as cholera and cerebro-spinal infections, but official checks and city medical services have usually kept these dangers under control.

## History

Ancient Mecca was an oasis on the caravan route joining the Mediterranean world with South Arabia, East Africa, and South Asia. Islamic tradition says Abraham and Ishmael built the Kabah. Under the Quraysh, Mecca became a trading, pilgrimage, and tribal center. [[Muhammad and the Religion of Islam (Muhammad)|Muhammad]], born about 570, fled in 622, returned eight years later, removed idols, and made the city Islam's major religious center.

## Assad Sulaiman Abdo

After early Islamic rule, Mecca passed through Umayyad, Abbasid, Qarmatian, sharifian, Mamluk, and Ottoman authority. The Qarmatians carried off the Black Stone in 930, while sharifs dominated local affairs for about 1,000 years. Ibn Saud entered in 1925, and Saudi rule improved pilgrimage facilities but faced disasters in 1990 and 1997, a Great Mosque seizure in 1979, and later protest clashes.
