## Introduction

![[attachments/oceans_001.webp|420]]

*Bathymetric map of Arctic Ocean basins and ridges*

Oceans ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean)) form the continuous body of salt water held in enormous basins on Earth's surface. Seen from space, they dominate the planet, covering nearly 71 percent of the surface with an average depth of 3,795 metres. Exposed land covers the remaining 29 percent and has a mean elevation of about 840 metres.

If Earth's land were smoothed into the ocean basins, the planet would be covered by a continuous seawater layer 2,686 metres deep, called the sphere depth of the oceans. This measure emphasizes the abundance of water at Earth's surface and the unusual planetary conditions that allow liquid water to dominate.

## Earth's Water Reservoirs

Earth is unique in the solar system because its distance from the Sun and rotation produce a mean surface temperature near 17 C, allowing water to exist as solid, liquid, and gas. About 97.957 percent of Earth's water is oceanic water and sea ice. Atmospheric water is only 0.001 percent, lakes and streams 0.036 percent, groundwater 0.365 percent, and glaciers and ice caps 1.641 percent.

## Hydrologic Cycle

Water moves continuously among reservoirs through the hydrologic cycle ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_cycle)), powered by solar energy. Evaporation, precipitation, atmospheric motion, river flow, glacier movement, and groundwater flow keep water in circulation. The oceans are the dominant reservoir in this system and help shape climate, weather, and the distribution of water across Earth.
