## Introduction

Henry Ford ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ford)) was an American industrialist who transformed the United States through mass production and the automobile industry. He was a figure of great influence, both admired for his technical innovation and criticized for his political opinions and personal eccentricities. His most famous achievement was the development of the assembly line ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line)), which made the Model T affordable to millions of Americans.

Ford was not only a businessman but also a public symbol of the shift from an agricultural to an industrial society. His life reflects the dramatic social transformation of the United States in the early 20th century.

Ford was born in 1863 in Michigan and grew up on a farm near Dearborn. He attended a one-room school and learned practical mechanics through work on the family farm and later in machine shops in Detroit. His early interest in engines and machinery led him to build a small tractor and other mechanical devices before becoming involved in automotive production.

In 1893, he completed his first gasoline-powered automobile. This work marked the start of his career in the automobile industry and eventually led to the founding of the Ford Motor Company.

## The Model T and Mass Production

Ford’s major contribution was to make the automobile available to ordinary Americans. By combining engineering skill with efficient factory organization, he pioneered methods of production that greatly reduced cost and increased output. The Model T ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Model_T)), introduced in 1909, became the most important symbol of this achievement.

The assembly-line system was central to this success. Ford reorganized work so that production moved in a continuous flow, reducing time and labor costs. This changed not only industry but also the daily life of American society.

## Public Life and Influence
Ford was an outspoken and controversial figure. He was a celebrated folk hero, but he also attracted criticism for his anti-Semitic views, his political statements, and his refusal to accept the modern industrial world he had helped create. He wanted to preserve some of the rural values of his youth even as he drove the nation toward urban industrialization.

Ford’s importance therefore lies both in his technical innovation and in what he represented culturally and politically. He became a symbol of modern capitalism, technological progress, and the social consequences of industrial change.
