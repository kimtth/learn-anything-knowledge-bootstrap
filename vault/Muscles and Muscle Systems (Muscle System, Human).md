# Muscles and Muscle Systems (Muscle System, Human)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> The human muscle system focuses on voluntary skeletal muscles that move the skeleton and sustain posture and balance. It explains their inherited vertebrate plan and the changes in locomotion associated with primate ancestry, arboreal movement, and human upright posture.

## Introduction

The human muscle system ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscular_system)) consists here of the skeletal muscles that move the skeleton, remain under voluntary control, and support movement, posture, and balance. Human muscle broadly includes skeletal or striated muscle, smooth muscle, and cardiac muscle, but smooth muscle and cardiac muscle are mostly arranged like those of other vertebrates. This account focuses on skeletal muscle changes linked to the evolutionary adoption of upright posture.

Human striated muscle ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striated_muscle_tissue)) still follows the basic vertebrate and mammalian plan inherited from pronograde, or horizontal four-legged, ancestors. Primates began as quadrupedal mammals but modified their locomotor systems after their appearance in the Late Cretaceous, about 65 million years ago. Some primates, especially gibbons, developed extreme forelimb adaptations for arm-powered movement through trees.

## Human Evolution and Locomotion

![[attachments/muscles_and_muscle_systems_muscle_system_human_001.png|420]]

*Chart of major skeletal muscles of the human body*

Modern humans are closest to the chimpanzee, gorilla, and orangutan. The orangutan's locomotor system is adapted for moving among vertical rainforest trunks, using both forelimbs and hind limbs almost like four hands. The common ancestor of humans, chimpanzees, and gorillas is inferred to have been a generalized tree-dwelling animal able to walk on branches and climb between them.

Two major locomotor trends developed from that ancestral condition. One led toward gorillas and chimpanzees, with elongated forelimbs and knuckle-supported movement on the ground. The other led toward humans, with a shorter trunk, relocated shoulder blades, stronger emphasis on hind-limb support, and increasing trunk erectness. By about 3 million to 3.5 million years ago, upright bipedal posture was already well established, as shown by limb bones and preserved early hominin footprints.

The major muscular changes of bipedal locomotion are in the lower limb. Skeletal shifts include longer hind limbs, a developed heel, and a knee joint with a flatter supporting surface. Compared with apes, humans have lower limbs adapted for weight-bearing, balance, forward propulsion, and efficient upright walking. These changes reoriented the muscular system from a quadrupedal climbing-and-walking pattern toward sustained standing and two-legged movement.

## Key Events & Developments

- Late Cretaceous: Primates appeared and inherited a quadrupedal vertebrate body plan.
- About 65 million years ago: Primate locomotor systems began diversifying after their early appearance.
- 3 million-3.5 million years ago: Upright bipedal posture was well established in early hominins.

## Key People

- Homo sapiens ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_sapiens)): The human species whose skeletal muscles are adapted for voluntary movement, posture, balance, and bipedal locomotion.

## Interesting Facts

- Smooth muscle controls structures such as blood vessels, bladder, intestines, and stomach without voluntary command.
- Cardiac muscle forms the heart and produces rhythmic pumping contractions.
- Orthograde posture means upright posture, while pronograde posture means horizontal four-legged posture.
- Gibbons show one of the most extreme living primate adaptations for arm-powered movement through trees.
