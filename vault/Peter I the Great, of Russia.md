# Peter I the Great, of Russia

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Peter I was the Russian tsar whose military campaigns, Baltic ambitions, travel, court conflicts, and sweeping internal reforms transformed Russia's state, armed forces, industry, and external orientation. His break with old Muscovite institutions and founding role in imperial modernization define his historical significance.

## Life and Accession

![[attachments/peter_i_the_great_001_mw.webp|420]]

*Portrait of Peter I the Great of Russia*

Peter I, or Peter the Great ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great)), was born Pyotr Alekseyevich and ruled Russia jointly with Ivan V from 1682 to 1696, then alone until 1725. In 1721, he was proclaimed emperor. He became one of Russia's greatest statesmen, organizers, military leaders, and reformers.

Peter was the son of Tsar Alexis and Natalya Kirillovna Naryshkina. After Alexis died in 1676, court struggles between the Miloslavsky and Naryshkin factions shaped Peter's youth. The streltsy revolt of 1682 killed several of his supporters, and Ivan and Peter were proclaimed joint tsars under the regency of Sophia. Peter's fear and exclusion from power left a lasting hostility toward the streltsy and old Muscovite politics.

## Grand Embassy

In 1697-1698, Peter traveled with the Grand Embassy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Embassy_of_Peter_the_Great)) to western Europe, officially to study diplomacy and strengthen anti-Turkish alliances but also to learn European economic, technical, and naval practices. Traveling incognito as Sgt. Pyotr Mikhaylov, he studied shipbuilding in the Dutch Republic and England, worked in dockyards, visited factories, arsenals, schools, museums, and Parliament, and recruited foreign experts for Russia.

## Streltsy and Turn to the Baltic

While Peter was in Vienna, a new streltsy revolt forced him back to Moscow in 1698. He suppressed it harshly, executing hundreds, exiling others, and disbanding the corps. Since Austria and the maritime powers would not support war against Turkey, Peter shifted focus from the Black Sea to the Baltic, where Sweden blocked Russian access.

## Northern War

The Northern War ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Wars)) of 1700-1721 was Peter's main military enterprise. Russia joined Saxony and Denmark-Norway against Sweden. Defeat at Narva in 1700 forced reforms, which Peter later called a blessing because necessity compelled work. He captured Narva in 1704, fought at Lesnaya in 1708, and designed the operational plan at Poltava in 1709, where Charles XII of Sweden suffered a decisive defeat.

## Turkish, Dynastic, and Persian Affairs

During the Northern War, Turkey declared war on Russia. Peter's Prut campaign in 1711 ended with his encirclement and the loss of Azov under later peace terms. His son Alexis, hostile to Peter's reforms, fled abroad, returned under pressure, was condemned for treason, and died in prison in 1718. After the Northern War, Peter pursued eastern policy and invaded the Caspian region in the Persian campaign of 1722-1723.

## Death and Succession

Peter's health declined after extreme exertions, war, and illness. In 1724, he reportedly plunged into icy water to help endangered soldiers, became seriously ill, and died early in 1725. He had issued a decree reserving the right to name a successor but failed to do so. His widow, Catherine I, whom he had crowned empress in 1724, succeeded him.

## Internal Reforms

Peter sought to overcome Russia's backwardness compared with western Europe. His reforms affected administration, industry, commerce, technology, culture, the army, the navy, and social life. He broke boyar and clerical resistance and punished opposition. Reform was coercive but aimed at national strength, economic modernization, and victory in wars for access to the seas.

## Industry and Armed Forces

The war with Sweden made industrial development urgent. Peter expanded metallurgy, manufacturing, armaments, and shipbuilding, granted privileges to industrialists, allowed factories to buy serf labour, invited foreign experts, and increased foreign trade. He replaced the unreliable streltsy and gentry militia with a regular army, conscripted peasants and townspeople for life service, modernized firearms, uniforms, artillery, drilling, regulations, and naval construction.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1682: Peter and Ivan V became joint tsars after streltsy violence.
- 1697-1698: Peter traveled through western Europe with the Grand Embassy.
- 1698: The streltsy revolt was crushed and the corps disbanded.
- 1700-1721: Russia fought Sweden in the Northern War.
- 1709: Peter defeated Sweden at Poltava.
- 1718: Tsarevich Alexis died after treason proceedings.
- 1721: Peter was proclaimed emperor.
- 1725: Peter died in St. Petersburg.
