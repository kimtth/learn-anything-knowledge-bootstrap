# Lenin

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Vladimir Ilich Lenin led the Bolshevik Revolution of 1917, founded the Soviet state, and organized the Communist International. His adaptation of Marxism to revolutionary party organization and imperial crisis became Leninism, later fused with Marxism as a global communist doctrine.

## Introduction

Vladimir Ilich Lenin, born Vladimir Ilich Ulyanov, founded the Russian Communist Party known as the Bolsheviks, led the Bolshevik Revolution of 1917, and became the first head of the Soviet state from 1917 to 1924. He also founded the Comintern, or Communist International.

After his death, Leninism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninism)) was codified by his successors and joined with [[Marx and Marxism (Marx)|Marx]]'s ideas as Marxism-Leninism. That doctrine became the official Communist worldview and shaped revolutionary movements far beyond Russia.

## Overview

Lenin was born in Simbirsk in 1870 and died near [[Moscow]] on January 21, 1924. His life moved from law student and clandestine organizer to revolutionary leader, head of government, and central symbol of Soviet Communism.

## Early Life

Bolshevik Revolution made Lenin one of the most important political figures of the 20th century. Admirers and critics alike treated him as a major revolutionary leader, statesman, and thinker after [[Marx and Marxism (Marx)|Marx]].

Lenin grew up in a cultured and educated Ulyanov family. He was gifted, disciplined, strong, and successful at school, especially in Latin and Greek, and nothing in childhood clearly marked him as a future professional revolutionary except his early turn toward atheism.

## Becoming a Revolutionary

Two adolescent shocks pushed Lenin toward revolution. His father was threatened by a reactionary government fearful of public education, and in 1887 his brother Aleksandr was hanged for plotting to assassinate Alexander III.

Lenin entered Kazan University in 1887 but was expelled after an illegal student assembly and banished to Kokushkino. During enforced idleness he met older exiled revolutionaries, read political literature, and became a Marxist in January 1889.

After passing law examinations in 1891, Lenin practiced in Samara for poor peasants and artisans, gaining hatred for class bias in the legal system. In 1893 he moved to St. Petersburg, joined Marxist circles, and helped form the Union for the Struggle for the Liberation of the Working Class.

Arrested in 1895, Lenin spent 15 months in jail and three years in Siberian exile at Shushenskoye. There he married Nadezhda Krupskaya, worked on clandestine correspondence, and continued research that led to The Development of Capitalism in Russia.

Lenin accepted Georgy Plekhanov's two-stage Marxist view but pushed beyond him on the peasant question. He argued that capitalism was already breaking up the peasant commune into rural bourgeois, middle peasant, and rural proletarian layers, making poor peasants allies of the small industrial proletariat.

## The Bolshevik-Menshevik Split

The 1903 Second Congress of the Russian Social-Democratic Workers' Party exposed disputes over membership, discipline, and the party's relation to the proletariat. In What Is To Be Done?, Lenin rejected reliance on spontaneous worker struggle and argued for a disciplined vanguard party to bring socialist consciousness from outside everyday trade-union struggles.

Lenin's model of democratic centralism required a centralized organization led by professional revolutionaries. Critics such as Plekhanov, Martov, and Leon Trotsky warned that this could become Jacobin, suppress party freedom, and lead to dictatorship.

The split produced Bolsheviks, Lenin's majority faction, and Mensheviks, Martov's minority faction. Though both remained within the same party for years, Lenin made the division final at the 1912 Prague Conference.

The 1905 Revolution sharpened the difference. Mensheviks wanted alliance with the bourgeoisie in a bourgeois revolution; Lenin argued that the proletariat and peasantry should lead a revolutionary democratic dictatorship that might pass toward socialist revolution if aided by the western European proletariat.

## World War I and Imperialism

When [[World Wars (World War I)|World War I]] began, Lenin denounced pro-war Socialists as social-chauvinists and declared the Second International dead. He called for a Third International and urged revolutionary Socialists to transform imperialist war into civil war against their own capitalist rulers.

At Zimmerwald and Kienthal in 1915 and 1916, anti-war Socialists rejected Lenin's more radical slogan and adopted peace without annexations or indemnities. Lenin remained isolated but kept arguing that imperialism grew from monopoly finance capitalism and could end only through overthrow of capitalism.

## Return to Russia, 1917

In March 1917, starving and war-weary workers and soldiers in Petrograd overthrew the tsar. Lenin returned through Germany and Sweden, arriving in Petrograd on April 16, 1917, after the Provisional Government had taken nominal power with support from the Petrograd Soviet.

Lenin shocked even Bolsheviks by rejecting support for the Provisional Government. He argued that only soviet power could bring peace, land to peasants, and control over industry, so he raised the slogan All power to the Soviets.

## Toward the October Revolution

After weeks of persuasion, Lenin won the Bolshevik Central Committee and April Party Conference to his program. The party would withhold support from the Provisional Government, win majorities in the soviets, seek immediate peace, confiscate landlords' estates, nationalize land, and control private industry.

By autumn 1917, war-weariness, economic breakdown, and disappointment with Aleksandr Kerensky helped Bolsheviks gain majorities in major soviets. Lenin, underground after accusations of being a German agent, decided the time had come for armed seizure of power.

In The State and Revolution, Lenin argued that proletarian revolution must smash the old state and create direct rule by armed workers and peasants. To him, soviets were more democratic than parliament because they represented workers, soldiers, and peasants rather than propertied classes.

## The October Revolution

On October 23, 1917, Lenin secretly attended a Bolshevik Central Committee meeting in Petrograd and won support for armed takeover after a 10-hour debate. Trotsky, as chairman of the Petrograd Soviet, led preparations through Red Guards, soldiers, and sailors.

On November 7-8, 1917, Bolshevik-led forces overthrew the Provisional Government with little resistance. The Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets accepted power, elected Lenin chairman of the Council of People's Commissars, and approved his Peace Decree and Land Decree.

## From Fugitive to Leader

The Second All-Russian Congress of Soviets marked Lenin's move from fugitive to head of government. At age 47 he had achieved the victory for which he had spent decades building a revolutionary party.

## In Power

Once in power, Lenin focused on consolidating rule and making peace. He accepted Left Socialist Revolutionaries in government but dispersed the Constituent Assembly in January 1918 after its Menshevik and Right SR majority rejected soviet power.

The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk forced Soviet Russia to accept harsh losses to Germany and its allies in March 1918. Lenin insisted on acceptance because the army could not resist and peace was the people's deepest demand.

Civil War from 1918 to 1920 nearly destroyed the Soviet state. Lenin survived an assassination attempt in August 1918, backed the Red Army led by Trotsky, supported peasants' seizure of land, favored industrial workers in supplies and power, and used national self-determination to keep non-Russian peoples from joining the Whites.

Grain requisitioning alienated peasants once the Whites were defeated. Facing rebellion, Lenin retreated in March 1921 through the New Economic Policy, ending requisitioning and allowing peasants to sell harvests on an open market, a partial return to capitalism.

Lenin founded the Third International in March 1919 after renaming the party the Russian Communist Party (Bolsheviks). The Comintern demanded disciplined Communist parties modeled on the Bolsheviks and gained sympathy in colonies by supporting self-determination and independence.

By 1921, Lenin's government had crushed opposition parties and imposed harsh measures, including show trials and the death penalty. He also tightened control over dissent inside the Bolshevik Party, helping create conditions later associated with Joseph Stalin's dictatorship.

In 1922, Lenin became alarmed by bureaucracy, Great Russian chauvinism, and Stalin's growing power as party general secretary. Illness weakened him, but he supported a federal [[Union of Soviet Socialist Republics]] against Stalin's more unitary plan and dictated a political Testament warning about Stalin and Trotsky.

After strokes in 1922 and 1923, Lenin lost speech and political activity ended. He died at Gorki on January 21, 1924, after spending his final period trying to reverse abuses, bureaucracy, and corruption of power.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1887: Aleksandr Ulyanov was executed, deeply shaping Lenin's revolutionary path.
- 1895: Lenin helped form the Union for the Struggle for the Liberation of the Working Class.
- 1903: The Bolshevik-Menshevik split began at the Second Congress.
- 1912: Lenin made the Bolshevik split final at the Prague Conference.
- 1917: Lenin returned to Petrograd, advanced soviet power, and led the Bolshevik seizure of power.
- 1918: The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk ended Russia's role in World War I.
- 1921: The New Economic Policy replaced grain requisitioning with market sale of harvests.
- 1924: Lenin died near [[Moscow]].

## Key People

- Vladimir Ilich Lenin ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Ilich_Lenin)): Bolshevik leader, Soviet founder, Comintern founder, and source of Leninism.
- Nadezhda Krupskaya ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadezhda_Krupskaya)): Lenin's wife, secretary, and political comrade.
- Georgy Plekhanov ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Plekhanov)): Russian Marxist whose ideas shaped Lenin before Lenin moved beyond him.
- L. Martov ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Martov)): Menshevik leader and early comrade who split with Lenin over party organization.
- Leon Trotsky ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky)): Bolshevik ally who prepared the Petrograd uprising and commanded the Red Army.
- Aleksandr Kerensky ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandr_Kerensky)): Head of the Provisional Government overthrown by the Bolsheviks.
- Joseph Stalin ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin)): Party general secretary whose power alarmed Lenin in his final years.

## Interesting Facts

- Lenin ranked first in his high-school class and seemed suited for classical scholarship before becoming a revolutionary.
- Law practice gave Lenin both a cover for activism and a lasting dislike of class bias in courts.
- Iskra, meaning The Spark, was intended to unite scattered Russian Marxists into a disciplined party.
- [[Germany]] allowed Lenin to cross its territory in 1917 hoping his return would weaken Russia's war effort.
- Leninism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninism)) became a doctrine only after Lenin's death, when successors codified it with Marx's works.
