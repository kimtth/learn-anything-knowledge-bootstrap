## Introduction

Digestion is the process by which food is broken down and chemically changed so that an organism can absorb it and use it to support life. Living things need nutrients from the environment for many reasons: some become building material for cells, some act as regulators of chemical reactions, and some release energy when they are oxidized. In this way, digestion is not only about eating; it is part of the larger alimentation of an organism, which includes taking in food, breaking it down, and removing what cannot be used.

## Ingestion

![[attachments/digestion_and_digestive_systems_digestion_001.webp|420]]

*Diagram of protein digestion*

Ingestion is the act of taking nutrients from the environment. In many green plants, and also in bacteria and fungi that absorb small molecules directly, nutrients are simple enough to cross cell membranes without bulk feeding. But carbohydrates, proteins, and lipids are often too large to move easily into cells, so they must first be broken into smaller units. This is where the difference between extracellular digestion ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_digestion)) and intracellular digestion becomes important.

## Egestion

Egestion is the removal of material that the organism cannot use. When unicellular organisms digest food in vacuoles, the vacuoles eventually fuse with the cell membrane and release the waste to the outside. In animals with an alimentary canal, indigestible material such as cellulose passes into the colon, where water and ions like sodium and chloride are reabsorbed. What remains becomes solid waste and is held until it is expelled through the anus, so egestion is the final stage of handling food that has already given up its useful nutrients.

## Key Events & Developments

- classical and early modern science: Digestion is studied as a chemical and mechanical process inside the body.
- 19th century: Experiments clarify the role of stomach acid, enzymes, and the nervous system.
- modern period: Digestion is understood as a coordinated system linking mouth, stomach, intestines, and accessory organs.

## Key People

- William Beaumont ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Beaumont)): Known for direct study of digestion in the stomach.
- Claude Bernard ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Bernard)): Helped explain internal bodily regulation and digestive chemistry.
- Ivan Pavlov ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Pavlov)): Associated with digestion, reflexes, and gastric secretions.
- Physiologists ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiologists)): They turned digestion into a measurable field of study.
