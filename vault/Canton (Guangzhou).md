# Canton (Guangzhou)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation

## Introduction

Canton, officially Guangzhou, is the capital of Guangdong province in southern China and one of the country's main commercial cities. Located near the head of the Pearl River Delta, it has long served as a trading gateway between inland China and the sea and was the first Chinese port regularly visited by European traders.
Guangzhou is both an ancient urban centre and a modern industrial metropolis. Its importance comes from trade, learning, political history, and the strong regional culture of the Cantonese people.

## Landscape and City Layout

The city sits on the north bank of the Pearl River, with Baiyun Mountain to the north and Yuexiu Mountain near the old core. Over time, river deposits and urban expansion pushed the city outward, and modernization in the 1920s and 1930s widened streets, improved sewers, and created parks and arcades.

Today the central districts run along the river, with old commercial areas, government offices, skyscrapers, and newer business districts all clustered around the historic riverfront. The city's expansion accelerated after the 1980s, when growth pushed in all directions.

## People and Culture

Most residents are Cantonese speakers, and the local language remains a strong marker of identity even as Mandarin has become more common. The return of overseas Chinese and the arrival of transient workers from other provinces, both of which have made the population more mixed.

Guangzhou is culturally proud and socially vibrant. Its museums, opera, food, festivals, and parklands all help sustain a city identity that blends commerce with strong regional tradition.

## Economy and Transportation

![[attachments/canton_guangzhou_001.webp|420]]

*Map locating Guangzhou in China*

Manufacturing expanded quickly after 1950, beginning with light industries and later moving into heavy industry, machinery, chemicals, steel, cement, automobile production, and shipbuilding. Foreign investment from Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan helped accelerate this growth, and Guangzhou became a major industrial complex in southern China.

Trade remains central to the city's role. The semiannual China Import and Export Fair made Guangzhou an international trading hub, while the port system, rail links, highways, and subway network turned it into a major transport node for the Pearl River region.

## Government and Public Services

The city government is organized through the Chinese state and Communist Party hierarchy. Municipal decision-making, budgeting, economic planning, and neighborhood administration all follow that layered structure, with district and street-level bodies carrying out local functions.

Health, education, and public welfare improved sharply in the 20th century. Guangzhou developed a strong hospital network, a major centre for traditional Chinese medicine, and a dense higher-education system, including Sun Yat-sen University and several specialized institutions.

## History

![[attachments/canton_guangzhou_002.webp|420]]

*The Canton Factories in Guangzhou around 1850*

The area was inhabited early by Baiyue peoples and later became integrated into Chinese dynastic rule. The city was first named Guangzhou in 226, and under the Tang and Song dynasties it grew into a rich trading city with international connections.

In modern times the city became associated with reform, revolution, and nationalism. It was a base for Sun Yat-sen and a cradle of the revolution of 1911-12, while the Communist period later transformed its economy, government, and urban form.

## Conclusion

Guangzhou is a long-lived coastal-commercial city that repeatedly adapted to changing dynasties, regimes, and markets. Its enduring strength lies in trade, regional culture, and its position as a bridge between inland China and the world.
