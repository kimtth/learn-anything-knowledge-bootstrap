## Introduction

New York City is the largest and most influential American metropolis, located at the mouth of the Hudson River in southeastern New York state. It includes five boroughs: Manhattan, Brooklyn, the Bronx, Queens, and Staten Island. The city is really a collection of neighbourhoods, each with its own lifestyle, and moving between them can feel like moving between countries. New York is the most populous and international city in the United States.

New York's harbour, where the Hudson and East rivers enter one of the world's great ports, made it both gateway to North America and exit to the oceans. Its images define American life: Wall Street for finance, Broadway for theatre, Fifth Avenue for shopping, Madison Avenue for advertising, Greenwich Village for bohemia, Seventh Avenue for fashion, Tammany Hall for machine politics, and Harlem for jazz, African American aspiration, and slums. The Statue of Liberty symbolizes immigration, but the whole city functions as an icon of transformation into American and New Yorker identity.

## Population and Metropolitan Scale

New York's [[Population|population]] exceeded 8 million in both 2000 and 2010, while its broader metropolitan area reached nearly 19 million. For two centuries it has been the largest and wealthiest American city, a place of possibility, congestion, contradiction, diversity, and global reach.

Upper New York Bay ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Harbor)) contains Liberty Island and forms part of the harbour landscape that made the city a global port. New York's location encouraged commerce, immigration, shipping, and world-city status. The same prominence made it a target for terrorism, especially the destruction of the World Trade Center in 2001.

## Manhattan

![[attachments/new_york_city_002.webp|420]]

*Lower Manhattan skyline from Jersey City*

Manhattan is the smallest borough but the administrative, business, financial, and cultural core. Its grid of avenues and streets makes it legible, while skyscrapers, museums, theatres, and landmarks make it globally recognizable. It contains sharp contrasts: Park Avenue and the Upper East Side stand near Harlem, the Lower East Side, Greenwich Village, Chinatown, Little Italy, Spanish Harlem, Black Harlem, Wall Street, Soho, and older Dutch street patterns below Houston Street.

## The Bronx

The Bronx is the northernmost borough and mostly mainland New York. Once rural, it was annexed in stages, developed through bridges, railroads, parks, subways, immigration, and public works. Bronx county formed in 1914. The borough contains the Bronx Zoo, New York Botanical Garden, Yankee Stadium, Grand Concourse, many Art Deco buildings, and extensive parkland, but it also suffered severe postwar decline and later reinvestment.

## Brooklyn

![[attachments/new_york_city_003.webp|420]]

*Brooklyn Bridge at night in New York City*

Brooklyn, on western Long Island, is the most populous borough. Dutch towns formed the early settlement pattern, and Kings county was consolidated in 1683. Brooklyn saw the Battle of Long Island in 1776, became the world's first modern commuter suburb, grew into an independent city in 1834, and developed industry, culture, Prospect Park, and major waterborne trade. The Brooklyn Bridge, completed in 1883, strengthened ties to Manhattan and helped lead to consolidation in 1898.

## Queens

Queens is the city's largest borough by area and one of its most ethnically varied. It includes middle-class neighbourhoods, private homes, apartments, former industrial zones, cemeteries, airports, and sites of two world's fairs. The Flushing Remonstrance of 1657 marked an early victory for religious tolerance. The Queensboro Bridge, Pennsylvania Station rail link, subways, La Guardia Airport, and John F. Kennedy International Airport turned Queens into a major transportation and immigration gateway.

## Long Island Rail Road and Queens Development

The Long Island Rail Road ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island_Rail_Road)), connected to Manhattan's Pennsylvania Station in 1910, accelerated Queens's growth. Steinway piano and Poppenhusen rubber company towns, Newtown Creek industry, silent film, bridges to the Bronx, the Midtown Tunnel, airports, and world's fairs reshaped the borough. Flushing became a major Asian centre, and Latin American, African American, Irish, Greek, Polish, Lithuanian, Italian, Chinese, Korean, and East Indian communities transformed neighbourhood life.

## Staten Island

Staten Island is the least densely populated and most rural borough, located between Upper and Lower New York Bays. It remained part of New York after the English conquest of 1664, served military, quarantine, charitable, and freight roles, and joined Greater New York with a population of just over 65,000. Bridges in 1928, 1931, and especially the Verrazano-Narrows Bridge in 1964 ended isolation and brought rapid development while residents preserved the large Greenbelt park.

## Government

New York City's [[The Forms Of Government Their Historical Development (Government)|government]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government)) is led by a mayor who appoints department heads, criminal-court justices, and prepares the budget, while the 51-member City Council legislates. Home-rule tensions with the state and borough decentralization have been constant themes. From Dutch director generals and English governors to modern mayors, city authority gradually shifted. Fiorello La Guardia in the 1930s exemplified the strong modern mayor.

## Education

New York's [[Education|education]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education)) began with schools in New Amsterdam in the 1630s. Public funds appeared after 1795, Catholic parochial schools developed under John Hughes in the 1840s, and compulsory primary attendance came in 1874. After consolidation, massive school construction served hundreds of thousands. Specialized high schools such as Stuyvesant, Bronx Science, Brooklyn Tech, and Performing Arts gained national reputations, while late-20th-century education became controversial amid white flight, overcrowding, unionization, and reform.

## Parks, Recreation, and Sports

![[attachments/new_york_city_004.webp|420]]

*Central Park landscape in New York City*

New York's public culture includes parades, festivals, Halloween and Thanksgiving Day parades, Yiddish theatre, vaudeville, burlesque, parks, zoos, botanical gardens, beaches, amusement parks, and sports. Central Park, Prospect Park, Coney Island, Rockaway Beach, Orchard Beach, Richmondtown, and the Queens Museum model of the city show public recreation across the boroughs. Baseball is especially tied to New York through the Yankees, Giants, and Dodgers traditions.

## History

European interest in New York began through the search for trade routes. Giovanni da Verrazzano visited in 1524, and Henry Hudson in 1609 reported a protected harbour near good farmland. The Dutch West India Company established a trading post on Manna-hata, and by 1626 New Amsterdam existed. Peter Minuit purchased land rights for trade goods, and commerce shaped the settlement.

Peter Stuyvesant, director general of New Netherland from 1647 to 1664, imposed order, granted city status in 1653, and clashed with religious dissenters such as Quakers in Flushing. English conquest in 1664 renamed the city New York. During the American Revolution, the city suffered British occupation and fires; afterward, mayors such as James Duane, Richard Varick, and DeWitt Clinton helped lay foundations for national dominance.

## Greater New York

Greater New York formed on January 1, 1898, joining Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island under one city. Tammany Hall expanded its machine power through immigrants, patronage, subways, bridges, parks, garment work, construction, manufacturing, and education. Fiorello La Guardia's Depression and World War II mayoralty modernized government, caged Tammany, used New Deal funds, unified the subway, and centralized administration.

## September 11

On September 11, 2001, hijackers flew two airliners into the World Trade Center towers, destroying them and nearby buildings and killing about 3,000 people. A smaller bombing had struck the complex in 1993. The ruins became Ground Zero, a pilgrimage site during cleanup. A memorial opened in 2011, and One World Trade Center rose as the centre of a new complex.

## Center in New York

The World Trade Center site symbolized New York's global role before and after the attacks. The city remained host of the United Nations, a leading tourist destination, a centre of finance and building, and a world metropolis. New skyscrapers such as the Bank of America Tower and New York Times Building continued the skyline's renewal, even as the late-2000s recession damaged financial institutions.
