## Introduction

Leo Tolstoy, Russian Lev Nikolayevich Tolstoy, was a Russian count, novelist, moral thinker, and religious teacher. He lived from 1828 to 1910 and is regarded as one of the world's greatest novelists.

Tolstoy is best known for War and Peace ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_and_Peace)) and Anna Karenina, both often ranked among the finest novels ever written. His shorter work The Death of Ivan Ilyich is a classic novella.

In his final decades, Tolstoy became famous as a moral and religious teacher. His doctrine of nonresistance to evil influenced [[Gandhi]], though his religious views later lost much of their former authority.

## Style and Reputation

Tolstoy's fiction is admired for realism, psychological observation, and the sense that life itself is unfolding on the page. Matthew Arnold said a Tolstoy novel was not a work of art but a piece of life.

Tolstoy could break a single act of consciousness into tiny steps. He observed changes of thought, gesture, emotion, body movement, self-deception, and moral choice with unusual precision.

His works often seem to resist artifice. Readers and critics have seen him as both godlike in perception and tormented in his struggle to define how people should live.

## Early Life

*Leo Tolstoy 1897, black and white, 37767u*

Tolstoy was born at Yasnaya Polyana, the estate where he later lived and wrote. His mother died before he was two, and his father died in 1837; several guardians also died during his childhood.

Despite these losses, Tolstoy remembered childhood in idyllic terms. His first published work, Childhood, was a fictionalized and nostalgic account of early life.

He studied at the University of Kazan but left without a degree. He admired Rousseau, read widely, gambled, drank, tried to be socially correct, and repeatedly resolved to reform himself.

## Army, Early Writing, and Education

In 1851, Tolstoy joined his brother in the Caucasus and entered military life. His experiences produced early works such as The Cossacks and the Sevastopol sketches.

The Sevastopol sketches showed courage, suffering, formal experiment, stream of consciousness, and Tolstoy's insistence that truth, not conventional heroism, was the real hero.

After the Crimean War, Tolstoy left the army and returned to literary life. He disliked intellectual camps and remained fiercely independent, often opposing fashionable trends.

He also became interested in pedagogy. At Yasnaya Polyana he opened a school for peasant children and published educational essays that challenged usual assumptions about progress and teaching.

## War and Peace

War and Peace ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_and_Peace)), written in 1865-69, combines historical narrative, fictional biography, and essays on history. It covers the Napoleonic wars, especially 1805 and the invasion of Russia in 1812.

Tolstoy rejected the idea that great men control history. Napoleon appears as egotistical and limited, while Kutuzov is wise because he understands the limits of planning and will.

The novel's central fictional lives include Pierre Bezukhov, Prince Andrey, Natasha Rostova, Nikolay Rostov, and Marya. Their stories value daily life, family, humility, patience, marriage, and ordinary goodness.

Tolstoy's historical essays argue that events arise from countless small decisions by ordinary people. History has no simple laws, no single cause, and no fully knowable pattern.

## Anna Karenina

![[attachments/tolstoy_003.webp|420]]

*Leo Tolstoy with family*

Anna Karenina, written in 1875-77, applies Tolstoy's moral vision to family life. Its famous opening contrasts happy families with unhappy ones.

The novel interweaves the Oblonsky, Karenin, and Levin stories. Dolly represents moral steadiness in daily domestic care, while Stiva shows how small selfish choices produce harm.

Anna leaves her husband Karenin and son for Vronsky. Tolstoy presents romantic passion as unstable when separated from family, truthfulness, responsibility, and ordinary love.

Konstantin Levin searches for the meaning of life in the face of death. His answers come not through abstract systems but through work, family, humility, and daily moral practice.

## Religious Crisis and Teaching

After his great novels, Tolstoy underwent a severe spiritual crisis. He rejected church dogma, miracles, the Trinity, ritual authority, and much traditional doctrine.

Tolstoy's Christianity centred on the Sermon on the Mount. He stressed not being angry, not lusting, not taking oaths, not resisting evil with violence, and loving enemies.

His doctrine of nonresistance made him a pacifist and a kind of anarchist, because he believed governments depend on violence. He urged refusal of military service, voting, courts, and coercive institutions.

Tolstoy knew that he failed to live fully by his principles. His family life, property, copyrights, marriage, and followers created deep contradictions and conflict.

## Later Works

The Death of Ivan Ilyich is often considered one of the greatest novellas. It examines ordinary ambition, social falseness, illness, death, and the possibility of spiritual awakening.

The Kreutzer Sonata expresses Tolstoy's severe views on [[Sex and Sexuality (Sex)|sexuality]], marriage, jealousy, and abstinence. It provoked controversy and reflected the darker side of his late moralism.

Resurrection, published in 1899, attacks church and legal institutions through the story of Nekhlyudov and Katyusha Maslova. Tolstoy used its royalties to help the persecuted Dukhobors emigrate to Canada.

In What Is Art?, Tolstoy argued that true art communicates sincere feeling by infection. He attacked much celebrated high art as imitation or counterfeit.

## Final Years and Assessment

Tolstoy's final years were marked by conflict between his teachings and his household. In 1910, he left Yasnaya Polyana secretly with his daughter Aleksandra and his doctor.

He soon contracted pneumonia and died of heart failure at the railway station of Astapovo. His flight and death became international news.

Tolstoy's greatness lies in consciousness, contingency, moral complexity, and everyday life. War and Peace and Anna Karenina remain summits of realist fiction.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1828: Tolstoy was born at Yasnaya Polyana.
- 1852: Childhood appeared as his first published work.
- 1855-56: the Sevastopol sketches established his early reputation.
- 1865-69: War and Peace was published.
- 1875-77: Anna Karenina was published.
- 1899: Resurrection appeared.
- 1910: Tolstoy died at Astapovo.
