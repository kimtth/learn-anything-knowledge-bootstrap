## Introduction

Electronics ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics)) is a branch of physics and electrical engineering that studies the emission, movement, behaviour, and effects of electrons, and the devices that use them. At first the word mainly referred to the behaviour of electrons in early electron tubes. Later it came to mean a much wider field, because scientists learned more about electrons and engineers learned how to use their motion in useful devices. Electronics is a broad field behind consumer, industrial, and military products, and it compares the modern electronic revolution with the 19th century industrial revolution in importance.

## Optoelectronics

Optoelectronics ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optoelectronics)), also called optical electronics, uses photons, or packets of light, instead of electrons for some electronic functions. It belongs within electronics because these light-based systems often perform the same tasks as electronic systems and are usually embedded in electronic environments. This field links lasers, optical fibres, optical switching, displays, and light-sensitive semiconductor devices.

## Czochralski

The Czochralski method ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czochralski_method)) produces a large single-crystal silicon ingot by slowly withdrawing and rotating a seed crystal from molten silicon. The resulting ingot can be up to 300 mm in diameter and can weigh up to 100 kg. After growth, the crystal is ground into a smooth cylinder and sliced with diamond tools into wafers about 0.6 mm thick. The wafer surfaces are then polished with successively finer abrasives until one side has a mirror finish.

## Rectification

Rectification ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rectifier)) is the conversion of alternating current to direct current. This function dates back to the vacuum tube era, but it is now performed by the semiconductor diode. A diode is a two-terminal device, and a semiconductor diode contains one n-type region and one p-type region. The boundary between them is the p-n junction, where electrons on the n-type side and holes on the p-type side determine how current can pass.

## Amplification

![[attachments/electronics_001.png|420]]

*Cross-section diagram of an NPN bipolar junction transistor*

Amplification is explained through the n-p-n transistor, which contains two p-n junctions close together. The n-p-n regions act as emitter, base, and collector, although the evidence also connects these roles with source, gate, and drain language. In a normal amplifier circuit, a small forward bias is applied to the base-emitter junction and a larger reverse bias to the base-collector junction. Resistors and batteries set steady operating conditions, while an AC signal is applied in the base lead.

## Using MOSFETs

MOSFETs are another important transistor type that developed by the early 1960s. Unlike the n-p-n example, a MOSFET controls current mainly through an electric field at the gate rather than by injecting a base current. This gave circuit designers another way to control signals and helped make dense integrated circuits practical.

## Cross-sectional

The metal-oxide-semiconductor field-effect transistor, or MOSFET, has source and drain regions of one conductivity type embedded in a body of the opposite type. Between source and drain is a thin layer of silicon dioxide made by heating silicon in an oxidizing atmosphere. A thin metal gate sits on this oxide. The junction field-effect transistor works in a similar way but is used less often.

## Oscillation

Oscillation occurs when feedback is positive, so the feedback signal reinforces the original signal. An amplifier can then generate an AC signal instead of only increasing an input signal. Such oscillator circuits are needed in many electronic systems. In a tunable oscillator for a radio receiver, an inductor and capacitor form a tuned circuit whose inductive and capacitive effects balance at one frequency, giving maximum voltage at that frequency.

## Core Equations

Engineering and technology topics often combine efficiency, signal flow, and computational cost:

$$\eta=\frac{\text{useful output}}{\text{input}}$$

Efficiency compares useful output with total input, so losses appear as the gap between the two.

$$SNR=10\log_{10}\left(\frac{P_s}{P_n}\right)$$

Signal-to-noise ratio compares signal power with noise power on a logarithmic decibel scale.

$$T(n)=O(n\log n)$$

Big-O notation describes how running time grows with input size, here at a rate typical of efficient divide-and-combine algorithms.

## Key Events & Developments

- 18th and 19th centuries: Studies of electricity led to early electrical machines and prepared the ground for electronics as a separate field.
- Late 19th century: Sir Joseph John Thomson identified the electron, helping electronics become a study of electron behaviour rather than only electrical effects.
- 1904: John Ambrose Fleming patented the thermionic valve, which rectified radio signals by allowing current to flow in one direction.
- 1907: Lee De Forest patented the Audion, a three-electrode vacuum tube that could amplify signals.
- 1947: John Bardeen, Walter H. Brattain, and William B. Shockley invented the transistor at Bell Laboratories.
- 1958 and 1959: Jack Kilby, Jean Hoerni, and Robert Noyce developed key integrated-circuit ideas and methods.
- About 1960: Silicon devices began replacing germanium devices, and transistors rapidly displaced vacuum tubes in many uses.
- 1962: Brian D. Josephson predicted and discovered the Josephson effect in superconducting junctions.
- 1966 to the early 1970s: The theory and production of high-purity optical fibres made long-distance light communication practical.
- 1971: Intel Corporation introduced memory integrated circuits and helped develop the microprocessor era.
- Mid-1980s: Inexpensive microprocessors spread into household, business, industrial, government, and consumer products.
- 1993: Optical fibres capable of carrying light signals more than 215 km were available, before later amplified fibres pushed distances much farther.
- 2001: Integrated-circuit dimensions had reached about 0.13 micrometre, showing the continuing shrinkage of semiconductor fabrication.

## Interesting Facts

- Vacuum tubes made radio broadcasting, television, long-distance telephony, and the first electronic digital computers possible, but they were fragile and needed warm-up time.
- The ENIAC, completed in 1946, is presented as one of the best-known early electronic computers and one of the largest vacuum-tube systems.
- A small semiconductor diode may conduct about 0.1 ampere with a 1.5-volt forward bias, while large industrial diodes can carry up to 5,000 amperes.
- Silicon wafers made by the Czochralski method can come from ingots up to 300 mm in diameter and 100 kg in weight.
- Semiconductor fabrication may require more than 600 sequential steps in an environment cleaner than a hospital operating room.
- Optical fibres guide light because the core and cladding have different refractive indexes, bending light back toward the core.
- Single-mode fibres reduce pulse spreading by forcing light to follow one main path instead of many modes.
- Josephson junctions are useful not only for fast switching but also for sensitive magnetic-field detection and absolute voltage standards.
- Quartz oscillators can reach precision as high as one part in 100 billion under carefully controlled conditions.
- Thyristors can handle very large power levels efficiently, with the largest examples blocking up to 5,000 volts and conducting up to 2,000 amperes.
