# Aristophanes

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Aristophanes was the leading surviving playwright of Athenian Old Comedy, a form that combined chorus, fantasy, obscenity, parody, and direct civic satire. His plays engage the Peloponnesian War, popular politics, lawsuits, intellectual fashions, and tragic literature while preserving an unusually vivid comic view of classical Athens.

## Introduction

Aristophanes, born about 450 BC and dead about 388 BC, is the greatest surviving poet of ancient Greek Old Comedy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Comedy)). This comic form used chorus, mime, burlesque, fantasy, invective, obscene humour, and free political criticism.

Aristophanes was an Athenian citizen of the deme Pandionis. He began in 427 BC with The Banqueters and is thought to have written about 40 plays. His comedies are closely tied to Athens during the Peloponnesian War and attack war politics, public institutions, intellectual fashions, and literary rivals.

## Babylonians

*Bust of Aristophanes*

Babylonians ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia)), produced in 426 BC, survives only in fragments. It attacked Cleon and showed Athenian allies as slaves of the Athenian Demos. Cleon impeached Aristophanes, but the punishment seems to have been light.

## Acharnians

Acharnians, produced in 425 BC, is the earliest complete surviving Aristophanic play. The farmer Dicaeopolis makes a private peace with Sparta, enjoys trade and pleasure, and exposes the Peloponnesian War as civic folly.

## Knights

Knights, produced in 424 BC, renews the attack on Cleon. Cleon appears as a corrupt favourite slave of the foolish Demos and is defeated by Agoracritus, a sausage seller even more shameless than he is.

## Clouds

Clouds, produced in 423 BC, attacks modern education and Sophistic argument. [[Socrates]] is unfairly turned into a comic teacher of verbal trickery, and Strepsiades seeks instruction to escape debt.

## Wasps

Wasps, produced in 422 BC, mocks Athenian love of lawsuits and juries. Philocleon is addicted to jury service, and Bdelycleon tries to cure him with a home court. The play also attacks Cleon’s use of subsidized juries.

## Peace

Peace, produced in 421 BC, appeared just before the Peace of Nicias. The farmer Trygaeus flies to heaven on a giant dung beetle and rescues the goddess Peace from the God of War, ending in rural celebration.

## Birds

[[Birds]], produced in 414 BC, is Aristophanes’ most fantastical play. Peisthetaerus persuades the birds to build Cloudcuckooland, a city between gods and humans, and the birds finally force the Olympian gods to submit.

## Lysistrata

Lysistrata, produced in 411 BC, turns political crisis into farce. Women seize the Acropolis and treasury and declare a sex strike until men make peace, mixing indecency, urgency, and antiwar feeling.

## Women at the Thesmophoria

Women at the Thesmophoria, produced in 411 BC, parodies tragedy and gender disguise. Athenian women plan revenge on [[Euripides]], and his disguised kinsman Mnesilochus is exposed while trying to defend him.

## Frogs

Frogs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs)), produced in 405 BC, is a literary comedy. Dionysus goes to Hades to bring back Euripides but chooses [[Aeschylus]] instead after a contest, judging him more able to help troubled Athens.

## Women at the Ecclesia

Women at the Ecclesia, produced about 392 BC, shows women disguising themselves as men, taking over the Athenian assembly, and imposing a communistic system of wealth, sex, and property.

## Wealth

Wealth ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth)), produced in 388 BC, is more moralizing and less forceful than earlier plays. Its lack of chorus may mark the transition from Old Comedy to Middle Comedy. Aristophanes died soon afterward.

## Key Events & Developments

- 427 BC: Aristophanes began with The Banqueters.
- 426 BC: Babylonians ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia)) brought Cleon’s legal action.
- 425-421 BC: Early plays attacked war politics and Cleon.
- 405 BC: Frogs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frogs)) made literary judgment a civic question.
- 388 BC: Wealth ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth)) suggested the move toward Middle Comedy.

## Key People

- Aristophanes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristophanes)): Main surviving poet of Old Comedy.
- Cleon ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleon)): Repeated target of political satire.
- [[Socrates]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socrates)): Comic target in Clouds.
- Lysistrata ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lysistrata)): Heroine who organizes women to force peace.
- [[Euripides]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripides)): Tragic poet mocked and parodied in several plays.

## Interesting Facts

- Old Comedy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Comedy)) allowed bold fantasy, obscenity, and direct political attack.
- Cloudcuckooland became a lasting term for impossible utopian schemes.
- Wealth ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth)) may be the only surviving example of Middle Comedy.
