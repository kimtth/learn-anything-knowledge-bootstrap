# Alexandria

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Alexandria is Egypt's major Mediterranean port, founded by Alexander the Great and renowned in antiquity as a centre of Hellenistic scholarship, science, trade, and urban life. It remains an important industrial and cultural city.

## Introduction

Alexandria, or Al-Iskandariyyah, is a major city and urban governorate in [[Egypt]]. It stands on the Mediterranean Sea at the western edge of the Nile delta, about 114 miles northwest of [[Cairo]]. Founded by [[Alexander the Great]] in 332 BCE, it served as the capital of [[Egypt]] until its surrender to Arab forces led by Amr ibn al-As in 642 CE. In antiquity it was one of the greatest cities of the Mediterranean world and a leading centre of Hellenic scholarship and science ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science)). In modern Egypt it remains one of the country's largest cities, its principal seaport, and a major industrial centre.

## Landscape

The modern city stretches roughly 25 miles from east to west along a limestone ridge that separates Lake Maryut ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mariout)) from the mainland. An hourglass-shaped promontory formed by the silting up of the Heptastadion, the mole built soon after the city's foundation, links the island of Pharos to the mainland centre. This formation creates the two great harbour basins of the city, the Eastern Harbour and the Western Harbour. Alexandria's site has always been central to its significance: protected anchorages, access to inland water, and a position on the Mediterranean made it both a naval base and a commercial gateway.

## Climate

The prevailing north wind blowing from the Mediterranean gives Alexandria a noticeably different climate from that of Egypt's desert interior. Summers are relatively temperate, though July and August are hot and humid, while winters are cool and often marked by strong storms, heavy rain, and even hail. This maritime climate distinguishes Alexandria from inland Egyptian cities and helps explain part of its long attraction as a settlement and port.

## City Layout and Monuments

![[attachments/alexandria_001.webp|420]]

*Qaitbay Citadel near the site of the ancient Pharos of Alexandria*

The city was designed by Dinocrates, Alexander's architect, and incorporated major features of Hellenic urban planning. Within a century of its foundation, its splendour rivaled the greatest cities of the ancient world. The most famous monument was the Pharos of Alexandria, the great lighthouse on the island of Pharos, one of the Seven Wonders of the World. It is said to have stood more than 350 feet high and survived into the 12th century. In 1477, Sultan Qait Bey reused stones from the ruined lighthouse to build a fort on or near its site.

## People

From the late 19th century to the 1980s, Alexandria's population increased tenfold through both high birth rates and migration from the countryside. After the 1952 revolution, population growth accelerated further, passing 1.5 million within a decade and more than 2 million by 1976, with a very young age structure. By the early 21st century the population had exceeded 4 million.

## Economy

Alexandria's economic importance remains very large. Manufacturing, shipping, warehousing, banking, food processing, and the production of petrochemicals and cement all contribute significantly to the national economy. The city and its surroundings account for roughly two-fifths of [[Egypt|Egypt's]] industrial production. Most industrial expansion has taken place to the west of the city around the Western Harbour and along the southern flank. Major industrial zones include Mina al-Basal, Al-Qabbari, Al-Maks, Al-Dukhaylah, and Al-Amiriyyah, where refineries, mills, salt works, tanning, and cement production are located.

## Transportation

Alexandria is linked to the rest of [[Egypt]] by railway, highways, air routes, and canal connections to the Nile. Inside the city, trams, taxis, and buses provide urban transport. The rail connection to [[Cairo]] is especially important, and the city is also the terminus for the line running west toward the Libyan frontier. The Alexandria-Cairo desert highway relieved pressure on the older agricultural route through the delta and encouraged desert development.

## Government and Services

Alexandria became the first fully constituted municipality in [[Egypt]] in 1890. It once enjoyed a high degree of municipal autonomy, but this was suspended in 1926 and reorganized under the central Egyptian government in 1935. After the 1952 revolution, urban administration became increasingly centralized. The governor is appointed by the president and works with an elected local council, while the governorate answers to the national ministry responsible for local affairs.

## Education and Cultural Life

![[attachments/alexandria_002.webp|420]]

*Bibliotheca Alexandrina in Alexandria*

Education follows the national Egyptian system of primary, preparatory, and secondary schooling, with higher education in universities and institutes. Alexandria University, founded in 1942, is the principal public university. Arabic is the usual language of instruction, though English is widely used in some professional programs. The city also later gained a private francophone university named for Leopold Senghor.

## History

Alexander founded the city in 332 BCE early in his Persian campaign, intending it to be the capital of his Egyptian dominion and a naval base controlling the Mediterranean. The site included the ancient settlement of Rhakotis and offered freshwater from Lake Maryut as well as excellent anchorage behind Pharos. After Alexander left, his viceroy Cleomenes continued the city's creation. After Alexander's death, control passed to Ptolemy I Soter, founder of the Ptolemaic dynasty.

## Conclusion
Alexandria is a city whose significance lies in the unusual combination of [[Geography|geography]], commerce, and cultural memory. It was once a capital, a great port, a centre of science and scholarship, and a city of many peoples and languages. Though it lost and regained prominence in different forms, it never became historically irrelevant. Modern Alexandria still depends on its ports, industries, and institutions of learning, while its image remains shaped by the long shadow of the ancient city founded by Alexander.

## Key Events & Developments

- 332 BCE: [[Alexander the Great]] founds Alexandria as the capital of his Egyptian dominion.
- 3rd century BCE: The Mouseion and the great library establish the city as a centre of learning.
- 642 CE: Arab forces under Amr ibn al-As take the city and end its role as capital.
- 15th century: Alexandria still prospers as a transit point between the Red Sea and the Mediterranean.
- 1798: French invasion finds the city reduced to a small town.
- 19th century: Cotton trade and renewed global commerce bring Alexandria back to importance.
- 2002: The Bibliotheca Alexandrina opens as a modern symbol of the city's scholarly heritage.
