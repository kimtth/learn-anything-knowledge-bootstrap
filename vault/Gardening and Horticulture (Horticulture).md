# Gardening and Horticulture (Horticulture)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Horticulture is plant cultivation focused on fruit, vegetables, flowers, and ornamental crops between household gardening and broad-acre agriculture. Its branches and methods apply biology, propagation, climate, soil, season, and light responses to food production and designed landscapes.

## Introduction

Horticulture is the branch of plant cultivation concerned with garden crops such as fruits, vegetables, flowers, and ornamental plants. It lies between domestic gardening and large-scale field agriculture, and it includes both food production and ornamental use. It also divides horticulture into pomology, olericulture, floriculture, and landscape horticulture. The central point is that horticulture combines biological knowledge with practical cultivation, especially in regions where climate, soil, and season strongly shape what can be grown.

These branches cover fruit and nut crops, kitchen vegetables, cut flowers, pot plants, greenery, lawn turf, shrubs, trees, and climbers, so horticultural production ranges from food markets to designed landscapes.

## Horticultural Regions

Horticulture is organized around major climatic zones. Temperate regions are especially important because they support a huge range of plants, from bulbs and annuals to deciduous trees and cereals. It also notes that altitude matters, since lower mountain slopes can behave like temperate zones. The length of daylight and the winter resting period are especially important for many plants, and photoperiodism, the way plants respond to light duration, acts as a major factor in flowering and growth.

## Propagation

Propagation is presented as the most basic horticultural practice. Plants may be increased by seed or by vegetative means such as tubers, corms, runners, cuttings, layering, grafting, and tissue culture. Seed propagation is inexpensive and often disease-free, but it can produce variation in cross-pollinated plants. Vegetative propagation preserves the exact characteristics of a cultivar and is therefore important for fruit trees, roses, and other horticultural crops that must remain true to type.

Some plants also reproduce through apomixis, forming asexual seed, while bulbs, corms, rhizomes, runners, tubers, and offsets act as natural storage and propagation organs.

## Breeding and Environmental Control

Plant breeding is a scientific way of developing improved cultivars suited to different regions and uses. It relies on the natural variation among plants and related wild species. It also shows that horticulture depends heavily on controlling the environment through structures such as cold frames, hotbeds, greenhouses, and shade houses. Temperature, light, water, and carbon dioxide can be managed to improve plant growth, especially in commercial nurseries.

Commercial horticulture uses many structures for production and protection. Greenhouses, cold frames, cloches, hot caps, and tunnels all help manage temperature and extend the season. Horticulture also includes the nursery trade, especially the production of ornamental shrubs, roses, and shade trees. It emphasizes that nursery growing is economically important but also risky because crops take time to mature and labour costs are high. Container-grown stock and improved propagation methods have helped reduce some of these difficulties.

## Landscape Horticulture

Horticulture connects closely to landscape architecture. Horticulturists provide the living plant materials used in designed landscapes, including shrubs, trees, climbers, and ground covers. These plants are not only decorative; they also help control erosion, provide shade and wind protection, and give structure to the outdoor environment. In this sense, horticulture supports the larger practice of shaping landscapes for human use and enjoyment.

## Key Events & Developments

- Horticulture developed as a specialized field between garden cultivation and field agriculture.
- The study of climate, light, and temperature became central to crop success.
- Modern propagation methods such as grafting and tissue culture expanded the industry.
- Greenhouses and other controlled structures transformed commercial production.
