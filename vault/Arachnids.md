# Arachnids

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Arachnids are chelicerate arthropods that include spiders, scorpions, mites, ticks, and related groups. Their two-part body plan, specialized appendages, predation or parasitism, molting, and diverse respiratory systems support a wide range of terrestrial life.

## Introduction

Arachnids ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachnid)) are members of an arthropod group that includes spiders, scorpions, daddy longlegs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opiliones)), mites, ticks, and several lesser-known lineages. They form a highly varied branch of the chelicerate arthropods, with only a minority of species having direct economic significance. Those that do matter most to humans are often mites and ticks that transmit disease to animals, plants, or people.

## General Body Plan

![[attachments/arachnids_001.webp|420]]

*Spider internal anatomy diagram*

The typical arachnid body is divided into two main regions: the cephalothorax, or prosoma, and the abdomen, or opisthosoma. This division is clear in most groups, although in daddy longlegs and many mites and ticks the boundary is less obvious or almost lost. This two-part body plan is the chief diagnostic feature of the class.

## Diversity of Form

There is strong diversity across the class. Scorpions have pincerlike pedipalps and a narrow postabdomen ending in a sting, while spiders often have venomous fangs and a narrow stalk joining the two body regions. Daddy longlegs are delicate and long-legged, and mites and ticks often have compact rounded bodies with reduced visible segmentation.

## Locomotion and Dispersal

Most arachnids move by coordinated leg patterns rather than by great speed or long travel, and many are not major long-distance travellers. When they do spread widely, they often rely on indirect methods.

## Feeding and Predation

Most arachnids are predators on smaller arthropods, though some groups are parasitic or plant-feeding. Arachnids generally lack true jaws and often digest prey externally. Digestive fluids are injected into or flowed over the prey, and the liquefied tissues are then sucked up.

## Behaviour and Associations

![[attachments/arachnids_002.webp|420]]

*Spider web showing silk-based prey capture*

Arachnids are usually solitary, but there are important exceptions. Some spiders form large communal webs in which many individuals share prey-catching space and live near one another. This shows that social association, while uncommon, is not absent from the class.

## Support, Growth, and Muscles

Like other arthropods, arachnids possess a chitinous exoskeleton that provides support and protection. Growth requires molting, because the rigid cuticle must be shed and replaced; this is a hormonally controlled process involving the production of a new cuticle beneath the old one.

## Nervous System and Sense Organs

The arachnid nervous system consists of a brain and associated ganglia, much of it concentrated toward the head region through fusion and forward migration. Arachnids usually possess simple eyes rather than compound eyes, and their number and arrangement vary by group.

## Respiration, Circulation, and Excretion

Arachnids use either book lungs, tracheae, or both. Book lungs consist of stacked lamellae in internal chambers where gases diffuse between air and hemolymph. Tracheae are tube systems that distribute gases through the body, and different orders rely on different combinations of these respiratory methods.

## Reproduction and Special Glands

The sexes are separate in arachnids, though outward difference between males and females may be subtle or, in some cases, very marked. Reproductive organs and sperm-transfer structures vary widely, and in several groups appendages are modified for mating functions.

## Classification

Arachnid classification relies mostly on external morphology: overall body shape, the degree of segmentation, modifications of prosoma and abdomen, appendage structure, and sperm-transfer organs. Internal anatomy and development play a smaller role, though newer molecular evidence may grow in importance.

## Conclusion

Arachnids ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachnid)) are a diverse class of chelicerate arthropods unified by a two-part body, six pairs of prosomal appendages, simple eyes, and specialized predatory or parasitic biology. Their many forms, from tarantulas to ticks, show how extensively a common structural plan can diversify.

## Key Events & Developments

- Arachnids diversify as terrestrial chelicerates with specialized appendages and external digestion.
- Spiders develop silk-based dispersal and prey capture strategies.
- Ticks and mites become major disease vectors and plant/animal parasites.
- Modern classification continues to refine the relationships among the orders.
- Molecular data are expected to change arachnid taxonomy further.

## Interesting Facts

- Arachnids have six pairs of appendages on the prosoma.
- Book lungs and tracheae are both used for respiration.
- Ballooning lets some spiders disperse by wind.
- Silk glands and spinnerets are arachnid specializations.
- Most arachnids are predators, but some are parasitic or plant-feeding.
