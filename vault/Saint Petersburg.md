# Saint Petersburg

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Saint Petersburg is Russia's northern Baltic port, imperial capital, revolutionary centre, and canal-lined cultural city. Founded by Peter I as a western-facing capital, its architecture, waterways, museums, flood-prone landscape, wartime siege, and political history express Russia's changing relationship with Europe and empire.

## Introduction

![[attachments/saint_petersburg_003.webp|420]]

*Boats on the Neva River in Saint Petersburg*

Saint Petersburg, Russian Sankt Peterburg, was called Petrograd from 1914 to 1924 and Leningrad from 1924 to 1991. It is a city and port in extreme northwestern Russia, about 400 miles, or 640 km, northwest of Moscow and only about seven degrees south of the Arctic Circle.

Founded by Peter I in 1703 as Russia's window on Europe, Saint Petersburg served as imperial capital from 1712 to 1918. It was central to the Revolutions of 1917 and became famous for its fierce defense during the World War II siege. Its historic district became a UNESCO World Heritage ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site)) site in 1990.

The city is known for three traits: a harmonious mix of western European and Russian architecture, no single medieval-style centre, and a landscape of waterways. The Neva River, its tributaries, canals, bridges, and embankments earned the city the nickname Venice of the North.

## Landscape

Saint Petersburg stands on the delta of the Neva River at the head of the Gulf of Finland. It spreads across 42 delta islands and adjacent mainland floodplain. The low, marshy site has long made flooding a danger, especially during autumn storms and spring thaw.

Severe floods occurred in 1777, 1824, and 1924. To control floodwaters, the city built an 18-mile, or 29-km, dike across the Gulf of Finland in the 1980s, and canals were cut to aid drainage.

Greater Saint Petersburg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg)) forms a horseshoe around the Gulf of Finland and includes Kotlin Island. It extends north toward Zelenogorsk through resort and dacha areas, south toward Peterhof and Lomonosov, and east up the Neva toward Ivanovskoye.

## Climate

The [[Atlantic Ocean]] moderates Saint Petersburg's far-northern climate, though winters remain cold. January averages about 21 F, or -6 C, and temperatures can fall below -40 F. Snow cover lasts about 132 days, and the Neva normally freezes from mid-November into April.

Summers are moderately warm, with July averaging about 65 F, or 18 C. Annual precipitation is about 25 inches, or 634 mm, with summer as the wettest season. Icebreakers help extend the navigation season.

## Admiralty Side

![[attachments/saint_petersburg_001.webp|420]]

*Palace Square in Saint Petersburg*

The Admiralty Side holds much of the city's historical and cultural heritage. The Admiralty, reconstructed by Andreyan Zakharov in 1806-23, grew from Peter's original city and remains a landmark through its ship-topped spire. Nearby Palace Square contains the Alexander Column and the Winter Palace.

The Winter Palace, built by Bartolomeo Rastrelli from 1754 to 1762, was the main residence of the tsars. Destroyed by fire in 1837, it was rebuilt by 1839. Together with the Hermitage, it houses one of the world's great collections of western European art.

Nevsky Prospekt functions as the city's central avenue. It includes churches, shops, theatres, Gostiny Dvor, Kazan Cathedral, and the approach to Alexander Nevsky Monastery. Canals and rivers such as the Moyka, Fontanka, Griboyedov, and Obvodny cut through the district.

The Smolny complex includes Rastrelli's cathedral and Quarenghi's Smolny Institute, which served as [[Lenin]]'s headquarters in 1917 and later housed city government. The Summer Garden, founded in 1704, contains Peter's early Summer Palace and a famous wrought-iron fence along the Neva.

## Vasilyevsky Island

Vasilyevsky Island was one of the first developed areas because it was defensible. At its eastern tip, the Strelka faces the split of the Neva. The former Exchange building, Rostral Columns, Twelve Colleges, Academy of Sciences, Menshikov Palace, Academy of Arts, and naval and literary institutions make the island a major cultural and architectural zone.

The Twelve Colleges, designed by Domenico Trezzini, originally housed Peter's supreme government bodies and later became part of the state university. Modern museums on the island include Erarta and Novy Muzei, both focused on contemporary art.

## Petrograd Side and Vyborg Side

![[attachments/saint_petersburg_002.webp|420]]

*Aerial view of Peter and Paul Fortress in Saint Petersburg*

The Petrograd Side contains the Peter-Paul Fortress, founded in 1703 as the city's first structure. Its stone walls, bastions, and Cathedral of St. Peter and St. Paul became symbols of the city. The cathedral, built in 1712-33, contains the tombs of most Russian tsars and tsarinas from Peter's time.

The fortress later served as a political prison and is now a museum. Nearby, the cruiser Aurora is moored as a museum; its blank shot in 1917 signaled the storming of the Winter Palace during the October Revolution.

The Vyborg Side developed as an industrial district in the late 19th century but later shifted toward offices, housing, and retail. Finland Railway Station is historically important because [[Lenin]] returned there in April 1917 and announced the course that led to Bolshevik power.

## Outer Region and Palaces

Saint Petersburg's outer region includes greenbelts, housing blocks, dachas, orchards, vegetable gardens, and historic satellite towns. Commuting is the price of cleaner air and more space, while dense apartment blocks keep built-up areas crowded.

Peterhof is famous for its terraces, parks, Great Palace, Grand Cascade, and fountains overlooking the Gulf of Finland. Much was plundered or destroyed during World War II and later restored. The town was officially called Petrodvorets from 1944 until the name Peterhof returned in 2009.

Pushkin, formerly Tsarskoye Selo, contains the Catherine Palace and Amber Room, the Chinese Village, Cameron Gallery, and Lycee attended by Aleksandr Pushkin. Pavlovsk contains a classical palace and park for Paul I. Gatchina is known for a palace built by Antonio Rinaldi for Grigory Orlov.

## People

Saint Petersburg's population is overwhelmingly Russian. Before the Revolution, it had Polish, Baltic, German, Tatar, Jewish, and Chinese communities. Many non-Russian residents emigrated after 1917, and others suffered under Stalinist purges.

The city continued attracting Russian peasant labour in the interwar period, and after World War II newcomers remained numerous. By the late 20th century, more than half the population was native to the city. Post-Soviet hate crimes often targeted immigrants from Asia, Africa, and former Soviet republics, yet residents were still associated with politeness, reserve, and cultivated manners.

## Economy and Manufacturing

Saint Petersburg is second only to Moscow in Russian industrial output. In late Soviet times, more than half the working population was in factories and building trades. After post-Soviet deindustrialization, industry declined but later recovered, and by the early 21st century the city's economy was growing faster than Russia's overall.

Major industries include mechanical engineering, metalworking, shipbuilding, armaments, rolling stock, nuclear reactors, turbines, electrical machinery, diesel engines, batteries, medical equipment, cameras, elevators, automobiles, and food processing. Shipbuilding remains especially important, producing icebreakers, tankers, timber carriers, and fishing vessels.

Services make up about three-fifths of the economy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy)). Banking, retail trade, foreign investment, transportation, real estate, and manufacturing all developed strongly by the early 21st century. Electrical power comes from hydroelectric plants on the Volkhov, Svir, and Vuoksa rivers and nuclear generation.

## Transportation

Saint Petersburg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg)) is one of Russia's major transport hubs. Its port is the country's largest and has international importance. Ferries connect with western European ports, and the Neva links smaller ships to Lake Ladoga, the Volga-Baltic Waterway, the White Sea Canal, the Volga basin, the Caspian, the Black Sea, and the Sea of Azov.

Pulkovo International Airport lies about 11 miles, or 18 km, south of the city. Rail lines connect Saint Petersburg with Helsinki, Warsaw, Moscow, and other Russian cities. The subway opened in 1955, and buses, streetcars, trolleybuses, and suburban electric trains serve the wider area.

## Government and Services

Since 1931, Saint Petersburg has had the status of a city directly subordinated to the Russian republic, like Moscow. It is divided into districts and governs some suburban districts, while surrounding rural districts belong to Leningrad oblast, a separate federal subject that retained its Soviet-era name.

Housing includes modern apartments and many older buildings. About one-fifth of residents still lived in communal apartments, a Soviet legacy in which several families share one apartment. Health care deteriorated in the 1990s, but the city retained many public and private clinics, hospitals, maternity centres, and specialized facilities.

## Education and Cultural Life

Saint Petersburg is a major centre of [[Education|education]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education)) and research. St. Petersburg State University began in 1724; the Academy of Arts dates to 1757, the Institute of Mines to 1773, and the Military Medical Academy to 1798. The city is important for Arctic, botanical, geological, forestry, zoological, astronomical, and permafrost research.

The city is one of Russia's great cultural capitals. Its institutions include the Mariinsky Theatre, Maly Theatre, Tovstonogov Theatre, Pushkin Theatre, Musical Comedy Theatre, October Great Concert Hall, Rimsky-Korsakov Conservatory, Hermitage, State Russian Museum, and many smaller museums, theatres, and auditoriums.

## History

The region around the Gulf of Finland was settled by Russians in the 8th or 9th century and was known as Ingria. It came under Novgorod, then Moscow, before Sweden annexed it in 1617. During the Second Northern War, Peter the Great captured Noteburg and Nienshants, gaining the Neva delta.

On May 16, 1703, Old Style, or May 27, New Style, Peter laid the foundations of the Peter-Paul Fortress on Zayachy Island. This date is treated as the founding of Saint Petersburg ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg)). He soon founded Kronshlot on Kotlin Island and the Admiralty shipyard, whose first warship launched in 1706.

The city grew rapidly as an imperial capital, with work by architects such as Giacomo Quarenghi, Carlo Rossi, and Andrey Voronikhin. Kazan Cathedral, St. Isaac's Cathedral, the Admiralty, Senate, Smolny Institute, Mikhaylovsky Palace, and other buildings gave the city its classical grandeur.

Industrialization in the 19th century created a large proletariat that contrasted with imperial splendour. Canals, steamships, railways, textiles, metalworking, and port traffic expanded. The city became a centre of revolutionary unrest and the stage of the February and October Revolutions of 1917.

During [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]], Leningrad endured a siege of about 900 days, suffering immense destruction and loss. In the post-Soviet period, crime, deindustrialization, homelessness, new cafes, restored landmarks, illuminated bridges, and cultural renewal all marked the city. In 2006, Saint Petersburg hosted the Group of Eight summit.
