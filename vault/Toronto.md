## Introduction

Toronto ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto)) is the capital of Ontario and the centre of Canada's most populous metropolitan area. It is also Canada's major financial and commercial centre.

The city lies on the northern shore of Lake Ontario ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ontario)), with access to Atlantic shipping through the St. Lawrence Seaway and to U.S. industrial centres through the Great Lakes.

Since the second half of the 20th century, Toronto grew from a provincial city into a lively cosmopolitan metropolis. In 1998 it amalgamated with East York, Etobicoke, North York, Scarborough, and York.

Toronto's site is mostly flat, with a rise inland marking the old shoreline of glacial Lake Iroquois. Streets follow a grid modified by diagonal routes roughly related to the shore.

The central business districts cluster around Yonge, Bloor, Queen, King, and Bay streets. The financial core near King and Bay includes major banks, insurance offices, and the Toronto Stock Exchange.

The skyline includes the CN Tower, First Canadian Place, Scotia Plaza, the Toronto-Dominion Centre, City Hall, the Royal Ontario Museum, and other high-rise and cultural buildings.

Toronto's lakefront is separated from downtown by rail lines, an expressway, and residential development. Ferries connect to the Toronto Islands, which have recreation, yacht clubs, an airport, and housing.

Lake Ontario moderates the [[Climate and Weather (Climate)|climate]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate)). Winters can fall below freezing but heavy snow is not constant; summers are humid and can be hot.

## People and Economy

Toronto was once largely Protestant and British in origin, but postwar immigration transformed it. European arrivals in the 1950s and 1960s were later joined by many immigrants from the West Indies and Asia.

The city benefits from its Great Lakes position, rail and trucking links, and location in Canada's richest province. Its economy is diverse, with finance, trade, manufacturing, tourism, services, and cultural industries.

Ontario produces much of Canada's manufactured output and exports. Toronto's access to raw materials, hydroelectric power, agriculture, timber, minerals, and markets helped make it a major economic centre.

## Transportation and Government

The Toronto Transit Commission coordinates public transport, including buses, streetcars, subway lines, and rapid transit. Provincial highways serve commuters across the metropolitan area.

Toronto Pearson International Airport, west of the city in Mississauga, is Canada's busiest air terminal.

After amalgamation, Toronto is governed by a mayor and City Council. Community councils consider planning and local matters for major areas such as North York, Scarborough, Etobicoke York, and Toronto-East York.

## Cultural Life

Toronto is a major cultural centre. It has the Toronto Symphony Orchestra, theatres, experimental companies, the Art Gallery of Ontario, the Royal Ontario Museum, the Ontario Science Centre, and many private galleries.

The restored Distillery District became a cultural and entertainment area with galleries, cafes, theatres, shops, and artists' studios.

The city hosts events such as Luminato, Nuit Blanche, the Toronto International Film Festival, and the Canadian National Exhibition, first launched in 1879 as the Toronto Industrial Exhibition.

Sports are also important. The Maple Leafs, Raptors, Argonauts, and Blue Jays represent hockey, basketball, Canadian football, and baseball.

## History

The first known settlements in the area were Teiaiagon and Ganatsekwyagon, inhabited by Seneca and later Mississauga peoples. Teiaiagon became a trading post on routes linking the Great Lakes and interior.

French traders established Magasin Royal from 1720 to 1730, and later small forts defended French trade. After France's defeat in 1759 and the end of the Seven Years' War, Canada came under British sovereignty.

After the American Revolution, the area attracted Loyalists. In 1787, British authorities purchased land for a future provincial capital.

In 1793, Lieutenant Governor John Graves Simcoe chose the site as the capital of Upper Canada and named it York. It later became Toronto and grew into Canada's largest metropolitan centre.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1720-1730: French traders operated Magasin Royal in the area.
- 1763: Canada came under British sovereignty after the Seven Years' War.
- 1793: York was founded as the capital of Upper Canada.
- 1879: the Toronto Industrial Exhibition began, later becoming the Canadian National Exhibition.
- 1998: Toronto amalgamated with surrounding municipalities into the present City of Toronto.
