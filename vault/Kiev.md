# Kiev

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Kiev, also called Kyiv, is Ukraine's capital and a major cultural, transport, and economic center on the Dnieper River. Its identity rests on its role as the center of Kievan Rus, reconstruction after World War II, and renewed national importance after Ukrainian independence.

## Introduction

Kiev, also called Kyiv or Kyyiv, is Ukraine's capital, a port on the Dnieper River, and a major railroad junction. Its long history gives it the title Mother of Rus Cities ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv)), because it was the centre of Kievan Rus, the first eastern Slavic state, about 1,000 years ago.

Modern Kiev was badly damaged in World War II, restored by the mid-1950s, and developed as an economic and cultural centre in the later 20th century. Ukraine's independence in 1991 renewed its status as a major European capital.

## Landscape

*Kyiv along the Dnieper River*

The city site stands on the Dnieper below the Desna confluence, far upriver from the Black Sea. The oldest city grew on the steep western bank, where wooded bluffs and high ground rise above the river. Since World War II, the city has also spread onto the flatter eastern floodplain.

Kiev's climate is moderately continental. Winters are cold but often include thawing days, while summers are warm. Snow usually lasts from mid-November to late March, and annual rainfall is heaviest in June and July.

The city layout centres on the ancient Upper Town and the old street pattern of the high western bank. St. Sophia Cathedral, built in the 11th century, preserves early Rus-Byzantine architecture with mosaics and frescoes. Other landmarks include St. Andrew's church, the Golden Gate ruins, and the Desiatynna Church remains.

Central Kiev includes Khreshchatyk, the main axis of the city centre, with gardens, squares, offices, shops, and public buildings. Nearby are the university on Taras Shevchenko Boulevard, St. Volodymyr's Cathedral, statues of Bohdan Khmelnytsky and Taras Shevchenko, the old Podil trading quarter, the river port, and the Pecherskyy government district.

## Economy

Kiev has major administrative employment because it is the capital of Ukraine. It is also an industrial centre with factories spread through the city, especially west of the centre and on the left bank of the Dnieper.

Manufacturing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing)) is led by engineering industries using metal from the Dnieper Bend and Donbas regions. Products include machinery, precision tools, chemical-factory equipment, metal-cutting machines, aircraft, elevators, electrical instruments, ships, motorcycles, and cinematography equipment. Chemical products, wood processing, building materials, cameras, knitwear, footwear, food, watches, and publishing are also important.

Power comes from natural gas, electricity from the Kiev hydroelectric station at Vyshhorod, and the larger Trypillya thermal station southeast of the city. These systems support the city's heavy industrial and administrative economy.

## Transportation

[[Transportation]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)) links Kiev with Moscow, Kharkiv, Donbas, Odessa, western Ukraine, and Poland by rail and all-weather roads. The Dnieper has been made more navigable by barrages and reservoirs. Boryspil International Airport connects the city with Ukrainian and international destinations, while urban transport includes subway, rail, bus, streetcar, and trolleybus service.

## Society And Culture

Health and education are well developed. Kiev has general and specialized hospitals, neighbourhood polyclinics, nursery schools, primary and secondary schools, evening schools, and technical schools. Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv leads the city's higher education, and the National Academy of Sciences supports research, including medical and cybernetic work.

Cultural life ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture)) continues Kiev's old role as a cultural centre. The city has opera, drama, youth, open-air, and musical comedy theatres, many cinemas, film production, concerts, museums, and libraries. The Taras Shevchenko National Opera and the Tchaikovsky Conservatory are especially important institutions.

## History

Kiev's earliest settlement reaches far back into prehistory. Archaeological finds include Upper Paleolithic remains, Neolithic farming settlements, and artifacts from the Copper, Bronze, and Iron ages. Local tribes traded with steppe peoples such as Scythians, Sarmatians, and Khazars, and with Greek colonies on the Black Sea.

The traditional foundation date is 482, though archaeology points to the 6th or 7th century. The Primary Chronicle says Kiev was founded by the Polyanian brothers Kyi, Shchek, and Khoryv, with the city named for Kyi. The story is legendary, but Byzantine, German, and Arab sources confirm Kiev's early importance.

Kievan Rus began after Varangians seized the city in the mid-9th century. Kiev's river position, defensive high bank, and trade routes helped it become the first Rus capital. Its power later declined after political fragmentation, invasion, and changing trade patterns.

Modern Ukrainian politics made Kiev central again during World War I. In 1918, the Central Rada proclaimed an independent Ukrainian state with Kiev as capital. The city then changed hands among Ukrainian nationalist, Soviet, White, Polish, and Red forces before Soviet rule was secured.

The Soviet period first made Kharkiv the Ukrainian capital, because Kiev was linked with Ukrainian nationalism. Kiev regained capital status in 1934. Soviet Five-Year Plans brought new machine-tool, electrical, and chemical industries, and the population reached 846,724 by 1939.

[[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II)) brought catastrophe. German forces entered Kiev on September 19, 1941, after an 80-day battle. Nearly 34,000 Jews were massacred at Baby Yar within days, and many more Jews, Roma, and Ukrainians were killed there over the next two years. Soviet troops retook Kiev on November 6, 1943.

Postwar Kiev rebuilt rapidly after losing more than 40 percent of its buildings and about 800 industrial enterprises. The Soviet government honoured the city with the Order of Lenin, Hero-City title, and Gold Star medal. After 1991, independent Ukraine returned Kiev to the world political stage.
