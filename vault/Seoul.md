## Korean Soul, formally Soul-t'ukpyolsi (Special City of Seoul)

![[attachments/seoul_001.webp|420]]

*Seoul skyline viewed from the Han River*

Seoul is the capital and largest city of South Korea. It lies on the Han River in the northwestern part of the country, about 37 miles, or 60 km, inland from the Yellow Sea. It is South Korea's cultural, economic, and political centre.

Except for a short break from 1399 to 1405, Seoul was Korea's capital from 1394 until the formal division of the country in 1948. The name Seoul itself means capital in Korean. Earlier names included Hanyang, Hansong, and Kyongsong, and Seoul became the official name after South Korea was founded in 1948.

## Landscape

Seoul occupies a strategic site on the Han River. The area has been inhabited for thousands of years and gained importance because the river linked inland routes with the Yellow Sea. Yi Song-gye, founder of the Choson dynasty, made it the capital of a unified kingdom in 1394.

The original city lay north of the Han in a topographic basin surrounded by low hills. Its defensive, central, river-linked position also suited p'ungsujirisol, Korean geomancy. In 1396, an 11-mile wall strengthened the basin's defenses, and remnants remain attractions.

## South and North City

The old city north of the Han remained Seoul's main area until the early 20th century. Later expansion crossed the river. Kangnam, or Gangnam, means South River and refers to the affluent South City, while Kangpuk, or Gangbuk, means North River and refers to the older North City.

Gangnam contains about half of Seoul's population and local tax income. Its high-rise apartments, offices, Teheran Street, tourism, design, fashion, information technology, and new industries made it a second business centre. A 1970s greenbelt pushed later growth into suburbs and satellite cities.

## Climate

Seoul has a continental climate ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate)) with a large annual temperature range. January averages about 26 F, or -3 C, while August averages about 78 F, or 25 C. Annual precipitation is about 54 inches, or 1,370 mm, concentrated in summer.

Air pollution became a serious urban problem, especially in the basin and industrial Yongdung-p'o. Han River pollution declined from the early 1980s through water controls and large sewage-treatment facilities.

## City Layout and Housing

Central Seoul north of the river follows a rectangular grid tied to the old wall's gates: Bukdaemun, Tongdaemun, Namdaemun, and Sodaemun. Main streets such as Euljiro and Jongno run east-west, while radiating and ring roads organize outer districts. Housing shortages led to many Han-side apartment blocks, though old hanok survive in some areas.

## People

Seoul's population was about 100,000 when it became the Choson capital, doubled by the 17th century, and reached about 900,000 by the end of World War II. Refugees from the north and Koreans returning from abroad after 1945 heavily increased the population.

By 1960, about 2.5 million people lived in Seoul, and rapid urbanization pushed the population to about 10 million by 1990. Growth later shifted to new towns and satellite cities. The population is overwhelmingly Korean, with a small foreign minority.

## Manufacturing and Services

Manufacturing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing)) remains a major employer, though information technology and electronics replaced older textile, clothing, machinery, and chemical industries. Food processing, beverages, publishing, and printing also matter.

The service sector ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector)) employs the largest share of workers. Seoul is South Korea's centre of finance, insurance, headquarters, trade shows, stock exchanges, and banks. Tongdaemun and Namdaemun remain major traditional markets, while Gangnam has large department stores and shopping centres.

## Transportation

![[attachments/seoul_003.webp|420]]

*Street scene in Seoul*

Seoul's [[Transportation|transportation]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)) system improved greatly after the Korean War. About two dozen bridges cross the Han, and highways run east-west along the river, north-south around the centre, and in rings to suburbs and satellite cities. Traffic congestion remains severe.

The subway, buses, and railways are main public transport forms. The demilitarized zone at the Han River mouth ended Seoul's role as a river port, so goods move mainly by rail and highway. Gimpo Airport was joined by Incheon International Airport in 2001.

## Government and Municipal Services

The Seoul Metropolitan Government is the executive branch, and the Seoul Metropolitan Council is the legislative branch. Administration has three levels: city, gu districts, and dong neighbourhoods. Mayors of the metropolitan government and gu serve four-year terms.

Municipal services include extensive water, sewage, hospitals, clinics, pharmacists, and drugstores. Traditional herbal medicine also exists alongside Western medical practice.

## Education and Cultural Life

![[attachments/seoul_002.webp|420]]

*Gyeonghoeru pavilion at Gyeongbokgung Palace in Seoul*

Compulsory [[Education|education]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education)) legally covers six years of elementary school, but most children continue to secondary school. About half of South Korea's major universities and research institutes are in Seoul, including Seoul National University, Yonsei University, Sookmyung Women's University, and Ewha Womans University.

Seoul is the country's cultural centre, with academies, learned societies, libraries, traditional music institutions, symphony orchestras, theatres, opera, museums, the Sejong Center, and the National Museum of Korea. Historic sites include Changgyeong, Gyeongbok, Deoksu, Changdeok, and Jongmyo; the last two became UNESCO World Heritage sites in 1995 and 1997.

## History

Human settlement along the Han dates to about 4000 BCE. During the Three Kingdoms period, the area was contested by Silla, Koguryo, and Paekche. The original Paekche capital, Wiryeseong, was in present-day Seoul, and King Munjong of Koryo built a summer palace there in 1068 CE.

After Yi Song-gye founded Choson, Seoul became capital in 1394. Gyeongbok and Changdeok palaces served Choson kings. Seoul remained the centre of the Hermit Kingdom until Korea opened to Western contacts in 1876, and Japanese rule, division, war, industrialization, and urbanization later made it a global metropolis.
