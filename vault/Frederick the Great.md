# Frederick the Great

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Frederick II of Prussia was an eighteenth-century ruler whose military command, diplomacy, territorial expansion, and administrative rule made Prussia a leading European state. His cultivation of French culture and identification with enlightened absolutism complicate his image as a purely military monarch.

## Introduction

Frederick II, known as Frederick the Great, ruled Prussia from 1740 to 1786. He is remembered as a brilliant military commander, a skilled diplomat, and an enlightened absolute monarch. Under his rule, Prussia became one of the strongest states in Europe, and its army became a model admired by other powers. Frederick also promoted French culture and art, and he built the palace of Sanssouci near Berlin. His reign is often seen as a key example of enlightened despotism in the 18th century.

## Early life and character

Frederick was born in 1712 in Berlin, the eldest surviving son of Frederick William I. He was educated in a strictly disciplined environment, but he showed little interest in the military tradition expected of him. He preferred music, literature, and philosophy, especially French culture. Although he was trained for kingship, he did not always fit the harsh, Prussian model of his father.

His early life also shaped his later political style. He was ambitious, intelligent, and deeply aware of the importance of prestige and power. These qualities helped him become one of the most successful rulers of his age.

Frederick’s reign was marked by a series of wars and diplomatic maneuvers. He expanded Prussia’s territory and strengthened its military power, especially against Austria and other European rivals. His campaigns helped establish Prussia as a leading military force, and his army became one of the most respected in Europe.

At the same time, Frederick was not merely a soldier. He also used diplomacy to gain advantage and to secure Prussia’s position among the great powers. His policies made him one of the dominant statesmen of the age.

## Enlightened rule

Frederick is often described as an enlightened ruler because he supported religious toleration, promoted administrative reform, and valued education and culture. He believed that the state should come before personal or dynastic interests, and he expected government to serve the power and stability of the nation. His court also reflected the cultural influence of French civilization, which he admired deeply.

However, his rule had limits. He relied heavily on the landed nobility, especially the Junker class, and this restricted the scope of reform. Even so, his example helped spread the idea that a monarch could combine strong authority with enlightened policies.

## Legacy

Frederick the Great left a lasting mark on European history. He transformed Prussia into a major European power and helped define the political culture of the 18th century. His reign combined military brilliance, political calculation, and cultural refinement. Although his achievements were not always as ideal as they appeared, his place in German and European history remains central.
