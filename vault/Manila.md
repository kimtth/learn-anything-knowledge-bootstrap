# Manila

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Manila is the Philippine capital on Luzon, where the Pasig River meets Manila Bay. As the core of the National Capital Region, its metropolitan growth joins colonial history, government, finance, manufacturing, housing, transport, and the cultural life of the Philippines.

## Introduction

![[attachments/manila_001.webp|420]]

*View of Intramuros and the Manila port area*

Manila is the Philippine capital and chief city, on Luzon beside Manila Bay at the mouth of the Pasig River. Its name comes from Maynilad ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maynilad_Water_Services)), the nilad marsh plant; in 1975, Manila and neighboring cities and municipalities became Metropolitan Manila, or the National Capital Region.

## Landscape

![[attachments/manila_002.webp|420]]

*Manila Bay and the Manila skyline*

Manila stands on the low deltaic plain of the Pasig River, with Manila Bay west, Laguna de Bay southeast, Sierra Madre uplands east, and the Bataan Peninsula sheltering its harbour.

## Climate

The city has a tropical climate ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate)), with a wet season from June to November, a dry season from December to May, and about 80 inches of annual rain. Its 17 districts grew from Intramuros and outside villages, with commerce north of the river and South Harbor as the main international port.

## Metropolitan Manila

San Juan, Las Pinas, Malabon, Navotas, Pasig, Pateros, Paranaque, Marikina, and other municipalities joined Manila, Caloocan City, Quezon City, and Pasay City in Metropolitan Manila to coordinate urban services and planning.

## Housing

Manila has a chronic housing shortage; tenements, resettlement projects, and the 1970s to 1980s BLISS effort tried to house poor residents, while barong-barong shacks remained common in poor districts.

## Architecture

Manila architecture reflects American, Spanish, Chinese, and Malay influences. Daniel H. Burnham designed Rizal Park and government buildings, while Manila Cathedral, rebuilt in the 1950s, replaced five earlier cathedrals.

## People

Metropolitan Manila is densely populated because of rural-urban migration. Most residents are Filipinos; the largest foreign community is Chinese, and religious life is mainly Roman Catholic, with Protestant, Muslim, Buddhist, Iglesia ni Kristo, and Aglipayan minorities.

## Manufacturing

Manila manufactures textiles, printed materials, food, tobacco, chemicals, lumber products, rope, soap, and other goods. It is also the Philippines' trade and finance center, and most imports and exports pass through its port.

## Banking and Finance

The Philippine Veterans Bank, Government Service Insurance System, Social Security System, major public and private banks, insurers, and the Philippine Stock Exchange help mobilize savings and investment.

## Transportation

Metropolitan Manila relies on buses, jeepneys, taxis, railways, light rail, airlines, and shipping, but traffic congestion is severe. The first Light Rail Transit phase opened in 1984, while South Harbor and North Harbor handle international and interisland traffic.

## Government

Metropolitan Manila is administered by the Metropolitan Manila Development Authority and local municipal assemblies. Water, sanitation, garbage collection, health centers, hospitals, police, fire services, and barangay volunteers support municipal services.

## Education

Nearly all residents over age 10 are literate, and Manila has more than 100 free public schools. Major institutions include the University of the Philippines, University of Santo Tomas, Ateneo de Manila, and other colleges, while cultural life centers on the Philippine Cultural Center, National Library, National Museum, Rizal Park, Fort Santiago, and city museums.

## History

![[attachments/manila_003.webp|420]]

*Fort Santiago in Intramuros, Manila*

In the late 16th century, Manila was a walled Muslim settlement controlling Pasig River trade. In 1571, Miguel Lopez de Legazpi founded Spanish Intramuros; Andres de Urdaneta and other friars made the city a Catholic center, and Manila received the title Distinguished and Ever Loyal City in 1574.

Manila endured Chinese attack in 1574, Dutch raids, British occupation in 1762, opening to foreign trade in 1832, and commercial acceleration after the 1869 Suez Canal opening. Jose Rizal's execution in 1896 sparked revolt, and U.S. forces took Manila after the 1898 Battle of Manila Bay.

Under U.S. rule, Manila gained public schools, the University of the Philippines in 1908, and stronger trade and tourism. Japan occupied the open city in 1942, U.S. recapture destroyed it in 1945, independence made it capital in 1946, Quezon City replaced it in 1948, and Manila regained permanent capital status in 1976.
