# The Study of History (History, Philosophy of)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Philosophy of history asks both whether historical change has direction, pattern, purpose, or law and how historians can know, explain, narrate, and judge the past. The source contrasts speculative schemes of providence or progress with critical inquiry into evidence, causation, interpretation, and historical explanation.

## Introduction

![[attachments/history_philosophy_001.webp|420]]

*Bust of Giambattista Vico*

Philosophy of history ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_history)) studies either the historical process itself or the methods by which historians understand the past. The word history can mean actual past events or the accounts and investigations that describe those events.

This creates two main kinds of inquiry. Speculative philosophy of history asks whether the whole course of history has a direction, pattern, purpose, or law. Critical philosophy of history asks how historical knowledge, explanation, evidence, causation, narrative, and interpretation work.

## Speculative Philosophy of History

Speculative theories begin from the belief that history may have an order or design. Religious traditions often saw history as providence, judgment, salvation, decline, or fulfillment. Later secular thinkers searched for progress, reason, freedom, social laws, or stages of development.

Such theories can be powerful because they connect many events into one large pattern. They can also be dangerous because they may force history into a scheme and ignore evidence that does not fit.

## Science, Law, and Criticism

Modern speculative theories were often influenced by science ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science)). Some thinkers hoped that historical and social life could be explained by laws like those of nature. This helped challenge vague older explanations and pushed historians to think about method.

Critics objected that many grand theories were not really scientific. They used broad claims that were hard to test, relied on hidden teleology, and treated history as if it must move toward a predetermined end.

Karl Popper strongly attacked historicism, the belief that history has discoverable laws that allow large-scale prediction. For him, such theories often confused prophecy with knowledge and could support dangerous political dogmas.

## Vico and Herder

![[attachments/history_philosophy_002.webp|420]]

*Portrait of Johann Gottfried Herder*

Giambattista Vico and Johann Gottfried Herder were speculative thinkers who also helped establish history as a distinctive discipline. They did not simply copy natural science as a model.

Vico argued that humans can know the world of nations because humans made it. He stressed myth, language, law, custom, imagination, and the poetic mentality of early peoples. This made history a form of knowledge different from physics.

Herder emphasized culture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture)), language, peoplehood, and historical individuality. He resisted judging all ages by one rational standard and insisted that each culture must be understood from within its own forms of life.

## Hegel and Historical Development

![[attachments/history_philosophy_003.webp|420]]

*Portrait of Hegel*

[[Hegel and Hegelianism (Hegel)|Hegel]] offered one of the most influential philosophies of history. He saw history as the development of Spirit and freedom through conflict, self-overcoming, contradiction, and the creation of rational social order.

For [[Hegel and Hegelianism (Hegel)|Hegel]], history did not move smoothly. Each society realized certain possibilities, then discovered its own limits. Its institutions became constraints, producing struggle and a new stage of development.

This dialectical view deeply influenced later thought, especially Marxism. Yet Hegel was criticized for treating history as too orderly, too Eurocentric, and too close to the political arrangements of his own time.

## Critical and Analytical Philosophy

![[attachments/history_philosophy_004.webp|420]]

*Portrait of Karl Popper*

Critical philosophy of history became prominent in the 20th century. It shifted attention from the destiny of history to the logic of historical inquiry.

Analytical philosophers asked how historians explain events, whether they use laws, how causes differ from reasons, how agents can be responsible, what counts as evidence, and how narrative gives shape to the past.

R.G. Collingwood argued that historical understanding involves rethinking past thought. Others, such as William Dray, examined whether historical explanations must fit covering laws or whether they can explain actions by showing purposes, contexts, and reasons.

## Explanation, Narrative, and Judgment

Historical explanation is difficult because human events involve action, intention, accident, structure, language, institutions, and interpretation. A battle, revolution, law, or migration can be described at many levels.

Narrative is not merely decoration. It selects beginnings, endings, turning points, agents, and connections. Philosophers therefore ask whether narrative discovers order in the past or partly constructs that order.

The philosophy of history also raises normative questions. Historians may ask whether established methods are adequate, whether evidence is being used fairly, and whether historical writing can avoid ideology, bias, or moral evasion.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1725: Vico's New Science argued that history has its own kind of knowledge.
- 18th century: Herder emphasized culture, language, and historical individuality.
- 19th century: [[Hegel and Hegelianism (Hegel)|Hegel]] and later Marxist thinkers developed powerful theories of historical development.
- 20th century: analytical philosophers focused on explanation, evidence, narrative, causation, and the logic of historical inquiry.

## Key People

- Vico: treated human history as a made world that humans can understand from within.
- Herder ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herding)): stressed culture, language, and the individuality of peoples and ages.
- [[Hegel and Hegelianism (Hegel)|Hegel]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Wilhelm_Friedrich_Hegel)): interpreted history as the dialectical development of freedom and Spirit.
- Popper: criticized historicism and large-scale historical prediction.
- Collingwood and Dray: major figures in critical and analytical philosophy of history.

## Interesting Facts

- History can mean both what happened and what historians write, which is why philosophy of history has two different tasks.
- Speculative theories often inspire broad understanding but risk turning history into prophecy.
- Analytical philosophy made ordinary historical explanation, narrative, and evidence into philosophical problems.
