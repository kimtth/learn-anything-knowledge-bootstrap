## Introduction

Thomas Alva Edison (born February 11, 1847, in Milan, Ohio; died October 18, 1931, in West Orange, New Jersey) was the American inventor who, alone or jointly, held a world-record 1,093 patents and created the world's first industrial research laboratory. The quintessential inventor of the age of Yankee ingenuity, he began his career in 1863 in the young telegraph industry and went on to play a central part in introducing the modern age of [[Electricity and Magnetism (Electricity)|electricity]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity)). From his workshops came the phonograph, the carbon-button telephone transmitter, the incandescent lamp, the first commercial electric light and power system, and key parts of motion-picture apparatus. An early loss of hearing, probably from mastoiditis, shaped both his character and his work.

## Early Career and Telegraphy

![[attachments/edison_002.webp|420]]

*Thomas Edison2*

The last surviving child of Samuel Edison, Jr. and Nancy Elliot Edison, Edison had little formal schooling and became an avid self-taught reader. In 1859 he left school to work as a trainboy on the railroad, and in 1863 he became an apprentice telegrapher; because early Morse messages were read as dots and dashes on paper, his deafness was no handicap. As an itinerant operator he poured his energy into improving the equipment, and by 1869 he had developed a duplex telegraph ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_telegraph)) and a printer, after which he gave up telegraphy for full-time invention. He moved to [[New York City]], partnered briefly with Frank L. Pope, and from 1870 worked in Newark, New Jersey, amid the fiercely competitive telegraph trade dominated by Western Union.

## Menlo Park

A sharp bargainer but a poor money manager, Edison built a laboratory and machine shop at Menlo Park, New Jersey, in 1876, joined by his key associates Charles Batchelor and John Kruesi. There he found that the electrical resistance of carbon varies with pressure, and by the end of 1877 he had produced the carbon-button transmitter still used in telephone speakers and microphones. The same year, through a stroke of serendipity, he achieved his most original invention, the phonograph.

## The Incandescent Light

![[attachments/edison_003.webp|420]]

*Light bulb Edison 2*

Edison boldly promised a safe, cheap electric light to replace gas, and a syndicate of financiers including J.P. Morgan and the Vanderbilts formed the Edison Electric Light Company and advanced him $30,000. Rejecting the series wiring of arc lights, he connected lamps in a parallel circuit using a high-resistance bulb, with mathematical help from Francis Upton, and supervised the world's first permanent commercial central power system in lower Manhattan, operating in September 1882. A side discovery, the Edison effect patented in 1883, anticipated the electron and later became the basis of the electron tube and the electronics industry.

## Motion Pictures and Later Work

![[attachments/edison_001.webp|420]]

*Edison and phonograph edit2*

Seeking to pair pictures with the phonograph, Edison assigned William K.L. Dickson to the task in 1888, and together they built the Kinetograph camera and the Kinetoscope viewer; unable to synchronize sound, they helped create the silent movie. Edison built the first motion-picture stage, the Black Maria, in 1893, later marketing the screen projector as the Vitascope. Another offshoot of the phonograph was the alkaline storage battery.

## Assessment

The direction of Edison's work shows in the clustering of his patents: 389 for electric light and power, 195 for the phonograph, 150 for the telegraph, 141 for storage batteries, and 34 for the telephone. He embodied the ideal of applied research, always inventing something he could manufacture and deriving principles from practical experiment rather than the other way round. Far from the lone genius, he ran an organized inventive establishment, and his rags-to-riches career made him a folk hero, even as his egotism and neglect of his family marked his private life.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1847: Thomas Alva Edison is born in Milan, Ohio.
- 1863: He becomes an apprentice telegrapher.
- 1877: He invents the phonograph and the carbon-button transmitter.
- 1882: His Manhattan central power system begins commercial operation.
- 1893: He builds the first motion-picture studio, the Black Maria.

## Interesting Facts

- Edison held or shared 1,093 patents, a world record.
