# Osaka-Kobe Metropolitan Area

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> The Osaka-Kobe metropolitan area is a major Japanese urban and port region shaped by its constrained coastal site, commercial and industrial development, transportation networks, education, and cultural institutions. Its history connects the two cities' modern metropolitan growth with Japan's wider urban and economic transformation.

## Introduction

![[attachments/osaka_kobe_002.webp|420]]

*Kobe Port Tower and Maritime Museum*

Osaka-Kobe metropolitan area is Japan's second largest urban and industrial agglomeration, located on Osaka Bay in west-central Honshu at the eastern end of the Inland Sea. Osaka and Kobe form the core of the Hanshin Industrial Zone, now part of the larger Keihanshin Industrial Zone extending toward Kyoto. The region corresponds broadly to Kansai, one of Japan's traditional cultural areas and the birthplace of the earliest Japanese state.

## Landscape and City Site

Osaka ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka)) lies on the delta of the Yodo River, while the wider metropolitan area spreads across the deltas of the Yodo, Yamato, and other rivers into uplands. It is bounded by the Ikoma Mountains, Izumi Mountains, Rokko Mountains, Awaji Island, and Osaka Bay. Osaka Castle, originally built by Toyotomi Hideyoshi, stands on an upland extension east of the central city.

## Climate and Environment

The region has a temperate climate ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate)), with annual mean temperature near 60 F and rainfall averaging 54 inches. Summers are hot and humid, winters cool with occasional snow, and rainy seasons occur in June-July and September-October. Typhoons and landslides have caused major disasters, including the 1934 typhoon and 1938 Rokko landslides. Air pollution improved after the mid-1970s, while water pollution and land subsidence remained concerns.

## Urban Layout

Central Osaka follows a grid plan, while the broader city combines planned grids with irregular streets. Mido-suji connects Osaka station and Namba, while Chuo Odori runs from the Port of Osaka toward the Ikoma Mountains. Shinsaibashi-suji is the main shopping district, Dotombori is known for theatres and restaurants, and Nakanoshima contains government, finance, media, and cultural institutions.

## Industry and Commerce

Osaka was once called the Manchester of the Orient because of textiles, but its leading industries became electrical and other machinery, iron and steel, fabricated metals, and chemicals. Amagasaki, Kobe, and other nearby cities specialize in machinery, metallurgy, shipbuilding, steel, and port-related industry. Osaka remains a major commercial centre, holding about one-seventh of Japan's wholesale trade and serving with Kobe as a leading foreign-trade port.

## Transportation

![[attachments/osaka_kobe_metropolitan_area_001.webp|420]]

*Akashi Kaikyo Bridge linking Kobe and Awaji Island*

Osaka ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka)) is a key national railway junction linking Tokyo with western and southern Japan. The region is served by Shinkansen trains, JR West, private railroads, subways, freight lines, expressways, ports, ferries, and airports. Kansai International Airport opened on a man-made island in Osaka Bay in 1994. The Akashi Kaikyo Bridge, completed in 1998, linked Kobe and Awaji Island and was then the world's longest suspension bridge.

## Government and Services

Osaka is the capital of Osaka urban prefecture, and Kobe is the capital of Hyogo prefecture. Osaka also functions as the centre of the Kinki region. Both cities are divided into wards with appointed ward mayors and elected city councils. Osaka's water comes mainly from the Yodo River, while Kobe uses reservoirs on the Rokko slopes. Hospitals, utilities, sewage services, and higher education institutions are distributed across the area.

## Education and Culture

The region includes more than 100 universities and junior colleges, including Osaka University, Kobe University, Osaka City University, Kansai University, and Kwansei Gakuin University. Osaka has a bustling democratic cultural tone and is famous for cuisine. Traditional and modern Japanese drama, Western music, opera, museums, libraries, parks, baseball, Takarazuka entertainment, Expo Memorial Park, and Kaiyukan Aquarium enrich metropolitan life.

## History

The Osaka plain was settled in Paleolithic times and became a political centre by about AD 300. Ancient Naniwa served intermittently as a palace or capital site before Nara became the permanent capital in 710. Sakai emerged as a wealthy medieval port and arts centre. Rennyo founded Ishiyama Hongan Temple in 1496; after Oda Nobunaga destroyed it, Toyotomi Hideyoshi built Osaka Castle and transformed the city.

## Key Events & Developments

- AD 300: Osaka plain had become a political centre.
- 710: Nara replaced Naniwa as permanent capital.
- 1496: Rennyo selected the Ishiyama Hongan Temple site.
- 1934: A major typhoon killed about 3,000 people.
- 1970: Expo 70 was held near Senri New Town.
- 1994: Kansai International Airport opened.
- 1998: Akashi Kaikyo Bridge linked Kobe and Awaji Island.

## Key People

- Oda Nobunaga ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oda_Nobunaga)): Defeated Sakai and destroyed Ishiyama Hongan Temple.
- Rennyo ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rennyo)): Founded the fortress temple that became an Osaka nucleus.
