## Life and Importance

![[attachments/planck_001.webp|420]]

*Portrait of Max Planck*

Max Planck, born Max Karl Ernst Ludwig Planck and died October 4, 1947, in Gottingen, West Germany, was a theoretical physicist who originated quantum theory. He received the Nobel Prize for Physics in 1918. His quantum theory and Albert Einstein's relativity became the two fundamental theories of 20th-century physics.

Planck was the sixth child of a Kiel jurist. Scholarship, duty, reliability, and music shaped him; he had absolute pitch and played piano daily.

## Education and Academic Career

Planck entered the University of Munich ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LMU_Munich)) in 1874, became associate professor at Kiel, moved to Berlin in 1889, and became full professor in 1892.

## Thermodynamics and Blackbody Radiation

Planck was deeply impressed by conservation of energy and entropy. The second law shaped his doctorate and quantum discovery. Kirchhoff's blackbody definition and experiments by Otto Lummer, Ernst Pringsheim, Heinrich Rubens, and Ferdinand Kurlbaum framed the radiation problem.

## Planck's Radiation Law

By 1900, experiments showed that Wien's law worked at high frequencies but failed at low frequencies. Planck combined high- and low-frequency expressions into Planck's radiation law, then sought a first-principles derivation.

## Quantum of Action

On December 14, 1900, Planck derived his formula by adopting Ludwig Boltzmann's statistical entropy and assuming oscillators absorbed and emitted energy only in discrete quanta. Each quantum had energy proportional to frequency through h, later Planck's constant ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_constant)).

## Quantum Revolution

Planck's constant ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_constant)) being small but finite meant atomic-scale processes were not classical. Planck was a reluctant revolutionary. [[Einstein]] later showed in 1905 that radiant energy itself could behave quantum mechanically.

## Berlin and Later Views

Planck helped bring [[Einstein]] to Berlin in 1914 and supported Max von Laue and Erwin Schrodinger. He opposed the indeterministic worldview of Niels Bohr, Max Born, and Werner Heisenberg.

## Public Authority and Moral Position

Planck served the Prussian Academy and Kaiser Wilhelm Society. His authority rested on integrity. He appealed to Adolf Hitler against racial policies but could not save German science.

## Core Equations

Physical models often begin by relating force, energy, waves, and conserved quantities:

$$\vec{F}=m\vec{a}$$

This is Newton's second law: force is the product of mass and acceleration, so a larger mass or a faster change in motion requires a larger net force.

$$E=h\nu$$

This relation says that photon energy is proportional to frequency; higher-frequency radiation carries more energy per photon.

$$E=mc^2$$

This expresses mass-energy equivalence: mass is a form of energy, with the speed of light squared as the conversion factor.

## Interesting Facts

- Planck ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Planck)) chose physics even though music remained central to his life.
- Planck's constant ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck_constant)) showed that the microphysical world is not classical.
- He opposed the observer-centred implications of later quantum mechanics.
