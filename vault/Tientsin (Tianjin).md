## Introduction

Tianjin ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianjin)), formerly Tientsin, is a province-level municipality in northern China. It lies east of Hebei at the northeastern edge of the North China Plain and is under direct control of China's State Council.

The city is North China's leading port and a major manufacturing centre. It stands about 120 km southeast of Beijing and about 35 miles inland from the Bo Hai, with Tanggu serving as its outport.

Tianjin means Heavenly Ford. Since the Yuan dynasty it has been a transport, trading, and storage centre, and its role as Beijing's commercial gateway made it cosmopolitan long before European merchants arrived.

## Landscape and Climate

![[attachments/tientsin_tianjin_001.webp|420]]

*Tianjin Eye and Tianjin*

Central Tianjin lies where the Ziya and Yongding rivers and sections of the Grand Canal meet before forming the Hai River, which flows to the Bo Hai.

The city is built on a very low alluvial plain, much of it below 12 feet above sea level. This location made Tianjin useful for transport but vulnerable to flooding and drainage problems.

Despite its closeness to the sea, Tianjin has a continental climate. Winters are cool, dry, and influenced by Siberian air; summers are hot, humid, and rainy under Pacific air masses.

## Water and Drainage

![[attachments/tientsin_tianjin_002.webp|420]]

*Hai River in Tianjin*

The Hai River was long prone to flooding and silting. Its water could be too high in spring and summer and too low for navigation in winter.

Water control began in the late 19th century through straightening, dredging, locks, canals, and settling basins. Since 1949, reservoirs, diversion channels, flood-control works, irrigation, and navigation projects have continued.

Fresh water has been a recurring problem because the city is low, coastal, industrial, and populous. Water has been diverted from the Huang He and Luan River to meet shortages.

## City Layout and Economy

Heping is the central commercial and financial district on the west bank of the Hai River. The old Chinese city lies northwest of it, with winding streets inside the line of the former rectangular wall.

Since 1949 Tianjin has developed heavy industry: machinery, chemicals, iron and steel, shipbuilding, mining equipment, machine tools, electrical goods, trucks, tractors, bicycles, watches, and precision instruments.

Chemical complexes at Dagu, Tanggu, and Hangu produce fertilizers, pesticides, pharmaceuticals, petrochemicals, plastics, fibres, dyestuffs, and paints. Textiles remain the chief light industry.

Economic reforms after the 1980s attracted foreign investment. Tianjin's port handles exports and imports, especially heavy machinery and capital-intensive goods.

## Transportation

Tianjin is North China's major transport centre. Railways connect it with Beijing, Shanghai, Shenyang, Shandong, Jiangsu, and the Northeast.

Inland waterways use the Grand Canal, Ziya River, and Daqing River connections. Expressways and roads link the city to Beijing, Tanggu, Shandong, Shanxi, Qinhuangdao, and other regional centres.

Urban transport includes buses, trolleybuses, light rail, and subway lines. Tianjin's port and transport networks make it a major collection and transshipment point for North China.

## Government and Public Services

The Tianjin People's Congress is the chief administrative body. Planning bodies oversee industry, commerce, raw materials, production, investment, manpower, public works, transport, and environmental policy.

Public works after 1949 improved flood control, water supply, sewage disposal, roads, drainage, parks, and electricity. Low marshlands were drained and some became recreation areas.

Tianjin has Western-style and Chinese hospitals, universities, technical institutions, museums, parks, libraries, theatres, cinemas, stadiums, and cultural halls.

## History

![[attachments/tientsin_tianjin_003.webp|420]]

*Historic European-style architecture in Tianjin*

The area was sparsely settled until the Song dynasty. Sanchakou and later Zhigu grew near river junctions, becoming port, warehouse, grain-transfer, and salt-production centres.

Under the Yuan, offices for [[Navigation|navigation]] and customs were established. In the Ming, the settlement became Tianjinwei, a garrison guarding the gateway to Beijing.

By the Qing, Tianjin was North China's leading economic centre because it stood at the northern terminus of the Grand Canal. Trade increased as inland waterway links improved.

In the 19th century, foreign pressure made Tianjin an open trading port. British, French, Japanese, German, Russian, Austrian, Italian, and Belgian concessions reshaped parts of the city, often sharply separated from Chinese districts.

The city suffered during antiforeign violence, the 1900 Allied occupation, Japanese pressure, the Sino-Japanese War, and the Chinese Civil War. Communist forces captured Tianjin in January 1949.

## Key Events & Developments

- Yuan dynasty: Tianjin became an important navigation, customs, storage, and trading centre.
- 1404: the Ming made it Tianjinwei, a garrison town.
- 1860: the Convention of Beijing opened Tianjin to foreign trade.
- 1900: Allied forces occupied the city and destroyed the old wall.
- 1949: communist forces captured Tianjin.
