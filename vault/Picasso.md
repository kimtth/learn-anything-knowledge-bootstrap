# Picasso

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Pablo Picasso was a Spanish artist whose early Barcelona and Paris career led through Blue and Rose periods to Cubism, collage, neoclassicism, Surrealist experiments, sculpture, ceramics, and antiwar work including Guernica. His constant reinvention made him a defining figure in twentieth-century modern art.

## Life and Importance

![[attachments/picasso_001.webp|420]]

*Portrait of Pablo Picasso in 1908*

Pablo Picasso, in full Pablo Ruiz y Picasso, was a Spanish expatriate painter, sculptor, printmaker, ceramicist, and stage designer. He died on April 8, 1973, in Mougins, France. One of the most influential artists of the 20th century, he created Cubism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism)) with Georges Braque and devoted nearly 80 of his 91 years to art.

Picasso was the son of Jose Ruiz Blasco, a drawing professor, and Maria Picasso Lopez. His drawing ability appeared early, and he became his father's pupil around age 10 in La Coruna. After moving to Barcelona in 1895, he studied at La Llotja. His Science and Charity ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_Charity)) won honourable mention in Madrid in 1897, but he soon rejected academic training and family expectations.

## Barcelona and Paris

In 1898, illness led Picasso to Horta de Ebro, where he learned independence and began turning away from the Ruiz name toward Picasso ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Picasso)). In Barcelona he joined Catalan artists and writers at Els Quatre Gats and held his first Barcelona exhibition in 1900. That year he traveled to Paris with Carles Casagemas, seeking the artistic world of Montmartre.

## Blue and Rose Periods

The Blue Period, from 1901 to mid-1904, used blue tones and subjects of poverty, loneliness, blindness, maternity, and marginal life, influenced by Paris prisons and Barcelona street people. In 1904, Picasso moved permanently to Paris. The Rose Period brought warmer tones, circus performers, saltimbanques, Harlequins, and the artist's social isolation, shaped by friendship with Guillaume Apollinaire and his relationship with Fernande Olivier.

## Les Demoiselles d'Avignon

Toward the end of 1906, Picasso began work leading to Les Demoiselles d'Avignon ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Demoiselles_d%27Avignon)). Influenced by Iberian sculpture, African masks, El Greco, Cezanne, and Ingres, the painting fractured bodies and space and presented prostitutes as a direct challenge to inherited ideals of beauty. Picasso kept the shocking canvas out of sight for years.

## Cubism

From 1909 to 1912, Picasso and Georges Braque developed Analytical Cubism. They rejected Renaissance perspective and illusion, showing multiple views of objects on a single canvas. Landscapes, portraits, seated figures, musical instruments, grids, limited palettes, and fractured forms turned painting into an analysis of objects, space, light, shadow, and representation.

Cubism ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism)) did not seek total abstraction. Instead, Picasso and Braque combined abstract and representational elements and treated the canvas as real space. Newspaper lettering, oval formats, and shifting axes suggested that images could be read forward from the picture plane rather than backward into illusionistic depth.

## Collage and Synthetic Cubism

By 1912, Picasso and Braque began using papier colle and collage, gluing real paper and materials onto canvases. Synthetic Cubism reintroduced colour and industrial materials such as sand or printed wallpaper. Picasso's Absinthe Glass of 1914 mixed bronze sculpture, collage, painting, and real object, hovering between reality and illusion.

## Parade and Mediterraneanism

World War I dispersed Picasso's circle, but he stayed in France. Through Erik Satie and Jean Cocteau, he joined the Ballets Russes project Parade in 1917, designing sets and costumes and meeting Olga Kokhlova, his future wife. After Italy and Barcelona, Picasso's late-1910s work took on Mediterraneanism, classical forms, naturalistic drawing, Ingres-like line, and a clarified Cubism.

## Surrealism

Picasso never formally joined Surrealism, but Andre Breton and Surrealist writers claimed him as close to their aims. His art already contained metamorphosis, monsters, erotic dislocation, and one thing becoming another. In the 1920s and 1930s, contorted bathers, Crucifixion drawings, and the minotaur explored sexuality, violence, myth, and the struggle between human and bestial forces.

## Sculpture and the 1930s

Picasso's sculpture gained full recognition only after his death because he kept much of it in his own collection. Beginning in 1928, he worked in iron and sheet metal with Julio Gonzalez. At Boisgeloup, inspired by Marie-Therese Walter, he made large plaster heads and found-object constructions. In 1935, their daughter Maya was born; soon after, Picasso became involved with Dora Maar.

## Guernica and War

The Spanish Civil War deeply affected Picasso. Though he never returned to Spain after 1934, his sympathies were Republican. For the Spanish pavilion at the Paris World's Fair, he painted Guernica in 1937. With Dora Maar documenting the process, he completed the huge canvas in just over three weeks. Its wounded horse, fallen soldier, screaming mothers, dead children, and bull condemned fascist destruction and useless slaughter.

## World War II and After

During World War II, Picasso remained in France with Dora Maar and Jaime Sabartes. Guernica's expressive forms carried into Weeping Woman, portraits, prints, still lifes, and skull-like wartime drawings. After liberation, he exhibited again, joined the Communist Party, worked in lithography, ceramics, painting, and sculpture, and opened his studio to visitors, becoming a public and political symbol.

## Ceramics and Late Myth

At Vallauris, Picasso's ceramics turned plates, jugs, and vases into playful experiments between decoration and form, two and three dimensions, and personal and universal meanings. His dove became a peace symbol, and War and Peace expressed postwar optimism. In later decades he revisited older masters such as Delacroix and his own earlier themes, placing himself among acrobats, kings, artists, and mythic figures.

## Assessment

Picasso's radical art made him unavoidable for 20th-century artists. Unlike many contemporaries who remained within early styles, he kept innovating into old age. His late work was long misunderstood but later influenced younger painters. At his death, he owned about 50,000 works in many media, reinforcing estimates of his astonishing invention and execution.

## Key Events & Developments

- 1895: Picasso entered La Llotja in Barcelona.
- 1900: He visited Paris and exhibited at Els Quatre Gats.
- 1901-1904: Blue Period.
- 1904-1906: Rose Period.
- 1907: Les Demoiselles d'Avignon ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Demoiselles_d%27Avignon)) changed modern art.
- 1909-1912: Picasso and Braque developed Analytical Cubism.
- 1912-1914: Synthetic Cubism and collage emerged.
- 1937: Picasso painted Guernica.
- 1973: Picasso died in Mougins.
