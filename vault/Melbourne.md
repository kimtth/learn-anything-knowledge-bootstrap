# Melbourne

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Melbourne is the capital of Victoria and a major Australian metropolis at Port Phillip Bay. Its planned central grid, parks, nineteenth-century architecture, financial strength, arts, recreation, and expanding suburbs reflect its role in the history and culture of southeastern Australia.

## Introduction

![[attachments/melbourne_001.webp|420]]

*Melbourne skyline*

Melbourne is the capital of Victoria, Australia, at the head of Port Phillip Bay on the southeastern coast. The central city is small, but it anchors a large metropolitan area, second in Australian population only to [[Sydney]]. Its flat site encouraged a rectangular street pattern, while parks, 19th-century architecture, financial strength, and expanding eastern suburbs shaped its reputation.

## Metropolitan Melbourne

![[attachments/melbourne_003.webp|420]]

*Yarra River at Melbourne*

Metropolitan Melbourne lies at the northern end of Port Phillip Bay, 30 nautical miles from the bay entrance. Growth followed geology and drainage: flat basalt plains lie west of the original site, while wetter eastern sandstone and shale country supported denser vegetation and later stronger suburban development. By 2006 the statistical division held more than 3.5 million people.

## Climate

[[Climate and Weather (Climate)|Climate]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate)) is shaped by eastward-moving high-pressure cells and low-pressure troughs. Rainfall averages about 26 inches and is spread through the year, with October wettest and January driest. Temperatures are moderate, and winds help spare the city from severe air pollution. The central layout includes Parliament, cathedrals, museums, law courts, the State Library, financial institutions, and pedestrian malls on Bourke and Swanston streets.

## Transportation

![[attachments/melbourne_002.webp|420]]

*Melbourne tram*

Melbourne uses an integrated [[Transportation|transportation]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)) network of electric trains, buses, and its signature tramcars. National highways, upgraded freeways, the Western Ring Road, City Link, tunnels, bridges, interchanges, an underground rail loop, and Tullamarine airport connect the city internally and with other regions.

## Government

The [[Victoria and the Victorian Age (Victoria)|Victoria]] state government controls major planning, health, education, and transport decisions for Melbourne. More than 30 local governments handle ordinances, building regulation, welfare, garbage collection, and parking through elected councils funded by property rates. Free public hospitals began in 1846, while the University of Melbourne began in 1853, followed by Monash, La Trobe, and Deakin universities.

## Arts

Arts life expanded strongly between 1968 and 1984 with the Victorian Arts Centre on the Yarra River. It includes the National Gallery of Victoria, Melbourne Concert Hall, and theaters for opera, ballet, drama, comedy, cabaret, and music. The Melbourne Symphony Orchestra, formed in 1949, toured internationally and supported the city's concert culture.

## Recreation

Melbourne has extensive parks and reserves, with more than one-fourth of the inner city in public open space. The Royal Botanic Gardens, founded in 1846, cover 89 acres and connect to the National Herbarium of Victoria. Sport is also central: the Melbourne Cricket Ground holds 100,000, Flemington hosts the Melbourne Cup, and the city hosted the 1956 Summer Olympic Games.

## History

Europeans reached Port Phillip Bay in 1802 through visits by John Murray and Matthew Flinders. Charles Grimes explored the Yarra in 1803, and David Collins briefly settled near Sorrento before moving to Tasmania. Permanent settlement began in 1835, when John Batman made a treaty with Aboriginal elders and John Fawkner's party settled on the Yarra. The settlement was named Melbourne in 1837, became a town in 1842 and a city in 1847, then surged after gold discoveries near Bendigo and Ballarat in the early 1850s.

Railways, port improvements, tariffs, and manufacturing made Melbourne dominant in Victoria, but the financial crisis of 1889, depression, bank failures, and drought slowed growth. It served as Australia's federal capital until 1927, when Canberra replaced it. After 1945 European immigration and manufacturing expanded, while after 1971 slower growth, office redevelopment, improved roads, suburban economic centers, the Victorian Arts Centre, and Docklands changed the city again.
