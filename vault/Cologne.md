## Introduction

Cologne is the fourth largest city in [[Germany]] and the largest in North Rhine-Westphalia, a major inland port and the historic, cultural, and economic capital of the Rhineland ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland)). Its importance grew where the great traffic artery of the Rhine crossed a major east-west land route, and in the Middle Ages it became a centre of the church, art, and learning. Much of the Inner City was destroyed in World War II, yet that heritage remains visible, crowned by Cologne Cathedral ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Cathedral)), the largest Gothic church in northern Europe and a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1996.

## Geography and cityscape

![[attachments/cologne_001.webp|420]]

*Exterior view of Cologne Cathedral in Cologne, Germany*

The city lies about 65 metres above sea level where the Rhine enters the North German Plain, mostly on the left bank but with suburbs on the right. The semicircular Inner City was shaped by a defensive wall about 6 km long, completed around 1200, when Cologne was larger than Paris; the medieval fortifications were demolished in the 1880s and replaced by the ring roads called the Ringstrassen. Dominating the centre is the cathedral, whose twin towers rise 157 metres; begun in the Gothic style after a fire in 1248, it was left unfinished from 1560 until work resumed in 1842 and was completed in 1880. The city also preserves Roman remains, many Romanesque churches such as Sankt Gereon and Gross Sankt Martin, reconstructed civic buildings, and the modern Telecommunications Tower of 1981.

## Economy and society

Cologne has been a banking centre since the Middle Ages and holds one of the world's oldest stock exchanges; it is a hub of insurance, media, publishing, and broadcasting, the European headquarters of the Ford Motor Company, and a centre of engineering, chemicals, and the famous eau de cologne ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eau_de_Cologne)) first sold commercially in the early 18th century. Major trade fairs are held at the KölnMesse. About four-fifths of the people are of German nationality, mostly Roman Catholic with a large Protestant minority, alongside Turkish, Italian, and Balkan guest workers and Muslim and Jewish communities. The University of Cologne, founded in 1388 and refounded in 1919, anchors a rich cultural life of museums, music, theatre, and the great Rhenish carnival that climaxes on Rose Monday.

## History

![[attachments/cologne_002.webp|420]]

*Cologne - Panoramic Image of the old town at dusk*

The Roman general Agrippa settled the Ubii here about 38 BC, and the town became the birthplace of Agrippina the Younger, at whose request it was made a Roman colony in AD 50 as Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium. [[Constantine the Great]] built a castle and a permanent Rhine bridge in 310; the Franks took the city about 456, and it is first recorded as a bishopric in 313. [[Charlemagne]] raised it to an archbishopric in the late 8th century, and the archbishop came to dominate the city until the merchants defeated him at the Battle of Worringen in 1288, after which Cologne was in effect a free imperial city.

## Conclusion

Cologne is a city where Roman origins, medieval power, ecclesiastical heritage, and modern urban life remain visibly layered together.
