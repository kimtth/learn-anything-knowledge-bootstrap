# Philippines

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> The Philippines is a Southeast Asian archipelago whose Spanish and American colonial legacies, predominantly Catholic population, linguistic diversity, and island geography shape its society. Its resources and industrial potential coexist with poverty, agricultural dependence, and a modern history that includes Marcos rule and the People Power restoration.

## Introduction

Philippines ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines)) is an island country of Southeast Asia in the western Pacific, consisting of about 7,100 islands and islets. Manila is the capital, Quezon City the largest city, and both are part of Metro Manila on Luzon. Mindanao is the second largest island. The country takes its name from Philip II of Spain and combines Asian identity with long Spanish and American colonial legacies.

The Philippines is the only predominantly Roman Catholic ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church)) country in Southeast Asia and one of the most populous countries with English as an official language. After authoritarian rule under Ferdinand Marcos, the People Power uprising of 1986 restored democratic government. The country remains marked by paradoxes: rich resources and industrial potential alongside deep poverty and strong agricultural dependence.

## Land and Islands

![[attachments/philippines_001.webp|420]]

*Relief location map of the Philippine archipelago*

The archipelago is bounded by the Philippine Sea ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Sea)), Celebes Sea, Sulu Sea, and South China Sea. It stretches about 1,150 miles north to south. Its islands form three major groups: Luzon in the north and west, the Visayas in the centre, and Mindanao in the south. Only about two-fifths of the islands have names, and only about 350 are at least one square mile in area.

Its triangular spread places [[Taiwan]] to the north, Borneo to the south of Palawan, and Indonesia to the south and southeast of Mindanao, making the archipelago a maritime bridge between several Asian worlds.

## Relief and Volcanoes

![[attachments/philippines_002.webp|420]]

*Mayon Volcano erupting near Daraga Church in the Philippines*

The Philippines has an irregular coastline of about 22,550 miles, extensive mountains, narrow coastal plains, volcanic rocks, coral formations, lakes, and north-trending river systems. Luzon contains the Cordillera Central, Sierra Madre, Caraballo Mountains, Cagayan Valley, Zambales Mountains, central plain, and Mayon Volcano. The Visayas and Mindanao have longitudinal ranges, volcanoes, and earthquake-prone landscapes.

The Cordillera Central and Sierra Madre help frame the fertile Cagayan Valley, while southern Luzon is broken by isolated volcanoes and irregular uplands.

## Drainage and Soils

Major rivers include the Cagayan, Agno, Pampanga, Pasig, Bicol, Mindanao, and Agusan. Laguna de Bay is the largest lake, while Taal Lake sits in a volcanic crater. Puerto Princesa Subterranean River National Park, a World Heritage site, carries an underground river to the sea. Fertile alluvial soils support rice; volcanic and mineral-rich soils support fruit, coffee, bananas, cassava, sugarcane, timber, and other crops.

## Climate

The Philippines has a tropical monsoon climate. Southwest winds bring rain from about May to October, and northeast winds bring drier conditions from November to February. Wet and dry seasons vary by region; eastern coasts facing the Pacific receive heavy rainfall. Typhoons strike from June to December, especially in Samar, Leyte, south-central Luzon, and the Batan Islands, often causing floods, wind damage, and loss of life.

## People and Languages

Filipinos are ethnically diverse, with nearly 100 cultural and linguistic groups. Major groups include the Tagalog, Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Waray-Waray, Bicol, Pampango, and Filipino mestizos, along with smaller Indigenous and immigrant communities. Negrito peoples such as Aeta, Ita, and Agta are among the earliest inhabitants. Chinese, Indian, U.S., Spanish, and other communities add to the cultural mix.

The Philippines has about 150 languages and dialects, mostly Western Malayo-Polynesian Austronesian languages. Filipino, based on Tagalog, and English are official languages and media of instruction. Cebuano, Ilocano, Hiligaynon, Waray-Waray, Bicol varieties, Tausug, Pampango, Pangasinan, Maguindanao, and Maranao are widely used regionally.

## Religion and Settlement

About four-fifths of Filipinos are Roman Catholic ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church)). Other Christians include Aglipayans, Iglesia ni Kristo, Protestants, and independent churches. Islam arrived in the south in the 15th century and remains strong among Moro communities in Mindanao and the Sulu Archipelago. Settlement is dense in central plains, coastal areas, Cebu, Luzon, Panay, and major cities, while rural houses often reflect local agricultural and fishing economies.

## Economy and Agriculture

![[attachments/philippines_003.webp|420]]

*Banaue rice terraces in the mountains of the Philippines*

The Philippines has a free-enterprise economy with strong agricultural roots. Agriculture employs nearly one-third of the workforce but contributes a smaller share of GDP. Major products include sugarcane, rice, coconuts, bananas, corn, pineapples, mangoes, citrus, papayas, coffee, tobacco, abaca, maguey, and vegetables. Rice output improved after the 1970s through high-yield strains, roads, irrigation, fertilizers, and insecticides, though chemical use created soil problems.

## Manufacturing, Finance, Trade, and Services

Manufacturing grew in the 20th century and includes [[Electronics|electronics]], garments, textiles, processed foods, beverages, chemicals, and petroleum products. Finance is centred on the Central Bank, Land Bank, Development Bank, privatized Philippine National Bank, NEDA, and Board of Investments. Exports include electronic equipment, garments, coconuts, copper, gold, and iron ore; imports include machinery, fuels, chemicals, transport equipment, and food. Services are the largest economic sector, with tourism growing in Metro Manila, Cebu, Boracay, and other destinations.

## Labour, Justice, and Security

Trade unions, farmer groups, teachers, government employees, and women participate broadly in the workforce and labour politics. The 1987 constitution restored judicial independence after Marcos and created a Supreme Court with a chief justice and 14 associate justices. The military includes army, navy, air force, and marines; it handles external defense, disaster relief, public works, literacy campaigns, UN peacekeeping, and internal conflicts involving groups such as the NDF, MILF, and MNLF.

## Housing and Education

The Philippines faces a serious housing shortage, especially in Manila, where many people live in informal or substandard settlements. Government resettlement and mortgage programs have tried to improve conditions. Education is compulsory at elementary level, with Filipino used in most elementary subjects and English in science, mathematics, secondary, and tertiary education. The University of Santo Tomas, founded in 1611, is the oldest university; the University of the Philippines is the national university.

## Culture

Philippine culture ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture)) blends diversity and homogeneity, Southeast Asian roots, Spanish Catholic influence, American education and English, Indigenous traditions, and local regional identities. Architects include Juan F. Nakpil, Leandro Locsin, and Juan Arellano. Jose Rizal's nationalist novels were literary landmarks, while Nick Joaquin became a major modern writer. Myths, epics such as Darangen and Biag ni Lam-ang, films, music, and regional arts sustain cultural memory.

## Early History and Spanish Rule

Before colonization, the Philippines had no centralized state or dominant culture. Communities arrived in waves from Asia and maintained varied contacts, including recorded Chinese trade by 982 and limited influence from Srivijaya and Majapahit. Lowland people lived in barangays led by datus, with social ranks including nobles, freeholders, dependents, and captives. Spanish colonization brought Christianity, colonial rule, and integration into global trade.

## Revolution and American Rule

The Philippine Revolution erupted in 1896 after Spanish discovery of Katipunan plans. Emilio Aguinaldo led rebel forces, accepted exile after a truce, then returned after U.S. victory at Manila Bay in 1898. Independence was declared on June 12, 1898, and the Malolos Republic formed in 1899. U.S. refusal to recognize full independence led to American rule after Spain ceded the islands.

## World War II and Contemporary Politics

Japan attacked the Philippines on December 8, 1941. Manuel Quezon and Sergio Osmena evacuated, Filipino and American forces surrendered in 1942, and a Japanese-sponsored republic under Jose Laurel was created in 1943. After independence and later turmoil, Marcos imposed authoritarian rule, overthrown in 1986. Later presidents included Fidel Ramos, Joseph Estrada, Gloria Macapagal Arroyo, and Benigno Aquino III. Super Typhoon Haiyan devastated central islands in 2013.
