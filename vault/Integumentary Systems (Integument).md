# Integumentary Systems (Integument)

## Introduction

The integument is the covering network that delimits an organism, separates it from the environment, protects it from foreign matter, and permits communication with the outside world. In unicellular organisms it consists of the cell membrane and any secreted coating. In invertebrates it is usually an epithelial surface with secreted cuticles or coatings. In vertebrates it becomes the complex integumentary system, including skin and derived structures such as scales, feathers, hair, glands, claws, nails, hooves, horns, and antlers.

All integuments arise from epithelium, usually called epidermis in animals. A dermis beneath the epidermis supplies nourishment and support. Many invertebrates also secrete noncellular cuticles, while vertebrate skin lacks such external coatings but produces many epidermal and dermal appendages. Integuments are adaptive, ranging from armadillo armour and Arctic bear fur to cod scales, amphibian mucous skin, reptile leatheriness, bird feathers, and porpoise smoothness.

## Invertebrate Integuments

![[attachments/integumentary_systems_integument_001.png|420]]

*Integumentary system*

Unicellular organisms ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicellular_organism)) have membranes and secreted coatings. Bacteria commonly possess external cell walls that maintain shape, turgidity, and protection. Protozoan coverings range from soft amoeboid surfaces to proteinaceous or cellulose cuticles, calcium carbonate shells, siliceous coverings, and internal silica lattices in radiolarians.

Sponges and cnidarians show simple but functional epithelia. Sponge pinacoderm covers external surfaces and internal waterways, and some sponges deposit calcium carbonate spicules below it. Cnidarian epidermis may contain epithelial, contractile, gland, pigment, stinging, and sensory cells, with secretions used for food capture, adhesion, cleaning, support, and protection. Reef corals secrete calcium carbonate skeletons through epidermal tissue.

Worm integuments vary with lifestyle. Parasitic flukes have thick ridged integuments with spines, sensory papillae, microvilli, and renewing secretory bodies that protect against host immune reactions. Roundworms have thick flexible cuticles. Annelids have thin horny cuticles pierced by gland pores, mucus secretion, chaetae or bristles, and specialized clitellar epidermis that secretes cocoons.

[[Arthropods]] have the most elaborate invertebrate exoskeletons. The epidermis secretes a multilayered cuticle made largely of chitin fibres in a protein matrix, with an epicuticle outside. Sclerotization cross-links proteins into sclerotin, producing hard natural plastic; crustaceans may also mineralize cuticle with calcite or aragonite. Terrestrial arthropods add waterproofing wax to prevent desiccation.

[[Mollusks]] use the epidermis for ciliary feeding, snail gliding, mucus secretion, byssus threads, opercula, rock boring, light organs, and rapid colour change in cephalopods. The mollusk shell is secreted by mantle epithelium and consists of outer conchiolin, a prismatic calcium carbonate layer, and an inner nacreous layer that can form pearls. [[Echinoderms]] have a calcareous exoskeleton beneath the epidermis, ranging from sea-urchin armour to sea-cucumber leathery support.

## Vertebrate Skin

![[attachments/integumentary_systems_integument_002.png|420]]

*Dermatology - Integumentary system 3 -- Smart-Servier*

Vertebrate skin has two main layers. The outer epidermis is thinner and cellular, providing a barrier against chemicals, radiation, microbes, and other threats. The deeper dermis is thicker, tougher, and rich in extracellular material. Sensory functions are shared by both layers, and appendages such as scales, feathers, claws, glands, and hair follicles arise from one or both.

Epidermal renewal begins in the stratum germinativum, where cells divide and move outward. In terrestrial vertebrates, surface cells become flattened, keratinized remnants forming the stratum corneum. These dead horny layers are shed or molted periodically or continuously. Keratin is the main tough protein of the barrier.

Cyclostomes and fishes retain aquatic skin traits. Lampreys have smooth scaleless skin with a thin cuticle, slime cells, and a thin dermis. Fishes have flexible glandular skin with mucus glands, poison glands, photophores in some deep-sea species, and scales. Scale types include placoid denticles in cartilaginous fishes, ganoid scales, cycloid scales, and ctenoid scales, while some fishes lack scales.

[[Amphibians]] usually lack horny scales and rely on moist, glandular skin. Their epidermis forms a stratum corneum that is best developed in land-dwelling forms and molts in sheets. The dermis is vascular and lymph-rich because the skin is often important for respiration. Mucous glands lubricate the surface, poison glands deter predators, and breeding-season nuptial pads are induced by male hormones.

[[Reptiles]] are the first fully terrestrial vertebrates in evolutionary terms, and their integument reduces water loss through a thick stratum corneum with wax layers. Reptilian scales are skin folds strengthened by beta keratin and alpha keratin. Snakes and lizards form a complete new epidermal surface before shedding the old cornified layer, while crocodiles and turtles shed more continuously. Bony plates may reinforce the skin.

Bird skin is thin, delicate, and feathered except on legs, feet, beak, comb, and wattle. Feathers consist of beta keratin and likely evolved from reptilian scales. Contour feathers shape the body and aid flight, down and plumules insulate, and colours serve camouflage or display. Feather tracts, remiges, coverts, and tail feathers are important for flight and classification. The uropygial gland secretes oil for preening.

Mammalian skin is marked by hair and alpha-keratin derivatives including nails, claws, hooves, quills, and horns. Epidermal keratinocytes arise from the basal layer, pass through spinosum and granulosum layers, and become dead stratum-corneum cells. In thick hairless skin such as human palms and soles, a stratum lucidum is present. Lipid platelets in the lower stratum corneum form the main water and chemical barrier.

Hair insulates warm-blooded mammals but also functions in sensing, social signaling, sexual display, colour patterns, camouflage, and scent dispersal. Vibrissae or whiskers are specialized sensory hairs with rich nerve supply, while human eyelashes trigger protective reflexes. Each hair grows from a follicle formed by interaction between epidermis and a dermal papilla.

Mammalian glands include sebaceous glands, apocrine tubular glands associated with follicles, and eccrine glands not associated with follicles. Sebaceous glands release oil by cell disintegration. Eccrine sweat glands provide highly effective cooling in humans, and similar footpad glands improve friction in many mammals. Glands also produce odours for sexual and social communication.

## Biodynamics

Skin functions include protection, sensation, communication, temperature regulation, immune response, water balance, and body appearance. It shields against injury, disease organisms, foreign matter, and solar radiation. Blood-vessel expansion, sweating, vessel constriction, and shivering regulate heat. Touch receptors report conditions constantly, and skin colour, contours, patterns, and composition help species recognition and sexual attraction.

The skin surface hosts bacteria and fungi on dead epidermal layers that are periodically shed. A normal skin microcosm can stabilize the surface, while disruption of skin biota may allow some microorganisms to flare up. The barrier is strong but incomplete, so harmful substances can enter and provoke immune responses.

## Key Events & Developments

- Vertebrate skin plan: Epidermis and dermis became the basic two-layer structure of vertebrate integument.
- Terrestrial adaptation: Reptiles developed thick keratinized skin and wax barriers to prevent desiccation.
- Feather evolution: Bird feathers evolved from scale-like epidermal structures and became flight, insulation, and display organs.
- Mammalian hair: Hair became a defining insulation and sensory structure of mammals.

## Interesting Facts

- Arthropod cuticle is both armour and skeleton.
- Sclerotin can be harder than calcite in crustacean claws and mandibles.
- Pearls form from nacre secreted by mollusk mantle tissue around foreign bodies.
- Amphibian skin can be an important respiratory organ.
- Feathers are made of beta keratin and are unique to birds.
- Human eccrine sweat glands create an unusually effective cooling system.
