## Introduction

San Francisco is a city and port in northern California, coextensive with San Francisco county. It sits on a peninsula between the Pacific Ocean and San Francisco Bay and covers about 46 square miles, or 120 square km. It is a cultural and financial centre of the western United States and one of the country's most cosmopolitan cities.

The city holds a powerful place in the American imagination as a cool, elegant seaport of steep streets, bay views, art, music, food, and sophistication. Residents often see it as almost an island, protected by geography and history from the sprawl of much urban California. Yet the city also faces congestion, pollution, violence, vandalism, high costs, homelessness, and inner-city decay.

San Francisco's population changed after [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]] as many white middle-class families moved to suburbs. The city became older, less family-centred statistically, and more racially and ethnically diverse. African American, East Asian, Filipino, Samoan, Vietnamese, Latin American, Native American, and other communities helped reshape what the city meant.

## Landscape and City Site

![[attachments/san_francisco_001.webp|420]]

*Golden Gate Bridge seen from Marshall's Beach*

San Francisco occupies the northern end of a hilly peninsula. Its setting between ocean and bay gives it dramatic views, steep streets, and a compact urban form. The gold-rush city developed around the central business district, financial district, North Beach, and Chinatown, with later landfill extending the waterfront.

The Presidio, a two-century-old military installation near the Golden Gate Bridge, became part of Golden Gate National Recreation Area in 1994. Golden Gate Park, south of the Presidio, was reclaimed from former sandy wasteland. Residential neighbourhoods range from wealthy Pacific Heights to working-class and historically African American Hunters Point.

Offshore, Alcatraz ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcatraz_Island)) became the United States' most notorious maximum-security prison from 1934 to 1963. Native American activists occupied it from 1969 to 1971, and it became part of Golden Gate National Recreation Area in 1972. It is now a major tourist site.

## Climate

San Francisco's [[Climate and Weather (Climate)|climate]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate)) is mild but famously variable. Winter is rainy and mild, spring sunny and temperate, summer foggy and cool, and autumn sunny and warm. Average temperatures range roughly from 51 F, or 11 C, to 63 F, or 17 C.

Most rain falls between November and April, averaging about 21 inches, or 533 mm. The defining feature is summer fog, formed when warm moist air meets cold coastal upwelling. It can cover the city until midday and surprise tourists expecting California heat.

## City Layout

![[attachments/san_francisco_002.webp|420]]

*San Francisco skyline viewed from Twin Peaks*

The city's old core includes the financial district, North Beach, Chinatown, and the gold-rush waterfront. Abandoned ships from 1849 still lie under later office buildings several blocks inland. Neighbourhoods outside the core contain stucco homes, Victorian painted ladies, and varied residential districts.

After the late 1960s, the Manhattanization of San Francisco became visible through high-rise development, especially downtown. Some welcomed it as a sign of economic vitality, while others resisted it as a threat to the city's scale, character, and views.

## Finance and Economy

San Francisco has been a financial centre since gold dust was first exchanged for cash. It became the seat of the Pacific Stock Exchange and home to major banks, including Bank of America and Wells Fargo. Even without many independent native banks, it remained one of the country's important investment banking centres.

Tourism, culture, retail, professional services, finance, technology-related activity, and regional headquarters all support the urban economy. The city's high cost of living and limited land area intensify social inequality, housing pressure, and debates over development.

## Transportation

![[attachments/san_francisco_003.webp|420]]

*San Francisco cable car on city streets*

Transportation is shaped by the peninsula and bay. The San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge, completed in 1936, links the city to the East Bay. The Golden Gate Bridge, completed in 1937, connects San Francisco to Marin County and had a 4,200-foot, or 1,280-metre, centre span.

Before bridges dominated travel, ferries carried huge numbers of passengers to the Ferry Building. Traffic later revived interest in ferries on a smaller scale. BART, the Bay Area Rapid Transit system, began operating in 1972 and connects San Francisco with East Bay communities through an underwater tube more than 3.6 miles long.

The city's beloved cable cars were invented by Andrew Hallidie and tested in 1873. They became San Francisco's most recognizable transportation symbol, created in response to the difficulty and cruelty of using horses on steep hills.

## Government and Public Utilities

San Francisco was incorporated in 1850 and has a consolidated city-county government. Its 1932 freeholders' charter gives the mayor strong executive powers while assigning authority to a chief administrative officer and controller. Legislative power belongs to an elected board of supervisors.

Since 1934, the city's main water source has been Hetch Hetchy Reservoir in the Sierra Nevada, 167 miles away. The project required damming a valley in Yosemite National Park and building long tunnels through the Coast Range. Hydroelectric power also became part of the Bay Area utility network.

## Education and Cultural Life

The Bay Area is a major educational centre, with the University of California at Berkeley and Stanford University nearby. Inside San Francisco are the University of San Francisco, San Francisco State University, Golden Gate University, City College of San Francisco, and the San Francisco Art Institute.

San Francisco's arts reputation developed early. By 1880, it had a large opera house, major hotels, public parks, churches, synagogues, and millionaire mansions. Performers such as Sarah Bernhardt, Edwin Booth, Luisa Tetrazzini, Lillie Langtry, and Lotta Crabtree appeared there, and Isadora Duncan began teaching modern dance in the city.

The city has been especially important for writers. Mark Twain, Ambrose Bierce, Jack London, Bret Harte, Frank Norris, Robert Louis Stevenson, Dashiell Hammett, William Saroyan, Wallace Stegner, Amy Tan, and others are linked to it. In the 1950s, Lawrence Ferlinghetti's City Lights Bookstore became a key Beat gathering place.

The San Francisco Symphony and San Francisco Opera are major musical institutions. Museums include the Asian Art Museum, California Palace of the Legion of Honor, San Francisco Museum of Modern Art, de Young Museum, and many smaller collections. The Civic Center, public library, and auditorium were important post-1906 civic projects.

## History

European sea explorers such as Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo, Francis Drake, and Sebastian Vizcaino passed the bay entrance without entering it. In 1769, an expedition led by Gaspar de Portola first saw San Francisco Bay from land. In 1775, Juan Manuel de Ayala brought the first Spanish ship into the bay.

In 1776, settlers under Jose Joaquin Moraga and Francisco Palou founded the Presidio and Mission San Francisco de Asis, or Mission Dolores. A village later grew at Yerba Buena Cove. William Anthony Richardson built the first dwelling in 1835.

The United States took Yerba Buena during the Mexican-American War. In 1847, the town was renamed San Francisco. Just before the gold discovery, it had about 800 settlers in roughly 200 shacks and adobes.

## Gold Rush and Metropolitan Growth

The Gold Rush ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_rush)) transformed San Francisco almost instantly. About 40,000 gold seekers arrived by sea, 30,000 crossed the Great Basin, and 9,000 came north from Mexico. By 1851, more than 800 abandoned ships lay in the cove.

Boom conditions created fortunes, fraud, violence, and instability. Eggs sold for a dollar each, real estate prices soared, and by the panic of 1857 many had become rich and bankrupt. Vigilance committees in the 1850s used extralegal justice to answer violence and weak law enforcement.

The discovery of the Comstock Lode in 1859 shifted San Francisco from frontier boomtown to metropolitan financial centre. Bankers, speculators, lawyers, restaurants, and hotels shaped the city, and by 1870 its population reached nearly 150,000.

## Disaster, Labour, War, and Protest

The 1906 earthquake and fire devastated San Francisco, but the city rebuilt quickly. In 1915, the Panama-Pacific International Exposition celebrated both recovery and the opening of the Panama Canal. Labour conflict intensified during the Great Depression, especially the 1934 waterfront strike and citywide general strike.

During [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]], San Francisco became a major embarkation point for the Pacific theatre, and shipyards around the bay drew about half a million workers. The United Nations was founded there at the San Francisco Conference from April to June 1945.

In the 1950s, San Francisco became a Beat centre and a stage for comedians and performers such as Lenny Bruce, Jonathan Winters, Woody Allen, Phyllis Diller, Barbra Streisand, and Mort Sahl. In the 1960s, it became associated with hippies, antiwar protest, civil rights, sexual liberation, environmentalism, gay rights, and psychedelic rock groups such as Jefferson Airplane and Grateful Dead.

The 1970s brought trauma and change: an assassination attempt on Gerald Ford, the Jonestown deaths connected with Jim Jones, and the murders of Mayor George Moscone and Supervisor Harvey Milk. Dianne Feinstein, the city's first female mayor, provided stability afterward.
