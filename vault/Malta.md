# Malta

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Malta is a small central Mediterranean island state whose strategic location brought successive Phoenician, Roman, Arab, European, and British rulers. Its wartime role, independence, republican government, and European Union membership show how geography has continually shaped its political history.

## Introduction

![[attachments/malta_001.webp|420]]

*Valletta skyline at dusk*

Malta is a small central Mediterranean island country whose position made it strategically important between Europe, Africa, and the Middle East. Maltese society was shaped by many rulers, including Phoenicians, Romans, Greeks, Arabs, Normans, Sicilians, Swabians, Aragonese, Hospitallers, French, and British rulers. Malta was an Allied base in [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]], received the George Cross ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Cross)) in 1942, became independent in 1964, became a republic in 1974, and joined the European Union in 2004. Valletta is the capital, and the Maltese cross ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_cross)) remains a national symbol.

## Land

![[attachments/malta_002.webp|420]]

*Grand Harbour at Valletta, Malta*

The country includes Malta, Gozo, Comino, Kemmunett, and Filfla, about 58 miles south of Sicily and 180 miles from Libya and Tunisia.

## Relief

Malta's islands are dominated by limestone, cliffs, bays, inlets, and coves. Malta island is crossed by the Victoria Lines Fault escarpment; Ta' Zuta reaches 830 feet, Ta' Dbiegi Hill on Gozo reaches 636 feet, and Valletta stands between Marsamxett Harbour and Grand Harbour.

## Drainage

Malta has no permanent streams or lakes, so water depends on aquifers and reverse-osmosis desalination, which supplies about half of daily needs.

## Soils

Maltese soils are thin, young, alkaline, and low in humus. The 1973 Fertile Soil Preservation Act requires construction-removed soil to be moved to agricultural land.

## Climate

Malta has hot dry summers, wet autumns, and short cool winters; over three-fourths of about 22 inches of rain falls from October to March. Reduced woodland leaves maquis, garigue, steppe, wetlands, salt-marsh plants, cliff flora, bats, reptiles, birds, insects, and many migrants.

## People

The population is almost entirely Maltese, with roots in Carthaginian, Phoenician, Italian, and other Mediterranean peoples. Maltese ethnic identity strengthened from the late 13th century and in nationalist thought of the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Language

Maltese and English are official languages. Maltese fuses North African Arabic with Sicilian Italian and is the only Semitic language officially written in Latin script; Italian, official in church and government until 1934, remains widely understood.

## Religion

[[Roman Catholicism]] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church)) is official, though religious freedom is guaranteed, and more than nine-tenths of Maltese are nominally Catholic. Settlement grew from maritime towns, clustered villages, British dockyard communities, and later tourism around Sliema. Dense population and overbuilding made environmental protection important.

## Economy

Until the mid-1960s, Malta depended on the British military presence. Withdrawal forced diversification, investment incentives, market reforms, privatization from 1999, and capital-control removal after EU entry in 2004. A small domestic market and import dependence remain constraints.

Agriculture is limited by fragmented land, shallow soils, and water shortage, though potatoes, tomatoes, fruit, grapes, and olives are grown. Fishing is seasonal, while aquaculture, introduced in the late 1980s, surpassed fishing income. Limestone is the only exploited mineral, and imported fossil fuels supply energy.

## Manufacturing

Manufacturing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing)) grew after the 1960s and produced about one-fifth of GDP by the early 21st century. Electronics, pharmaceuticals, semiconductors, software, and vehicle or aircraft parts replaced much low-cost production. Shipbuilding and repair, rooted in the Hospitallers' move to Grand Harbour in 1570, moved toward privatization after EU accession.

## Finance

The Central Bank of Malta was founded in 1968, the lira was adopted in 1972, and the euro became currency on January 1, 2008. The Malta Financial Services Authority, created in 2002, regulates financial services, and foreign investment rose in the early 2000s.

## Trade

Malta imports machinery, transport equipment, chemicals, and mineral fuels. It exports [[Semiconductors|semiconductors]], other manufactures, and refined petroleum to partners including Italy, France, the United Kingdom, the United States, Germany, and Singapore.

## Services

Services produce about half of GDP and employ about three-fifths of workers. Tourism peaks from June through October and draws visitors to temples, Mdina, castles, cathedrals, and UNESCO-listed sites. Unions are influential, and taxes come mainly from income tax, value-added tax, and real estate.

Transport uses roads, buses from Valletta, ferries to Gozo and Sicily, and Air Malta links abroad. Telecommunications liberalized in 2004. The 1964 constitution made Malta an independent parliamentary monarchy, the 1974 amendment made it a republic, and local councils began in 1993.

## Justice

Maltese law was codified mainly from 1854 to 1873 and is based largely on Napoleonic law, while criminal, fiscal, and maritime areas follow English common law. Politics is polarized between the Nationalist Party and Malta Labour Party, with high turnout.

## Security

From 1964 to 1972, defense was tied to the United Kingdom; from 1972 to 1987, Malta followed nonalignment, and neutrality entered the constitution in 1987. Health care is broad and mostly free, private hospitals grew after the 1990s, and social insurance began in 1956.

## Education

Education changed from the early 1970s to the mid-1980s, moving away from British and church models. Schooling covers ages 5 to 16, regional colleges began in 2005, and higher education admission follows competence after the 1988 Education Act. The University of Malta began in 1592; MCAST began in 2000.

## Culture and Institutions

The International Maritime Law Institute and Mediterranean Academy of Diplomatic Studies are at the University of Malta campus. Cultural life combines Arab, Italian, Catholic, and British influences through festas, Good Friday processions, Mnarja, Carnival, dances, Neolithic ruins, Baroque architecture, Caravaggio, Dun Karm, theatres, museums, sports, and media.

## History

![[attachments/malta_003.webp|420]]

*Hagar Qim megalithic temple in Malta*

The earliest remains date from about 5000 BCE, with Neolithic farmers and a death cult after 4000 BCE. The Hal Saflieni hypogeum became a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1980. Later cultures included Tarxien Cemetery, Borg In-Nadur, Phoenician and Carthaginian influence, Roman rule from 218 BCE, and St. Paul's traditional shipwreck in 60 CE.

Malta followed Sicily through Byzantine, Arab, Norman, Swabian, Angevin, and Spanish rule. In 1530, Charles V gave Malta to the Knights of Rhodes. Under Jean de Valette, Malta resisted the Ottoman siege of 1565, and Valletta was founded in 1566.

Napoleon Bonaparte captured Malta in 1798, but British forces received the French surrender in 1800. British rule was confirmed in 1814-15. The dockyard drove the economy, the Crimean War and 1869 Suez Canal helped Malta, self-government came in 1921, [[World Wars (World War II)|World War II]] brought the George Cross, and independence came on September 21, 1964.

## Salvino Busuttil

After independence, politics polarized between the Nationalist Party and Malta Labour Party. The PN governed from 1962 to 1971 with Western alignment; the MLP pursued nonalignment from 1971 and negotiated British withdrawal by 1979. The PN later revived EU accession, voters approved it in 2003, Malta joined on May 1, 2004, and adopted the euro in 2008.

## Lino Briguglio

In May 2011, Maltese voters approved legalizing divorce. Malta had been the only EU country without divorce law; parliament passed it in June 2011, and it took effect in October 2011.
