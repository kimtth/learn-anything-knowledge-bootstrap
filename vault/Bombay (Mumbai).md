# Bombay (Mumbai)

> [!abstract] Reference navigation
> Mumbai, formerly Bombay, is Maharashtra’s capital and India’s leading financial, commercial, port, and cultural metropolis. Built from seven reclaimed islands, it combines dense migration-driven diversity, industrial and service growth, transport centrality, major educational institutions, and Bollywood with persistent housing pressure, inequality, congestion, and environmental constraint.

## Introduction

![[attachments/bombay_mumbai_001.webp|420]]

*Bombay (Mumbai)*

Mumbai ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai)), formerly Bombay, is the capital of Maharashtra and India’s leading financial and commercial centre. It is a crowded island city whose growth, diversity, and economic strength have made it one of the world’s great urban centres. The city is full of contradictions: wealth and poverty, dense housing and global finance, strong institutions and severe congestion.

## Landscape and Climate

![[attachments/bombay_mumbai_002.webp|420]]

*Marine Drive and the Mumbai skyline*

The city occupies a peninsular site formed from seven originally separate islets. Reclamation, drainage, and causeways turned them into a single urban mass, and the resulting geography remains a major constraint on expansion and housing. Mumbai's built form was created by engineering as much as by settlement.

## Population and Society

Mumbai is densely populated and highly cosmopolitan, with major Hindu, Muslim, Christian, Jain, Sikh, Zoroastrian, and Jewish communities and a wide range of Indian and foreign languages. Almost every religious and regional group in India can be found there.

## Economy and Manufacturing

The city is India’s economic hub. Cotton textiles once dominated, but later industry diversified into metals, chemicals, automobiles, electronics, food processing, printing, and publishing. The economy shifted from older manufacturing to a broader industrial and service base.

## Transportation and Government

Mumbai is India’s principal western port and a major rail and air hub. Suburban rail lines, buses, the port, and the airport all make the city a central point in the country’s transport network. The city's transport system is as important as its commercial institutions.

## Education, Health, and Culture

Mumbai has a strong educational and medical infrastructure, including universities, technical institutes, major hospitals, and research centres. Cultural life is equally prominent, with museums, theaters, parks, newspapers, radio, and the Bollywood film industry. These institutions give Mumbai a public life that extends far beyond commerce.

## History

![[attachments/bombay_mumbai_003.webp|420]]

*Gateway of India in Mumbai*

Mumbai ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai)) grew from ancient settlement and maritime trade into a Portuguese and then British possession before becoming a major colonial port. Its rise continued under British rule, with later urban expansion, post-independence growth, religious tensions, and modern terrorist attacks. The historical section shows repeated reinvention rather than a single founding moment.

## Conclusion

Mumbai is a city of immense energy and contradiction: economically powerful, culturally rich, and globally connected, but also overcrowded, unequal, and environmentally stressed. These contradictions are not accidental; they are built into the city’s geography, history, and economic role.

## Key Events & Developments

- The city develops from seven islands joined by reclamation and causeways.
- 19th century: Cotton textiles and port trade make Bombay a major urban center.
- 20th century: Finance, manufacturing diversification, and large-scale migration transform the city.
- Post-independence: The city grows rapidly and becomes even more cosmopolitan.
- 1992-1993: Sectarian violence follows the destruction of the Babri Masjid.
- 2006 and 2008: Major terrorist attacks strike the city.

## Key People

- Residents of Mumbai: A highly diverse population shaped by migration.
- State and municipal authorities: Manage the city across different layers of government.
- Business and financial leaders: Help make Mumbai India's economic center.

## Interesting Facts

- Mumbai is built on land that was once seven separate islets.
- It handles a major share of India's international and domestic flights.
- The city is home to both the country's leading stock exchange and its biggest film industry.
- Its transport and housing problems are directly tied to its island geography.
